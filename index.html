Diageo announces savings plan as profits continue to decline - Drinks International

Diageo, el mayor productor mundial de bebidas espirituosas, reportó caída del 27.2% en ganancias operativas y 3% en ventas netas en fiscal 2026, con debilidad crítica en América del Norte y Asia Pacífico, aunque muestra crecimiento en Europa, LATAM y África. El CEO anunció un programa de reestructuración dirigido a ahorrar USD 1.000 millones en tres años. Este movimiento es estratégico para la industria regional: señala presión competitiva global, posibles cambios en portafolios de marcas y realineamientos de inversión que podrían afectar dinámicas comerciales en mercados latinoamericanos donde Diageo opera con portafolios significativos.

Texto completo del artículo
In its fiscal 26 preliminary results, Diageo has revealed a 27.2% decline in operating profit and a 3% decline in net sales as the world’s largest spirits group continues to struggle in North American and Asia Pacific markets. The group did, however, show growth in Europe, Latin America, the Caribbean and Africa. In response to the performance, chief executive Dave Lewis outlined a restructuring programme that is expected to save the company around $1 billion over the next three years. According to the group, the operating framework redesign is expected to deliver around $850 million of savings by fiscal 28, with an additional $150 million in savings from supply chain initiatives. Speaking on the results, Lewis said: “We are pleased with our progress in LAC, Europe and Africa. We are focused on recovering our competitiveness in NAM and we are working through the consequences of Government policy in Chinese white spirits. “The revised operating framework is being rolled out across Diageo, and the changes are significant. In 2026 this change incurs a cost of $0.8 billion with savings realised over 2 years starting in fiscal 27. These savings will allow us to invest in the turnaround without needing to reduce operating profit.” The US continues to be a point of friction for Diageo with net spirits sales falling by 11.5%. Tequila, a category that has been a source of regional growth, saw marked declines of 21.1%, “driven by both Don Julio and Casamigos, reflecting a softer category, increased competitive intensity, and tough comparatives in the prior period, and both brands lost share.” Declines were more dramatic in Greater China, where net sales fell by 34.9%, largely driven by declines in sales of baijiu. The declines were partially offset by a strong performance in India where net sales grew by 7.1% thanks to a strong Scotch performance. In Europe, a strong Guinness performance in the UK and Ireland offset a softer spirits showing.

Texto completo del artículo
Female-founded ready-to-drink (RTD) brand Pimentae will launch its first crowdfunding campaign next week with a target of £1 million (US$1.3m). Hosted on Crowdcube, the campaign will open from 11 August for priority members who signed up for early access. It will then open to the public on 12 August and close on 25 August – or earlier if the £1m target is hit beforehand. “We want the people who’ve championed Pimentae from day one, queued at our festival bars, picked us off grocery shelves, enjoyed us at their weddings – to own a piece of what’s next,” the brand wrote on its website. The investment will be used for expanding the sales team across grocery and wholesale, continuing to drive expansion of its immersive brand world across digital, out-of-home and social channels; and supporting major grocery and stadium launches. Last June, the brand secured £1.5m (US$2m) in funding, which would go towards accelerating retail expansion and growing its multi-format portfolio across cans, bottles and kegs. The female-founded brand was launched in 2021 by Alice Parmiter and Wynter Karo. The brand focuses on Tequila-based drinks and began with bottled cocktails, before moving into different formats with kegs and bag-in-box, and then canned cocktails . It also recently expanded into a mezcal-based Margarita with Ojo de Dios. In the five years since launching the brand, the pair have landed national listings in the UK at Sainsbury’s, Waitrose, Co-op, Ocado and Tesco. A partnership with British Airways has also been formed, as well as availability in all major UK airports and London-based stadiums such as Twickenham and Tottenham Hotspur. Pimentae’s revenue since launching has totalled over £4.2m (US$5.6m) and it was the number one canned Margarita brand in UK supermarkets, according to Circana data to the 26 weeks ending 1 November 2025. On why now for the crowdfund, Karo told The Spirits Business the brand was entering its highest growth period to date. “We went live with Sainsbury’s in March, joining existing listings with Waitrose, Co-op, Ocado and Tesco Ireland,” she said. “Two new grocery announcements are expected in the next six months, alongside multi-group pub listings.” In April 2023 , Pimentae gained investment from Diageo’s Distill Ventures accelerator programme, which was shuttered last year . The brand was part of Distill Ventures’ pre-accelerator programme for nine months, which gives brands up to £300,000 (US$404,000) as a one-off investment and then eight-12 months of accelerator support, including Diageo’s knowledge base and departmental knowledge, but not its distribution network. Karo confirmed that Pimentae did not “progress onto the full accelerator programme, as it wasn’t the right growth strategy for us at that time”. She added: “While we haven’t formally worked with [Distill Ventures] since 2023, we still have a great relationship with the team – they were instrumental in helping us build the business infrastructure needed to get us scale-ready.”

The GLP-1 effect reaches ANZ

La adopción de medicamentos GLP-1 en Australia y Nueva Zelanda alcanza el 78% de conciencia de marca, con el 55% de consumidores considerando su uso y 17% de hogares australianos ya adoptando estas drogas. Esta tendencia modifica hábitos de compra de alimentos en la región Asia-Pacífico, impactando significativamente la demanda de bebidas y snacks calóricos. Para empresas de bebidas en Argentina con operaciones regionales, representa un precedente crítico sobre cambios de consumo que eventualmente podrían replicarse en mercados locales, requiriendo revisión de portafolios hacia opciones bajas en calorías y funcionales.

Texto completo del artículo
North America and Europe may have dominated headlines about GLP-1 medications, but Australia and New Zealand are catching up. They’re increasingly demonstrating how those consumer shifts are spreading through Asia-Pacific as uptake accelerates and food purchasing habits evolve. According to NIQ Homescan data, awareness in the region has reached 78%, up five percentage points in a single quarter, while 55% would consider using the drugs. Seventeen percent of Australian households already include someone taking a GLP-1 medication, with 13% using it for weight management. Kemilyn Ferraz, category marketing executive for Australia and New Zealand at Ingredion, believes those figures show ANZ has become one of the region’s most informative GLP-1 markets. “ANZ stands as one of the more mature markets for GLP-1 in Asia-Pacific, with the wider APAC region following closely behind,” she says. “We have a market with interest in GLP-1 medications alongside a broader appetite for healthier products, yet access remains narrow.” Access is shaped by Australia’s Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme, which subsidises GLP-1 medicines for type 2 diabetes rather than weight loss. Consumers using the drugs for obesity management are therefore typically paying between AU$4,000 and AU$5,000 a year, meaning uptake remains concentrated among older and higher-income households, with average treatment lasting between nine and 14 months. Ferraz believes, however, that the industry’s attention shouldn’t stop with prescription users. “What we’re seeing extends well beyond the people actually taking GLP-1 medications,” she says. “The wider health-conscious consumer is increasingly looking for the same things from food.” Rather than viewing GLP-1 users as a niche demographic, Ferraz believes manufacturers should recognise that their expectations increasingly overlap with consumers already pursuing healthier lifestyles through other means. “There is an exciting opportunity for brands to innovate for both segments simultaneously,” she says. “Some are medically driven. Others are following holistic approaches such as keto, intermittent fasting or avoiding ultra-processed foods. “When we look at what they want from food, they land in similar places – more protein, more fibre, better gut health, cleaner labels. Both want more nutrition from a smaller portion, and both are drinking less.” Alcohol illustrates how these trends are beginning to overlap. Reduced consumption has become one of the most widely reported behavioural changes among GLP-1 users, reflecting the medications’ influence on appetite and reward pathways. Yet Ferraz argues the drinks industry was already moving in that direction. NIQ’s April 2026 survey found alcoholic beverages recorded a net 32-point decline in spending intentions across Australia and New Zealand over the next year, making it one of the fastest-declining categories in the research. “GLP-1 arrived in the middle of one trend and pushed harder,” she says. “Non-alcoholic alternatives are one of the fastest-moving spaces in beverage right now, and the formulation brief looks familiar. Deliver the experience and mouthfeel with less sugar, fewer calories and a clean label. That’s the same challenge a protein shake or a reduced-sugar yoghurt is solving. “Manufacturers are increasingly focusing on the shared nutritional attributes both groups want, rather than targeting only GLP-1 users.” “Over the next few years, I anticipate the shifts to occur within the dairy, beverages and snacks categories in the ANZ region,” says Ferraz. The pattern is more gradual than many manufacturers might expect. During consumers’ first year on GLP-1 treatment, NIQ data shows purchasing habits shift quickly towards healthier options. Yoghurt volumes increase by 15.3%, dried fruit and nuts rise 10%, while fresh fruit grows 6%. Interestingly, indulgence doesn’t disappear overnight. “Ice cream consumption actually rises by 5.3% during that same period,” Ferraz notes. Only after consumers have remained on treatment for more than a year do more substantial declines appear. Ice cream volumes fall 13.6%, while impulse snacks decline 12.7%. “The cutback is inevitable, but it just takes longer than people expect. We see innovation moving particularly fast in ready-to-drink beverages and shakes, where brands are taking different strategic approaches to respond to GLP-1.” The commercial challenge, however, extends well beyond simply increasing protein or fibre content. “Adjusting formulations for additional nutritional benefits often leads to texture and mouthfeel challenges,” says Ferraz. “When we add fibre, we need to manage grittiness, unwanted thickness or changes in colour and flavour. Add protein and we have to address chalkiness, gelation and separation, especially in ready-to-drink beverages. Take out sugar and we need to address bulk, mouthfeel and sweetness quality all at once.” Taste remains the deciding factor. “Health claims may drive the first purchase; however, taste is what secures the second,” says Ferraz. In ANZ, manufacturers must also contend with the Health Star Rating system. “You are not just balancing taste and nutrition; you are doing it against a public-facing score.” Texture has emerged as one of Ingredion’s most important findings. “Thirty-nine percent of current GLP-1 users say texture has become more important since starting treatment. When we asked why, the top answer was that it makes food feel more satisfying and filling. It makes eating more enjoyable. More practically, it also helps reduce nausea and discomfort and helps with portion control.” Those findings reinforce what many manufacturers are already discovering in product development. Smaller portions place greater emphasis on eating quality. Every bite has to work harder, making mouthfeel, moisture, aeration and layered textures increasingly important alongside nutritional credentials. Another finding surprised Ferraz even more. “Ninety percent of surveyed GLP-1 users have actively done something to learn about diet, nutrition or exercise. They’ve talked to friends and family, consulted dietitians, watched videos or followed people online. But only 39% are following an actual nutrition plan. So you have a group who are motivated, informed, but unsupported. They know what they should be eating. That gap between intent and behaviour is not a research finding; it is a product brief.” Labelling creates its own set of challenges. Globally, Ingredion’s research found 54% of shoppers say a product labelled specifically for GLP-1 users makes them question whether it will actually taste good. At the same time, 91% of millennials taking GLP-1 medications say they want products to identify themselves clearly on pack. “I believe GLP-1’s influence will merge into a broader health and wellness movement,” says Ferraz. “But it will leave something permanent behind, and that’s the part worth paying attention to. “Look at what GLP-1 users want – protein, fibre, gut health, clean labels and smaller portions that still satisfy. Now look at what the holistic wellness consumer wants, whether they’re following keto, intermittent fasting or simply avoiding processed foods. It’s the same list of benefits.” Ferraz believes that convergence will fundamentally change how manufacturers think about growth. “Developed markets were already heading toward what we call Peak Calorie. GLP-1 medications bring that forward and accelerate the descent. Once that happens, volume per pack stops being the lever. Value per bite becomes the lever. “People cycle off. But they do not unlearn the experience. Once someone has spent a year eating smaller portions and paying attention to whether the food earned its place, they do not go back to buying on volume.” Around half of GLP-1 users have already changed how they plan, shop and cook. Ferraz argues manufacturers risk becoming too focused on nutritional claims while overlooking the eating experience. “Brands need to focus on the sensory experience to have consumers coming back for more. While health drives consideration, the eating experience remains the primary driver of consumer enjoyment.” Australia and New Zealand may be following a path already established in North America and Europe, but manufacturers across the region are confronting the same commercial reality. Products designed to deliver more protein, fibre or other nutritional benefits will only succeed if consumers still enjoy eating them.

From Fizzy to Fulfilled: Today’s Non-Alcoholic Beverages Need to Do More Than Refresh

ADM publicó un estudio sobre segmentación de consumidores de bebidas no alcohólicas en 9 mercados globales (incluyendo Brasil), identificando 8 segmentos basados en ocasiones de consumo. Los consumidores modernos buscan bebidas que se alineen con sus estilos de vida y estado emocional deseado, más allá del refresco tradicional. Este insight es crítico para redefinir estrategias de producto, positioning y ocasiones de consumo en mercados emergentes como Brasil y Nigeria, donde la demanda por bebidas funcionales y de bienestar está en crecimiento.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
Refreshment and great taste still define the global beverage category. But today’s consumers expect more from every sip. They’re choosing beverages that fit their lifestyles, support how they want to feel, and match the moments that matter most. ADM identified eight occasion-based beverage consumer segments across nine global markets—Germany, the UK, USA, South Africa, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, Brazil, Australia and Vietnam—and uncovered what’s shaping non-alcoholic beverage choices. The findings reveal three shifts creating new opportunities for innovation. Functional beverages remain a global growth opportunity, but consumers are no longer buying benefits alone. They increasingly choose drinks based on how those benefits make them feel, and that emotional connection is reshaping value. Sweetness remains essential, but it’s no longer enough to stand out. Consumers seek beverages with greater depth and complexity, creating room for layered sensory experiences that balance sweet with spicy, sour, and savory through controlled bitterness and botanical notes. Consumers make different decisions depending on whether they’re relaxing alone, commuting, entertaining at home, or socializing out. Understanding these moments gives brands a clearer roadmap for relevant and more personal beverage innovation. Brands that can bring these elements together will be well-positioned to lead the next wave of beverage innovation. Texto completo del artículo
Every two-to-seven years, the El Niño phenomenon causes temperatures to rise globally and rainfall patterns to change. It can often put a strain on food production, but 2026/27 is predicted to be a ‘super El Niño’, according to the organisation World Resources Institute (WRI), with even more severe effects. This is only compounded by the effects of climate change, which are already pressuring crop yields globally. Yet many of the food giants have practices in place to protect their supply chains against climate volatility of the sort that El Niño is likely to bring. While Nestlé is monitoring El Niño closely, it views it as a smaller part of the larger picture of climate volatility, explains a spokesperson for the company. It is prioritising the resilience of supply chains most exposed to such risks, including coffee, cocoa and dairy. In cocoa, it is investing in regenerative agriculture, farmer productivity, improved agronomy and climate-resilient farming practices, the spokesperson explains, through various initiatives including the Nestlé Cocoa Plan and the Nestlé Income Accelerator Program. It is aiming to diversify sourcing and increase flexibility, in order to avoid dependence on one region. To protect its tomato supply chain, Kraft Heinz has developed unique seeds for different locations around the world, explains Jake Lake, global head of agriculture and seed at Kraft Heinz. For example, in California, the US, tomato plants have been developed that have more extensive root systems, and can therefore reach water and nutrients deeper underground. The company is now investing in regenerative agriculture to strengthen supply chain resilience. It’s also using new technology in agriculture, Lake explains. For example, in Spain it’s using precision drip irrigation systems to feed plants the right amount of water and nutrients at the right time. Chocolate major Barry Callebaut is optimistic about its position when it comes to El Niño. “Barry Callebaut is closely monitoring the development of El Niño and its potential impact on cocoa-producing regions”, says a spokesperson for the company. Barry Callebaut is in a better position to deal with climate volatility than it was two years ago, the spokesperson suggests. It has strengthened its sourcing flexibility, increased origin diversification, and enhanced bean blending capabilities. The chocolate industry as a whole is also in a better position as it enters the 2026/27 crop than it was when entering the 2023/24 crop, the last time El Niño took place, says the spokesperson. At that time, “El Niño coincided with the main crop and marked the third consecutive year of deficit. In contrast, today, we are coming from a position of a strong surplus with ample cocoa stocks entering the new crop year.” Danone also laid out its strategy for El Niño. “Danone continuously monitors climate-related risks across its sourcing, supply and logistics networks”, says a spokesperson for the company. “We focus on building resilient supply chains through diversified sourcing, strong supplier partnerships, robust risk management processes and long-term investments that support the resilience of agricultural systems and sourcing regions.” In short, these food giants already have measures in place to try to protect their supply chains from the climate volatility that will likely come about as a result of El Niño.
