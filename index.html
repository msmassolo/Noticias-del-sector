Will Iraq become PepsiCo’s next Middle East manufacturing hub?

PepsiCo está evaluando Iraq como potencial centro de manufactura en Medio Oriente, aprovechando la estabilidad relativa del país a pesar del conflicto regional con Irán. Esta expansión estratégica representa una reconfiguración de la cadena de suministro en la región y una apuesta por diversificar operaciones en mercados emergentes con demanda creciente. El movimiento refleja la estrategia corporativa de PepsiCo de acceder a nuevos mercados de producción en contextos geopolíticamente complejos.

Texto completo del artículo
The war in Iran has dominated news throughout 2026, but with no end to the conflict in sight, life and business as usual has been moving ahead in other parts of the region. One such example is neighbouring Iraq, which was a longstanding centre of conflict-related news throughout the 2000s. Despite its proximity to the centre of the war in Iran, this country is slowly emerging as a potential new manufacturing centre, receiving attention even from major brands such as PepsiCo. On the surface, the draw of this market is clear, due to its geographical advantages, large young population, and high access to locally produced food ingredients — according to the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO), these include many staple crops as well as dates, fruits, tomatoes, potatoes and animal protein. “As one of the region’s historic farming heartlands, we want to support Iraq’s agricultural potential and industrial capacity to become a hub for food exports,” PepsiCo CEO of Foods, Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan Ahmed Elsheikh said during the recent US-Iraq High Level Business Summit. “This MOU provides a basis for exploring opportunities to grow Iraq’s food manufacturing sector and strengthen the country’s farming sector [as] strong food manufacturing begins with strong agriculture. By working across the value chain, we hope to create opportunities that benefit farmers and local industry while supporting products proudly made in Iraq.” This is not the firm’s only project in Iraq this year, as it also signed an MoU with the Baghdad Soft Drinks Co. (BSDC) that aims to boost local production capacity to go beyond 250 million cases annually. According to PepsiCo, this MOU builds on a partnership that has been in place since 1984, for over four decades. “This MOU reflects PepsiCo’s confidence in Iraq’s industrial future, and supports employment and local manufacturing,” said the firm. A big part of the MOU is the aim to support modernisation for the local manufacturing sector, an important area that needs rapid improvement if the country really hopes to upgrade its role in the region. “The main goal is threefold: advanced manufacturing, skilled employment, and continued investment in Iraq’s local industry,” PepsiCo declared after the MOU was signed. This was further bolstered by PepsiCo SVP and CEO, Middle East and Africa Beverage FOBO (franchise-owned bottling operations) Mohamed Shelbaya, who confirmed the company views Iraq as a major beverage manufacturing player in the region. “[The MOU] with BSDC outlines a shared ambition to explore potential opportunities related to manufacturing capacity expansion, strengthening Iraq’s role as a leading beverage production hub in the Middle East, and further developing the people and capabilities that drive our business forward,” he said via social media. “I’ve always believed that the strongest partnerships are built over time. Our relationship with BSDC spans more than four decades, and this next step reflects the trust we’ve built together and our shared belief in Iraq’s long-term potential.” Investment and assistance from international names such as PepsiCo are crucial to deciding whether Iraq will be able to take off as the region’s next modern manufacturing hub. According to the World Bank report <a href="https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0d8484f9-90ee-5aa4-b821-92c3add840ef/content" target="_blank" rel="">Breaking Out of Fragility</a>, Iraq faces many challenges as remnants of its war-torn history some two decades ago, but the major, obvious ones that pose the greatest hurdles to its manufacturing growth include infrastructure and logistic deficits. Some of the most problematic would include cold storage facilities, electrical supply stability, as well as transportation logistics and timings. “Although Iraq’s geography gives it the potential to be a regional transit hub, its performance at logistics and border management is weaker than any of its neighbours’, turning it instead into a regional bottleneck,” the report said. “[That said], Iraq has latent export potential for a variety of goods that, if tapped, could diversify the country’s economy, raise living standards, and boost economic resilience. Under the right conditions, including a return of domestic security, improved trade policy could also bring about better prices and quality for Iraqi consumers.”

As GLP-1 Patients Rethink Food, CPGs Must Follow Suit

El crecimiento del uso de fármacos GLP-1 para pérdida de peso está transformando las preferencias de consumo: los usuarios reducen volúmenes pero priorizan calidad nutricional, rechazando productos ultra-procesados con alto contenido de sal, azúcar y grasas. Esta tendencia obliga a los fabricantes de bebidas y snacks a reformular portafolios hacia opciones más saludables. Para las empresas de bebidas argentinas, esto implica revisar estrategias de bebidas azucaradas y desarrollar alternativas funcionales/bajas en calorías antes de que la penetración de GLP-1 en la región replique el patrón de mercados desarrollados.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
A funny thing has happened since Americans went on a GLP-1 weight-loss binge: They began thinking differently about food and what they were willing to put into their newly svelte bodies. People are eating less, making what they do eat more important. Now food manufacturers are rethinking their snack offerings, which for decades relied on salt, sugar and fat to appeal to cravings, fueling the obesity epidemic. Consumers taking GLP-1s, however, are just not that interested. “Brands need to recognize that GLP-1s are fundamentally changing the way a growing group of consumers think about food,” Valentino Perrina, co-founder & CEO of Nutré Meal Plans, told The Food Institute . “The focus needs to shift from simply selling low-calorie products to creating foods that deliver more nutritional value in smaller portions, prioritizing things like protein, fiber, quality ingredients and balanced nutrition.” GLP-1s were first approved by the Food and Drug Administration in 2005 to treat diabetes but didn’t come into widespread use until 2017 with the introduction of Ozempic. Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound also are in the category while generics are popping up overseas and some compounding pharmacies offer their own versions. The American Medical Association has estimated spending on the drugs surged to $71.7 billion in 2023. Morgan Stanley projects 15% of the U.S. population will be taking GLP-1 drugs by 2035, The Wall Street Journal reported. JP Morgan predicts that could mean revenue losses for the food and beverage industry exceeding $30 billion by 2030. PwC estimates 20% of U.S. households include at least one person taking the drugs. Not just GLP-1s are threatening profits: High prices are turning consumers away from processed foods, and away from brand names to less expensive store brands, the Journal said. NielsenIQ data indicate that after nearly a year on GLP-1s, consumers buy less pizza (15%), potato chips (8%) and bacon (nearly 10%) while spending on beef, yogurt, strawberries and brussels sprouts shows double-digit increases, and those foods sporting 20 grams of protein are up nearly 20% among long-term users. Morgan Harms, a plastic surgeon at Harris Facial Plastics, told FI he has taken GLP-1s. “Consumers want to know that what they’re putting into their bodies is actually good for them. There’s a return to better ingredients, fewer ingredients and greater transparency,” Harms said. “Brands should be talking openly about their products on social media, including how they source their ingredients and what they’re changing to meet the mindset of today’s consumer.” NielsenIQ data also indicate consumers are paying more attention to protein, fiber and clean-label food and beverage products, and at the same time are looking for bolder and more complex flavors, trading calorie volume for calorie quality. “GLP-1 users still want enjoyable food, and aren’t necessarily looking for deprivation, but what they perceive as enjoyable can change the longer they’re on the medication,” said Sherry Frey, vice president of Total Wellness at NielsenIQ. “Essentially, we’re seeing a shift toward foods and beverages that deliver both pleasure and purpose, and the CPGs that can successfully combine taste, nutrition, convenience and function will be best positioned to grow.” Even in the throes of weight-loss mania, consumers want foods that taste good and satisfy them. Consumer trends expert Katherine Plueger, vice president for strategy and insights at JT Mega, said there’s an opportunity to rethink what indulgence’means. “Ingredients like full-fat dairy and whole milk can enhance sensorial properties like flavor and texture while also boosting protein,” Plueger said. “The goal is to create foods that deliver joy and satisfaction without exacerbating the digestive discomfort some consumers may experience with GLP-1 use.” Plueger advised CPG food manufacturers to “take inspiration from the global rise of small-plate culture, creating smaller portions that are still fun to eat. Spanish tapas, Basque pintxos, Mediterranean mezze, Japanese izakaya and Korean banchan all demonstrate that smaller portions don’t have to equal a less enjoyable experience.” The Journal reported Conagra is testing recipes, leaning into flavors that hark back to mom’s and grandma’s kitchens that would appeal to everyone while keeping GLP-1 patients in mind. General Mills is using artificial intelligence to mimic the reactions of a woman who has dieted all her life and a male gym rat who likes to maximize his protein intake.

Gallup poll: US alcohol consumption remains at record lows - Harpers Wine & Spirit Trade News

El consumo de alcohol en EE.UU. se mantiene en mínimos históricos con 54% de adultos consumidores en 2026, sin cambios respecto a 2025 pero representando una caída de 13 puntos porcentuales desde 2022. Esta tendencia secular afecta directamente la demanda del mercado de bebidas alcohólicas estadounidense, el principal mercado de exportación para productores argentinos. La contracción sostenida en consumo per cápita genera presión sobre volúmenes y rentabilidad, exigiendo reposicionamiento de portafolio hacia segmentos premium, no alcohólicos o funcionales para mantener ingresos.

Texto completo del artículo
New figures from American analytics and advisory firm Gallup detail that US drinking levels remains at record lows. In 2026, the proportion of Americans who consume alcohol remains at 54%, the same figure as last year. This represents a steep decline from 2022 when 67% of survey respondents said they drank alcoholic beverages. The data for this figure comes from its ‘Consumption Habits’ survey based on interviews with US adults aged 18 and older which took place from 1 to 19 July 2026. Gallup’s data stretches back to 1939, with the highest level of alcohol consumption being 71%, from the period 1976 to 1978. Gallup also found that the percentage of Americans’ self-reporting over drinking fell to 13%, also at record lows (this question first assessed in 1978). Before 2000, this figure remained resolutely above 20% reaching its high in 1989 of 35%. Among those Americans who drink alcohol it was detailed that respondents drank an average of 3.2 drinks in the past week, similar to last year’s figure of 2.8. It is down from the average of the past five readings of 3.9, however. Perceived effects of drinking were polled additionally. With the question first asked in 2001, the proportion of respondents who said it was ‘good for health’ is at its lowest point over this period – 6%. Respondent took a kinder view in the early 2000s, with this same figure floating between 20-25%. Those who view it as ‘bad for health’ has jumped from 26% in 2016 to 51% in 2026. President and CEO at International Alliance for Responsible Drinking (IARD) Julian Braithwaite believes the latest Gallup research shows that Americans “are drinking differently – increasingly they are deciding for themselves when, what and how much they drink, including choosing alcohol-free alternatives, as seen in the data”. “In addition, Gallup’s finding that just 13% of U.S. adults say they sometimes drink more than they think they should – the lowest figure Gallup has ever recorded on that question – is encouraging and consistent with the larger global picture,” he said. There has been a significant shift in alcohol health outcomes across the globe, Braithwaite noted. Reflecting on IARD’s own Global Burden of Disease data he added: “Since 2010, age-standardized alcohol-attributable mortality has fallen by approximately 26%, alcohol-attributable disability by 23%, and high-alcohol-use exposure by 10%. “While these are different measures and are not directly comparable, their shared direction strengthens the evidence that meaningful reductions in alcohol-related harm are achievable through sustained, evidence-based action. And, while it is good to see some progress, there is still more to be done to reduce these numbers further.” In a first for the Gallup poll, respondents were asked about their use of non-alcoholic alternatives. Overall, only 17% of people said they have consumed the drinks within the category in the past year. Interestingly, this figure is higher for drinkers (20%) than non-drinkers (14%). Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 9 días · Actualizado 2026-08-21

La semana estuvo dominada por la temporada de resultados del Q2/H1 2026, con señales mixtas en el sector global de bebidas: crecimiento en energéticas y segmentos sin alcohol, frente a debilidad en cerveza mainstream, vino y spirits premium. El movimiento de M&A fue intenso, con la adquisición de Huel por Danone (USD 1.200M) y la compra de Au Vodka por Sazerac como operaciones destacadas. Diageo protagonizó la semana con su reestructuración de 1.900+ empleos y múltiples frentes regulatorios abiertos en India. A nivel local, Argentina mostró señales de contracción en el consumo masivo, con supermercados reduciendo formatos y la cerveza artesanal en crisis de demanda.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Carlsberg eleva su guidance anual al rango 4-6% de crecimiento orgánico gracias a Britvic, superando expectativas y validando la adquisición de la división de soft drinks.
  • Diageo ejecuta reestructuración de USD 1.000M con eliminación de más de 1.900 empleos y enfrenta simultáneamente prohibiciones regulatorias y confiscaciones de producto en India.
  • Danone obtiene luz verde regulatoria de la CMA británica para adquirir Huel por USD 1.200M, consolidando su apuesta por nutrición funcional y bebidas RTD premium.
  • Sazerac adquiere Au Vodka, el vodka RTD enlatado más vendido en Reino Unido, en una operación que puede alcanzar GBP 500M, reforzando el segmento de spirits accesibles.
  • Argentina registra contracción visible en consumo masivo de bebidas: crisis en cerveza artesanal, supermercados migrando a formatos reducidos y caída en frecuencia de compra.
Finanzas

Carlsberg fue la sorpresa positiva de la semana al elevar su guidance anual gracias a la integración de Britvic, mientras Treasury Wine Estates reportó una pérdida de AU$1.000M por write-downs y Brown-Forman rechazó una oferta de adquisición no revelada. Diageo anunció un plan de reestructuración de USD 1.000M con 1.900+ despidos, y Kweichow Moutai registró una caída histórica de ganancias reflejando la desaceleración del consumo premium en China.

Marketing

Coca-Cola Argentina lanzó la campaña 'Nos une el país que queremos' para visibilizar el impacto de su cadena de valor local, mientras que a nivel global la compañía profundizó su estrategia de localización en China con innovación de producto y engagement digital. 7UP ejecutó su mayor reformulación de marca en 15 años con un perfil centrado en lima, y PepsiCo anunció la reformulación de sus marcas líderes incorporando fibra, proteína e ingredientes funcionales para capturar la tendencia salud.

Supply Chain

La cadena de suministro global de bebidas mostró estabilidad aparente en Q3 2026 según Agrowgate, pero persisten presiones inflacionarias en commodities que impactaron a actores como Juice Burst Drinks Co con caídas de margen del 72%. Heineken lanzó un programa de agricultura regenerativa para su cadena de cebada malteada en UK, y la industria del aluminio en Brasil reportó resiliencia con producción récord, señal positiva para el envasado de bebidas en la región.

Ventas / Comercial

En Argentina, los supermercados aceleran la reconversión hacia formatos más chicos y menor costo operativo ante la caída del consumo y la mayor frecuencia de compra con menor ticket. En México, Walmart reportó crecimiento de ticket promedio pero caída en número de transacciones, mientras las ventas de ANTAD avanzaron apenas 1%, reflejando un consumidor masivo bajo presión en toda la región.
