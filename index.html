Pernod Ricard-owned Altos has revealed its inaugural global campaign, designed to broaden the appeal of Tequila in cocktails. The new campaign, called Altos Always Works, aims to bring ‘greater confidence’ to consumers making cocktails at home. The initiative puts the focus on Altos as the ‘ideal’ high-quality Tequila for making Margaritas and Palomas. Sapporo to invest $643m in Carlsberg Southeast Asia joint venture

Sapporo Breweries invertirá $643 millones en una joint venture con Carlsberg para expandir operaciones en el Sudeste Asiático y Hong Kong, con Carlsberg controlando el 75% de la estructura. La venture, con sede en Singapur, se establecerá en diciembre de 2026 y consolida la colaboración existente en Malaysia, Hong Kong y Singapur. Este movimiento de M&A refleja la estrategia de ambos jugadores globales por fortalecer presencia en mercados asiáticos de alto crecimiento, donde compiten marcas premium y regionales con demanda creciente de bebidas alcohólicas.

Leer nota original Ver en español También en: FoodBev Media
Texto completo del artículo
Japanese brewer Sapporo Breweries has announced plans to plough around $643 million (£470m) for a stake in a new joint venture with Danish brewer Carlsberg, as the companies seek to expand their presence across Southeast Asia. The Singapore-based venture, which is expected to be established in December 2026, will be 75% owned by Carlsberg and will cover operations in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong. The partnership builds on the companies’ existing collaboration in Malaysia, Hong Kong and Singapore, extending it to Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia. Sapporo said it aims to increase sales of its flagship Sapporo Premium Beer in the target markets to around ten times 2025 levels by 2035, supported by Carlsberg’s established distribution network and market presence across the region. Under the agreement, Sapporo will grant the joint venture a long-term licence to produce and sell Sapporo Premium Beer. The Japanese brewer said it also expects to generate additional income through dividends, royalty payments and manufacturing-related earnings. The announcement reflects growing efforts by global brewers to strengthen their position in Southeast Asia, where rising incomes and expanding consumer markets are expected to drive long-term demand for premium beer brands. Until now, Sapporo Premium Beer has not been widely available in the UK Carlsberg Britvic is to produce and distribute Sapporo Premium Beer in the UK as part of a tie-up between parent company Carlsberg Group and Sapporo Breweries in southeast Asia and Hong Kong. The joint venture – which will be 75% owned by Carlsberg – also includes an agreement for the Danish brewer to produce and distribute Sapporo in Laos, Vietnam and Cambodia, as well as in Malaysia, Hong Kong and Singapore where it has sold Sapporo since 2024. Tequila Tromba lands in UK

Tequila Tromba ingresa al mercado británico mediante alianza con distribuidor independiente Maverick Drinks, expandiendo su portafolio de cuatro expresiones (blanco, reposado, añejo y extra añejo) a través de canales retail, on-trade, wholesale y e-commerce. Relevante para monitorear competencia en categoría tequila/spirits y modelos de go-to-market en mercados desarrollados.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
Independent distributor Maverick Drinks has partnered with Tequila brand Tromba to launch its portfolio in the UK. According to Tromba, Maverick Drinks’ track record of building craft brands across retail, on-trade, wholesale, and e-commerce channels, and expertise makes it an ideal partner. Tequila Tromba makes its UK debut with four expressions: blanco, reposado, añejo, and extra añejo. The blanco offers bright notes of pepper, mint and caramelised pineapple on the palate. Reposado, aged six to eight months in white oak barrels, presents notes of chocolate, caramel, and dried red fruits, while Tromba Añejo delivers ‘deeper complexity’ achieved by longer maturation. The brand’s extra añejo is aged more than 36 months in American white oak casks, resulting in notes of vanilla, wood, and caramelised agave on the palate. Tromba claims to be a 100% agave, additive-free, small-batch Tequila from Jalisco, created by master distiller Marco Cedano and now led by master tequilero Lilia Quintero. The brand is dedicated to caring for the ecosystems that support agave cultivation. The Agave Rewilding Project (ARP) is Tromba’s ongoing initiative to restore and protect endangered agave species throughout Mexico’s growing regions. So far, the project has planted more than 5,000 endangered agaves in Zapotitlán de Vadillo, as well as 10,000 native trees and plants across two hectares in Atotonilco, Jalisco. Part of UK-based Atom Group, Maverick Drinks returned to the ownership of its founder , Justin Petszaft, in 2024. Alongside Master of Malt and Atom Brands, Maverick Drinks was previously owned by ZX Ventures , the growth and innovation unit of AB InBev.

Carlsberg Group has entered into an agreement with Sapporo Breweries, forming a major joint venture across Southeast Asia and Hong Kong, and partnership in the UK. The joint venture builds on Carlsberg and Sapporo’s existing relationship, having collaborated on the sale of Sapporo Premium Beer in Malaysia, Hong Kong and Singapore since 2024. It will include Carlsberg’s existing operations in these regions, as well as in Laos, Vietnam and Cambodia, and includes long-term exclusive rights to produce and distribute Sapporo Premium Beer in these markets. Sapporo Premium Beer is described as ‘highly complementary” to Carlsberg’s brand portfolio in the JV markets, known for its Japanese provenance and premium brand positioning. A report exposing the truth about how the British beer scene is being unfairly dominated by four global companies has been revealed in a move that urges the Competition and Markets Authority to investigate the matter. Jessica Mason reports. The in-depth report, titled Beer in the UK , has been published by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) and backed by the Society of Independent Brewers and Associates (SIBA) in an effort to tackle the issue of fairness in beer sales in UK pubs and bars. The findings show how, despite consumers asking for local indie beer, the nation’s brewers are being excluded from bar taps across the UK, even when publicans want to stock these beers. The report essentially sets out the reality behind how the nation’s true brewing heritage is being bypassed and also reflects a genuine and authoritative consumer view on how the UK beer scene actually works, or indeed doesn’t work, for beer drinkers and independent brewers, revealing how UK consumer’s preferences and palates are being overlooked in favour of “big beer” taking over. The report also includes the fact that seven of the top ten selling “craft beers” in the UK are made by just four global brewing conglomerates. Additionally, it addressed how many of the UK’s favourite “continental” and “import” lagers are actually brewed in the UK, bypassing authentic marketing, messaging. Added to this, it shows how SIBA data has shown that the demand for independent beer is 280% higher than the share that these beers have of the pub market. CAMRA chairman Ash Corbett-Collins said: “Ordinary drinkers are being short-changed when it comes to choice and quality at the bar. Our report proves how the global players are exploiting the status quo to squeeze out independent brewers, to the detriment of ordinary publicans and beer drinkers.” Speaking to the drinks business , report editor Tim Webb said: “We’re asking the Competition and Markets Authority to launch a market investigation into the on-trade beer market. For pubs, stocking beers that are more interesting than those found in supermarkets – such as cask, local brewery or prize-winning – should be a no-brainer. They just need to be allowed to do it, without pressure from pub companies or global brewers to do otherwise.” Webb told db : “A lot of ‘beer making’ in the UK consists mainly of large foreign-owned companies brewing foreign-branded single-style lagers, often diluted especially for the UK. The market is operated by these firms, excluding smaller independent UK-owned brewers. The latter create jobs because they use more labour-intensive processes; have links with local communities. The big firms cut jobs in the name of efficiency; see local communities mostly as marketing opportunities; and don’t seem to care about our brewing heritage.” Assessing what needs to be done, Webb explained that “unquestionably, we need to revive interest in the beer styles that are the basis of the global craft beer market – many of which have their origins in the UK” and warned that “the big beer firms don’t seem interested or able to do that”. To tackle the issue, Webb assured that CAMRA is already working to make sure the issues of unfairness at present are understood by the government, and especially ministers in the department for business and trade. Corbett-Collins stated that “the government needs to step up, start taking the issue seriously, and take action that matches up to their statements about supporting pubs and the communities they serve.” He explained that “Andy Burnham recently said that ‘people need to be able to look forward to a night out’. The best way he can deliver on that is by ordering a proper market investigation to sort this mess out and deliver a fair deal for publicans and drinkers, and the independent brewers that they want to support.” Corbett-Collins added: “If this report makes you as angry as it makes me, that’s good. CAMRA is fighting for changes to the status quo, and I hope you’ll join us and get involved.” Plus, noting how sitting back and “doing nothing” has made the British brewing scene a “national embarrassment” Webb insisted that things had reached a point where they simply could not be ignored any longer. They cannot grow the UK economy. The way that better, independent, brewers are excluded from the on-trade beer market is scandalous. He pointed out that “brewing has lost half of the UK’s alcoholic beverage market in just 40 years and it is still falling. The big beer companies have no ideas about how to turn this around, yet seem, at every opportunity, to get in the way of the independent brewers with the talent and ideas to do so”. Ché Jayawardena, Sunny Khamkar, and Joseph Chen | July 6, 2026 Over the past two years, the Korean café movement, or “K-Café,” has moved from Seoul’s hyper-competitive coffee scene to the menus of North American independents and global chains alike. Driven by Gen Z’s appetite for novelty and visual drama, East Asian café innovators are pioneering highly customizable, visually striking beverages built on unique texture combinations. According to MenuData, Korean-inspired beverage formats are among the fastest-growing segments on North American foodservice menus, with menu penetration growing at a rate comparable to dirty soda at its peak, while traditional coffee formats remain relatively flat. Social media mentions of key K-Café items, including cheese foam drinks, cloud matcha, and dalgona-style coffee, have surged by over 200% in the past 12 months. Consumers are drawn to K-Café drinks for several overlapping reasons. The format is built around customization: customers choose their base, topper, and add-ins, giving them both agency and a sense of discovery. The drinks are also inherently experiential. A dalgona poured tableside or a matcha layered beneath a cloud of cream cheese foam is a performance as much as a beverage. For a generation that curates its identity online, the visual impact of these drinks makes them perfect for social sharing. The movement is also supply-driven. South Korea’s coffee market has reached USD $13 billion, roughly 31% of the country’s entire beverage market, more than carbonated drinks, teas, and juices combined. With tens of thousands of cafés competing block-to-block in Seoul, Korean operators have been forced to out-innovate one another at a pace Western chains have never faced. That competitive pressure produces a constant stream of new sensory formats that travel globally through social media and franchise expansion. Paris Baguette has surpassed 200 U.S. locations and is targeting 1,000. Gong Cha and several emerging Korean coffee concepts are actively pursuing North American franchise deals. The broader non-alcoholic indulgence trend amplifies all of this. The global bubble tea market, which shares both its aesthetic and its consumer base with the K-Café movement, is forecast to reach USD $9.72 billion by 2035, growing at a 9.5% CAGR. In the U.S., the 2025 bubble tea market alone exceeded $2.5 billion. The consumer looking for an exciting, customizable, non-alcoholic treat is the same consumer the K-Café is built for. The K-Café movement has produced a distinct flavor and format vocabulary. Cheese foam , a lightly salted cream cheese topping, is the movement’s must-have ingredient, delivering salty-sweet contrast over cold matcha or milk tea and making every cup photogenic. Dalgona , the whipped coffee preparation that first went viral in 2020, has matured into a full category, with newer iterations like whipped hojicha, brown sugar, and matcha dalgona all gaining traction. Social media discussions of dalgona formats are up 17% year-over-year. The Korean einspanner , a short espresso topped with a thick pillow of whipped cream, and cloud coffee formats with aerated foam layers round out the core menu. Emerging flavors include black sesame, purple sweet potato, yuzu, and calamansi, all of which offer striking natural color and flavor profiles that photograph as well as they taste. The K-Café trend is more than a passing aesthetic. It’s a signal about where consumer expectations for the café experience are heading. As Korean brands expand physically into North American markets and social media continues to amplify demand, operators who treat K-Café inspiration as a strategic direction rather than a novelty will be positioned nicely to capture the next generation of café consumers. With both Pernod Ricard and Carlsberg reportedly considering applying for Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) in India, how might this reshape the drinks sector in the market? Ron Emler reports. India is the single biggest growth engine for the world’s beverage alcohol sector: it is also a nation of shareholders where the growing middle class and increases in wealth encourages individual investment. That is why global alcohol companies are evaluating ways to unlock value from their Indian operations as they seek to benefit from rising consumption in the fastest-growing big economy. Today there are almost a million people in India of legal drinking age and the market is predicted to continue expanding steadily in comparison to America and Europe where moderation is becoming more prevalent. Total beverage alcohol (TBA) volumes in India expanded at a combined annual growth rate of 3% between 2019 and 2024, capped by a 6% gain in 2024 and a 4% increase in 2025, according to IWSR data. Growth is expected to continue with volumes predicted to rise at a CAGR of +3% between 2024 and 2034. And as consumers move up the scale to more aspirational brands the scope for wider margins from premiumisation grows. That is why we may soon see two of the world’s biggest drinks groups offering to sell minority parts of their Indian businesses on the local market while retaining overall control. The strategy is twofold. It releases cash from a buoyant part of the global business to reinvest, while it further engages the connection between the company’s brands and individual shareholders. Earlier this year Pernod Ricard appointed advisers to consider an Initial Public Offering (IPO) of its business in the subcontinent, its second largest export market and its fastest growing major market despite the legal wrangles which prevent it trading in the Delhi area. The French giant neither confirmed nor denied the story, sheltering behind the normal corporate platitudes about always considering the best interests of shareholders and the use of funds. But such a move would make sense in lowering the group’s debt level. Meanwhile, Carlsberg, which holds about 22% of the Indian beer market through its wholly owned local subsidiary, has confirmed that it has filed official notice to the financial authorities that it is considering an IPO, but details remain confidential. However local reports suggest that an offering worth up to US$700 million is on the cards for later this year if the market conditions are favourable. The offer to the public, its is reported, would not involve the issue of new shares and would value Carlsberg’s Indian company at about US$4 billion. It is not uncommon for big drinks companies to have minority shareholders in subsidiary companies. For instance, Diageo holds a 65% controlling stake in East African Breweries which it plans to sell to Asahi later this year. And Asahi has agreed with East African governments that it will not seek to increase that stake. Although it consolidates its United Spirits India subsidiary its its own accounts, Diageo’s controlling stake in the business is just 54.8% with the remainder owned by institutional and individual shareholders. The same is true of Heineken, which is India’s biggest brewer through its 61.5% controlling holding in United Breweries. After buying these stakes from Vijay Mallya and increasing them as his business empire collapsed, neither is likely to seek to increase their holdings. Indeed, such an exercise would be costly as they would be required to seek 100% control by offering a substantial premium to other shareholders at a time when all drinks companies are seeking to cut their debts, not add to them. Calls for UK beer market competition investigation over domination of global brewers

CAMRA, grupo de consumidores europeo reconocido, solicita investigación de la autoridad de competencia británica (CMA) sobre la concentración del mercado de cerveza en manos de grandes cervecerías globales. El reporte evidencia que cervecerías independientes están siendo excluidas de pubs y bares, limitando la oferta de productos. Esta situación refleja dinámicas de consolidación y control de canales de distribución que afectan la competencia y relevancia estratégica para entender presiones regulatorias en mercados cerveceros europeos.

Texto completo del artículo
CAMRA (The Campaign for Real Ale), which is recognised as one of the most successful consumer groups in Europe, has set out the case for a Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) investigation to look at how the UK market is dominated by a small number of global brewers. In its <i>Beer in the UK</i> report it highlights concerns that independent brewers are being excluded from pubs and bars across the UK which is restricting choice for consumers. It highlights that seven of the top selling ‘craft beers’ in the UK are made by four global brewers while claiming that many of the UK’s favourite continental’ and ‘import’ lagers are brewed in the UK. CAMRA Chairman Ash Corbett-Collins, said that ordinary drinkers are being “short changed” at the bar and called for a proper market investigation to “sort this mess out.” <b>“</b>Our report proves how the global players are exploiting the status quo to squeeze out independent brewers, to the detriment of ordinary publicans and beer drinkers,” he said. “The Government needs to step up, start taking the issue seriously, and take action that matches up to their statements about supporting pubs and the communities they serve.” CAMRA report editor Tim Webb called the fact that UK brewing is dominated by multinational corporations as a “national embarrassment.” He added: “Brewing is a business that competition authorities need to investigate, take apart, and reassemble, so the UK can return to playing a leading role in the world of beer.” Andy Slee, Society of Independent Brewers & Associates (SIBA) Chief Executive said that strong consumer demand for a broad range of beers from independent breweries is being stifled by the strong hold of global brewers on the market. “As Europe’s largest beer consumer group, CAMRA are right to call for change for a UK brewing industry which is dominated by global beer giants which restrict access for British independent breweries. “At the heart of their excellent report is a call for greater consumer choice, transparency around brewery ownership, and lower taxes for small independent breweries – which SIBA wholeheartedly supports.” SIBA’s own Indie Beer campaign has already called for more transparency of ownership in the UK to make it easier for consumers to find beer from independent brewers, rather than global-owned craft beer brands.

Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 9 días · Actualizado 2026-07-03

La semana estuvo dominada por el impacto del Mundial FIFA 2026 como catalizador de consumo de cerveza a nivel global y regional, con marcas como Budweiser y Quilmes reportando incrementos significativos en puntos de venta y proyecciones de demanda. Constellation Brands concentró la atención financiera al superar expectativas en Q1 FY27 impulsado por cerveza, mientras posterga su planta en Veracruz. Diageo protagonizó el flanco negativo con reestructuraciones y despidos en Irlanda y Norteamérica, en contraste con movimientos ofensivos de Sazerac, Pernod Ricard y AB InBev. En Argentina, el efecto mundialista favoreció el canal on-trade, aunque la cerveza artesanal local acusa caída de demanda y presión de costos.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Constellation Brands superó estimaciones en Q1 FY27 pero posterga su planta de Veracruz a finales de 2026, generando dudas sobre su plan de capacidad.
  • Diageo anunció ~150 despidos en Irlanda y recortes en Norteamérica, marcando la reestructuración más visible del sector en la semana.
  • Budweiser creó 11.000 nuevos puntos de venta durante el Mundial para instalar el desayuno como nueva ocasión de consumo de cerveza.
  • Pernod Ricard desinvirtió Bodega Etchart vendiéndola a Molinos Río de la Plata, consolidando el reposicionamiento del grupo en su portafolio de vinos argentinos.
  • Quilmes ganó siete premios en Cannes Lions 2026 y proyecta un alza de consumo de hasta 30% en bares argentinos durante el Mundial.
Finanzas

Constellation Brands superó las estimaciones de ventas netas en Q1 FY27 con crecimiento liderado por cerveza, aunque posterga el inicio de su planta de Veracruz a finales de 2026, generando incertidumbre sobre su capacidad de expansión. Diageo anunció despidos colectivos de aproximadamente 150 posiciones en Irlanda y recortes adicionales en su unidad norteamericana, señalando una reestructuración profunda en medio de un contexto de ingresos bajo presión.

Marketing

Budweiser ejecutó una campaña mundialista de alto impacto creando 11.000 nuevos puntos de venta para posicionar el desayuno como ocasión de consumo de cerveza, mientras Cervecería y Maltería Quilmes cosechó siete premios en Cannes Lions 2026 y proyecta un alza de hasta 30% en consumo en bares durante el torneo. AB InBev evalúa el retorno de su patrocinio FIFA en China, mercado donde enfrenta desafíos estructurales de penetración pese a la visibilidad global de la copa.

Supply Chain

Suntory anunció una inversión de £14,5 millones para asegurar la cadena de suministro de grosella negra en Reino Unido y garantizar la producción de Ribena a largo plazo, mientras Carlsberg Denmark aceleró su transición hacia agricultura regenerativa en sus cultivos clave. En Argentina, ARCA prorrogó hasta fin de año la exención de IVA y Ganancias para la importación de 3.200 productos de primera necesidad, con implicancia directa sobre costos de insumos del sector.

Ventas / Comercial

El canal on-trade en Argentina y Reino Unido registró alzas significativas traccionadas por el Mundial, con proyecciones de hasta 30% de incremento en bares locales y ventas récord en el mercado británico potenciadas además por ola de calor. Cencosud adquirió 21 tiendas de Makro en Colombia, consolidando su ingreso al canal mayorista en la región y reconfigurando la ecuación de distribución para proveedores de bebidas en ese mercado.
