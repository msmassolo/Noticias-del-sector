Briefing para Finanzas

Molson Coors reportó una caída del 4,9% en volumen de marcas de cerveza en Q3 2026, con JPMorgan degradando la acción a 'Underweight' por presión sostenida en márgenes y débil demanda; la acción podría estar hasta un 8% sobrevaluada según análisis de Simply Wall St. PepsiCo enfrenta presión en su guidance de rentabilidad mientras lleva una disputa de marca ante la Corte Suprema, aunque su estrategia zero-sugar busca sostener la demanda de bebidas. Briefing para Marketing

Coca-Cola apuesta por una campaña navideña disruptiva bajo el concepto 'Caos do Natal' para reforzar la asociación emocional de la marca con la temporada, mientras Starbucks/Alsea activa bebidas de temporada y colecciones temáticas (Snoopy) para recuperar tráfico en cafeterías. Los fundadores de Poppi —vendida a PepsiCo— lanzan Jetty, una nueva marca de hidratación que apunta directamente a desafiar a Gatorade en un mercado valuado en USD 13.000 millones, señal de que el segmento funcional/hidratación sigue siendo el de mayor dinamismo competitivo. Briefing para Supply Chain

Carlsberg desinvierte las operaciones de Britvic en Brasil (marca Maguary), lo que señala una reorganización de activos en la región y puede generar oportunidades o disrupciones en cadenas de distribución de jugos y néctares en el mercado latinoamericano. El sector vitivinícola enfrenta doble presión: los costos de producción en Italia subieron 8,9% y las exportaciones cayeron 6,2% en el primer semestre de 2026, lo que anticipa tensión en precios de vino importado y posibles ajustes en portafolios de distribuidores locales. El reporte Agrowgate Q4 2026 advierte sobre estabilidad relativa pero con riesgos latentes en materias primas agrícolas, contexto que exige monitoreo estrecho de contratos de insumos clave para bebidas.

Briefing para Ventas / Comercial

Heineken reportó el retorno al crecimiento de su negocio cervecero en México en Q3 2026, confirmando resiliencia en mercados latinoamericanos frente a la debilidad global del segmento. Las viñas chilenas están rediseñando su propuesta comercial ante la caída estructural del consumo de vino, lo que puede presionar a distribuidores y retailers argentinos a revisar espacios de góndola y mix de categoría. El lanzamiento de Jetty (ex-Poppi) con foco en retail masivo y el crecimiento de Huel superando £100M en ventas minoristas refuerzan la presión competitiva en canales modernos por espacios dedicados a bebidas funcionales y de hidratación, categoría aún subdesarrollada en el punto de venta local.

Joyburst embraces functional beverage trends

Joyburst, empresa canadiense, capitaliza la convergencia de categorías de bebidas funcionales (hidratación, wellness, proteína) impulsada por cambios en el comportamiento del consumidor. La hidratación, históricamente vinculada a ocasiones deportivas, se posiciona como consumo diario en múltiples momentos (mañana, trabajo, post-ejercicio, viajes). Esta tendencia refleja oportunidades estratégicas en el segmento de bebidas funcionales, donde la fragmentación de ocasiones de consumo requiere portfolios más diversificados y adaptados a necesidades específicas del consumidor moderno.

Texto completo del artículo
Hydration. Wellness. Protein. The lines between beverage categories continue to blur as consumers look for products that support different needs throughout the day. For Brad Woodgate, founder of Joyburst, Mississauga, Ontario, this blurring of lines means nothing less than opportunity. “Hydration used to be a sports occasion,” he says. “Today, it’s becoming an everyday behavior. Consumers are drinking hydration products when they wake up, at work, after a workout, while traveling, during a night out, and really throughout the day. It’s moved well beyond the traditional sports drink consumer.” Woodgate adds that consumers also have become more educated about what they’re putting into their bodies. “They want function, but they don’t necessarily want the sugar, calories, or ingredients that historically came with it,” he says. “That creates a massive opportunity, but it also creates a very crowded market. The brands that win will be the ones that deliver real functionality, great taste, convenience and a brand people actually want to be part of.” Woodgate also points to another important shift in the beverage market — wellness has become mainstream. “You don’t have to be a professional athlete to care about hydration or protein anymore,” he explains. “A mom heading to work, a teenager going to school, someone sitting at a desk all day, or someone going to the gym can all have very different reasons for choosing a functional beverage. “And consumers increasingly want convenience,” he continues. “They don’t want five different products to solve five different needs. The consumer isn’t asking, ‘What beverage category should I buy?’ They’re asking, ‘What do I need right now?’” Founded on the principle of living a healthier lifestyle, Joyburst is continuing its goal to support consumers’ healthy lifestyles through a diverse drink portfolio. When Joyburst first launched, Woodgate says the idea was simple: create products people genuinely want to drink while removing some of the compromises traditionally associated with beverages. “What’s changed is the size of the opportunity,” he explains. “We’re no longer thinking about Joyburst as a hydration company. We’re building a portfolio around the way consumers actually live. Different occasions, same consumer. That’s the opportunity we see.” For example, Woodgate points out that Joyburst Renew Hydration gives consumers hydration and electrolytes without sugar, while the brand’s Protein Coffee brings 30 grams of protein and zero sugar into an everyday coffee occasion. “Protein Soda takes protein into a completely different beverage occasion,” he says. “And we’re continuing to develop products around ingredients like creatine and other functional benefits.” Joyburst also recently launched new hydration drink stick-packs. “We want Joyburst to show up in those moments,” Woodgate says. “The stick packs are about portability and convenience.” As for what differentiates Joyburst’s portfolio from others in the market, Woodgate says the biggest difference is that the brand is not trying to build one beverage. “We’re building a functional beverage platform,” he says. “That’s where our innovation comes from. And we’re very deliberate about it. I don’t want to launch a product just to add another SKU to the portfolio. If we’re going to put something new on a shelf, I want consumers to look at it and say, ‘I’ve never seen that before.’” Woodgate adds that although Renew Hydration has been the foundation of Joyburst and put the brand on the map, what’s exciting now is seeing the portfolio develop beyond hydration. “Protein Coffee has been a particularly strong innovation because it combines two huge consumer behaviors — coffee and protein — into one product,” he says. “Protein Soda has also created a completely new conversation for us. And Protein Soda MAX, with 30 grams of protein, takes that concept into the active nutrition space. What’s most exciting to me isn’t necessarily one individual SKU. It’s the opportunity to create multiple major product platforms within one brand. “We recently crossed $100 million in annual sales, and I don't look at that as the finish line,” Woodgate continues. “I look at it as proof that the model works. Now the question is how many more big platforms we can build.” With innovation being a big part of Joyburst’s philosophy, Woodgate says the brand is nowhere near finished when it comes to expanding its portfolio. “We’re working across hydration, protein, creatine, energy, coffee and functional beverages, and some of the things we’re developing today will look very different from what people currently expect from a beverage company,” he explains. “But we’re not interested in innovation for the sake of innovation. “I’ve launched hundreds of consumer products over my career. One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned is that having an idea is easy. Building something consumers actually want, retailers want to carry, and that can scale profitably is much harder,” Woodgate continues. “So, we’ll continue to move quickly, but we’re going to be disciplined about what earns the Joyburst name.” As for Joyburst’s distribution footprint, Woodgate notes that the United States has become a massive part of the brand’s growth story. Joyburst is currently available in major retailers including Costco, Target, Walmart, Sam’s Club, BJ’s, Meijer, CVS, Dollar Tree, and other regional and specialty retailers, in addition to eCommerce channels. “One thing I’m particularly proud of is the diversity of our distribution,” Woodgate says. “We’re not dependent on one channel or one type of consumer. Hydration can live in club, grocery, and mass. Protein Soda can live in soda and active nutrition. Protein Coffee can live in coffee and functional beverage. “That gives us a lot of room to grow within the same consumer base,” he continues. “And we’re still early.” As for Joyburst’s distribution footprint abroad, Woodgate says that Canada remains an important market for the brand as it continues to build its international business. “International expansion has always been part of my background,” he explains. “I’ve spent more than 25 years building consumer brands and have taken products into dozens of countries. “What makes Joyburst particularly interesting internationally is that the trends we’re building around are global,” he continues. “Consumers everywhere are looking for less sugar, more function, better nutrition and greater convenience. Hydration is global. Protein is global. Wellness is global. That's a pretty powerful combination.” With eyes on the future, Joyburst is investing heavily in experiential marketing, creators, partnerships, events and sampling. “Beverages are uniquely experiential,” Woodgate explains. “You can see a product on a screen, but the moment somebody actually tastes it, everything changes. And we’re constantly looking for ways to make Joyburst culturally relevant.” For instance, Joyburst launched Disney's Moana x Joyburst, the brand’s newest Renew Hydration flavor: Pineapple Coconut, in celebration of Disney’s live-action adaptation of “Moana” that released earlier this year. “Disney has been an incredible partner for Joyburst, and the Moana partnership felt very natural,” Woodgate says. “The inspiration for Pineapple Coconut came directly from the world and feeling of Moana. It’s tropical, it’s adventurous and it has that feeling of being somewhere completely different. “We didn’t want to simply put a Disney logo on an existing product,” he continues. “We wanted to create something that felt native to the partnership, which is why Pineapple Coconut was developed specifically around the Moana collaboration. We’ve now had the opportunity to work with Disney across several major properties, and each partnership has shown us how powerful the intersection of entertainment, product and consumer engagement can be.” Alongside partnerships, Woodgate explains that Joyburst is engaging with consumers differently from traditional CPG companies. “We’re building a functional beverage platform. That’s where our innovation comes from. And we’re very deliberate about it. I don’t want to launch a product just to add another SKU to the portfolio. If we’re going to put something new on a shelf, I want consumers to look at it and say, ‘I’ve never seen that before.” “Retail is obviously incredibly important. Sampling, demos, merchandising and getting the product into people’s hands still matter enormously,” he says. “But we’re also building a social-first brand. TikTok, Instagram and other social platforms allow us to have a real conversation with consumers. We’re able to see what people are responding to, what they’re asking for and what they actually care about.” As for what the future holds for hydration drinks and where Joyburst fits in with that future, Woodgate anticipates that hydration is going to become less of a category and more of an everyday behavior. “The consumer doesn’t really care whether something is technically classified as a sports drink, hydration drink, or functional beverage,” he says. “They care about what it does for them. That’s going to push the category toward more functionality and more personalization. “Zero sugar will continue to matter,” he continues. “Electrolytes will continue to matter. But I think we’re going to see hydration increasingly intersect with protein, energy, creatine, vitamins, recovery, and other functional benefits. That’s exactly where we want Joyburst to play.” As for what aspirations Woodgate has for Joyburst in the next few years, he says the ambition is “very big.” “We recently crossed $100 million in annual sales, which is an incredible milestone for a company that was founded only a few years ago,” he says. “But honestly, I don’t spend much time looking backwards. “I’ve spent more than 25 years building consumer brands,” he continues. “I’ve started companies from scratch, built products that have generated billions in sales, managed large teams, and taken brands into markets around the world. Joyburst is the next chapter.” Ultimately, Woodgate’s vision for Joyburst involves building the brand to become a billion-dollar global beverage company. “We’ve never raised outside venture capital, and that has allowed us to think long term and make decisions based on what we believe is right for the business rather than what we need to do for the next funding round,” he explains. “The next few years are about continuing to innovate, expanding distribution, building a global brand and creating products consumers genuinely love. “We’ve proven that Joyburst can get to $100 million,” Woodgate continues. “Now I want to see how far we can take it. And that’s honestly the part that gets me most excited.” Looking for a reprint of this article? From high-res PDFs to custom plaques, order your copy today ! 'Caos do Natal': a aposta da Coca-Cola para renovar campanha e reforçar tradição da marca

Esta estrategia refleja un giro en la comunicación de marca hacia autenticidad y conexión emocional, buscando reforzar la asociación de Coca-Cola con momentos compartidos familiares en contexto de creciente competencia por presencia en temporada de alto consumo en Latinoamérica.

Texto completo del artículo
Campanha de Natal 2026 da Coca-Cola: marca explora os imprevistos e o lado caótico das celebrações para destacar a importância dos momentos compartilhados (Divulgação) A Coca-Cola lançou nesta segunda-feira, 9, sua campanha de Natal de 2026, que retrata o caos dos preparativos e defende que a celebração não precisa ser perfeita para criar momentos inesquecíveis. A iniciativa inclui filme, mais de 300 caravanas, ativações presenciais, experiências digitais e parcerias comerciais. A pressão para preparar a ceia, a casa cheia de gente, os presentes de última hora e a expectativa de que tudo saia perfeito. Em vez de retratar o Natal como uma celebração sem contratempos, a Coca-Cola decidiu explorar justamente o lado caótico da data em sua campanha de 2026. A iniciativa, lançada nesta segunda-feira, 9, destaca que, "apesar do estresse", esses momentos ajudam a fazer do Natal uma data única. "Acho que a principal mensagem é que não precisamos de um Natal perfeito para viver momentos inesquecíveis ao lado de quem amamos. Às vezes, a bagunça faz parte da celebração. É preciso abrir o coração e entender que, muitas vezes, são justamente essas experiências que tornam os encontros tão especiais", afirma Raquel Ribeiro, diretora de marketing da marca Coca-Cola no Brasil, em entrevista exclusiva à EXAME. A campanha também brinca com quem diz odiar o Natal por esses motivos. No filme “Spread the Spirit” , que estreia agora nas plataformas digitais e chega à TV em 1º de dezembro, a proposta é usar a temática para falar sobre o que a marca considera o principal significado da data: estar junto. Segundo a executiva, a escolha do tema também acompanha uma estratégia mais ampla da companhia, que busca associar a marca aos momentos de consumo compartilhado . Embora Ribeiro não detalhe projeções de vendas ou metas comerciais específicas para a iniciativa, a executiva reconhece a importância da temporada para os negócios. "O Natal é um dos momentos do ano mais importantes para a Coca-Cola, tanto pela conexão histórica e afetiva que construímos com a data quanto pelas oportunidades que ela representa para o negócio." A relação da Coca-Cola com o Natal é um clássico. Ao longo de décadas, a companhia construiu uma associação tão forte com a data que alguns de seus elementos visuais se tornaram parte do imaginário natalino global. Um dos exemplos mais conhecidos é a representação moderna do Papai Noel . Em campanhas a partir da década de 1930, a Coca-Cola trabalhou com o ilustrador Haddon Sundblom , que ajudou a popularizar a imagem do personagem com roupa vermelha, barba branca e expressão acolhedora. A marca não criou o Papai Noel nem suas cores, mas teve papel importante na consolidação dessa representação na cultura popular. No Brasil, outro símbolo dessa associação são as Caravanas de Natal . O que começou como uma ativação de marketing se transformou em uma tradição aguardada por famílias em diferentes regiões do país. Para Ribeiro, preservar esses símbolos é parte importante da construção de marca, mas não significa repetir a mesma comunicação todos os anos. "Somos uma marca com mais de 140 anos de história e um dos nossos grandes desafios é justamente saber equilibrar tradição e inovação. Buscamos preservar tudo aquilo que faz parte da relação afetiva das pessoas com a Coca-Cola, ao mesmo tempo em que encontramos novas maneiras de conversar com diferentes gerações e acompanhar as transformações culturais", reforça. Segundo a diretora da Coca-Cola, a operação das caravanas também busca incorporar características culturais dos locais por onde passa. Como exemplo, ela cita a participação de ajudantes do Papai Noel dançando passinho do funk no Rio de Janeiro, em 2025. "Essa proximidade com os territórios, construída ao lado dos nossos fabricantes, nos ajuda a entender as particularidades de cada região e transformar esse conhecimento em experiências que realmente façam sentido para as pessoas." Além do filme e das caravanas, a campanha de 2026 reúne ativações presenciais e digitais. Em São Paulo, a tradicional árvore de Natal da Coca-Cola será instalada no Parque Villa-Lobos, com visitação aberta ao público a partir de 14 de novembro. No ambiente digital, a companhia retoma o Meu Papel Mágico , experiência criada para a América Latina que permite personalizar papéis de presente com fotografias, imagens e mensagens próprias. A ferramenta é gratuita e pode ser acessada por meio de um chatbot no WhatsApp . Neste ano, a novidade é a inclusão de um espaço dedicado a mensagens pessoais nos papéis personalizados. A companhia também amplia sua presença no varejo e em ocasiões de consumo por meio de parcerias comerciais. Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 9 días · Actualizado 2026-11-06

La semana estuvo dominada por la temporada de reportes de resultados Q3 2026, con un desempeño polarizado entre ganadores y perdedores dentro de la industria global de bebidas. Monster Beverage y Diageo superaron expectativas, mientras Molson Coors recortó su guidance anual y AB InBev enfrentó presiones en China y EE.UU. En América Latina, Arca Continental, Ambev y FEMSA mostraron resiliencia operativa con crecimientos en ventas y EBITDA, consolidando a la región como un pilar de estabilidad para el sistema Coca-Cola. Las categorías sin azúcar, sin alcohol y energéticas continuaron siendo los motores de crecimiento estructural en un contexto de demanda débil para la cerveza tradicional.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Molson Coors recortó su guidance 2026 tras caída del 4.9% en volumen de cerveza en Q3, citando debilitamiento de demanda e inflación en mercados clave.
  • Monster Beverage superó USD 2.5 mil millones en ventas trimestrales por primera vez, con crecimiento internacional del 28%, aunque la presión de márgenes castigó su cotización.
  • Diageo reportó crecimiento orgánico inesperado del 1.4% en Q1 2027, liderado por Guinness con expansión de doble dígito, validando su estrategia de recortes selectivos de precios.
  • Heineken adquirió el control de Better Drinks en Brasil, dueña de las marcas Praya y Baer-Mate, en un movimiento estratégico para ganar escala en el mayor mercado cervecero de América Latina.
  • Las bebidas sin azúcar duplicaron ventas para Coca-Cola y PepsiCo en Q3, mientras los cócteles enlatados crecieron 28% en EE.UU., redefiniendo las prioridades de portafolio y marketing del sector.
Finanzas

Molson Coors recortó su guidance para 2026 tras reportar una caída del 4.9% en volumen de cerveza en Q3, citando presiones macroeconómicas en Reino Unido e inflación persistente, mientras sus acciones acumulan revisiones a la baja. En contraste, Monster Beverage superó los USD 2.5 mil millones en ventas trimestrales por primera vez con crecimiento internacional del 28%, aunque la presión de márgenes generó una caída en su cotización after-hours.

Marketing

Las bebidas sin azúcar duplicaron ventas para Coca-Cola y PepsiCo en el trimestre de septiembre, consolidando esta plataforma como el eje central de la comunicación y el portafolio de ambas compañías a nivel global. El mercado de cócteles enlatados en EE.UU. creció un 28% anual, con cerveceras ganando terreno frente a las destileras tradicionales, lo que redefine la estrategia de categoría de jugadores como Diageo y Pernod Ricard.

Supply Chain

Coca-Cola planea construir una nueva planta en el sureste de México para reducir costos logísticos desde Toluca, señalando que el modelo de distribución centralizado ya no es sostenible a escala regional. Tilray consolidó su red de cervecerías en EE.UU. a solo seis instalaciones con reducción de workforce, mientras Colombia avanzó en una propuesta de nuevos impuestos al alcohol de hasta el 19%, sumando presión regulatoria en la región.

Ventas / Comercial

Arca Continental reportó crecimiento del 5.9% en ventas y 6.2% en EBITDA en Q3 2026, con precios por encima de la inflación anticipados para los próximos períodos, reforzando su posición como el embotellador más sólido de Latinoamérica. Heineken adquirió el control de Better Drinks en Brasil —dueña de Praya y Baer-Mate— consolidando su acceso al canal on-trade y expandiendo su portafolio en el mercado cervecero más grande de la región.
