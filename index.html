Texto completo del artículo
William Grant & Sons (WG&S) has released its results for the year ending 31 December 2025, detailing a difficult year as challenging headwinds continue to prevail. The Scotch giant reported a turnover of £1,758m, with a profit before tax of £337m – 13% lower than 2024. WG&S said that, despite difficult conditions including ongoing geopolitical and trade pressures and weak consumer demand, its focus has continued to be long-term shareholder growth. The business implemented significant brand investment and expansion of distillery capacity over 2025, acquiring the well-known Scotch whisky The Famous Grouse and the Naked Malt brands, along with continuing its work on the expansion of the Girvan Distillery on the west coast of Scotland. Overall volumes were down year-on-year, with performance varying across the portfolio – WG&S said that it saw “resilient contributions” in select areas of the business, including share growth in several categories and ongoing progress in some key markets. The distiller would not expand on which markets it had seen progress in, or the specifics of the volume decline when approached by Harpers . Scotch sales over 2025 were hampered by tariffs implemented by President Donald Trump – the US being the category’s largest market for value sales, and third by volume. As Harpers previously reported , value sales to the United States fell 4% across the whole of 2025, with volume declining by 9%. However, this year the situation for Scotch exports has somewhat improved, with tariffs lifted by the US government in July 2026, along with a Free Trade Agreement between India and the UK coming into force which significantly cut whisky tariffs. Other challenges – such as the cost of doing business in the UK and weakening consumer demand – remain. Its CFO, Graeme Jenkins, said: “2025 was another challenging year for the spirits industry, with external pressures affecting performance across many of the company’s key markets. Against this backdrop, our focus has been on outstanding execution, while at the same time maintaining our long-term approach to brand and distillery investment. “While the results reflect the current operating environment, our confidence in the long-term fundamentals of the spirits industry remains strong. The company will continue to take those actions that ensure we are well positioned to benefit when market conditions improve.” WG&S is a sixth-generation independent family-owned distiller founded by William Grant in 1887.The company distils, bottles and distributes of the world’s leading Scotch brands, along with other spirits. Carlsberg Britvic has launched a 10-year partnership with The Rivers Trust to support the restoration of rivers, wetlands and floodplains near its manufacturing site in Rugby, UK. The partnership forms part of Carlsberg Group’s wider commitment to replenish 100% of water consumed at production sites in high-risk areas by 2032. Rugby has been identified as a high-risk site under the group’s water risk assessment, which considers factors including water availability, quality, regulation and community-related risks. The initiative will support multiple nature-based projects across the Warwickshire Avon catchment, approximately 15 miles from Carlsberg Britvic’s Rugby site. Projects will include work on the River Leam and other local waterways to reconnect and enhance floodplains, create wetland habitats and improve the landscape’s ability to store and slow water. The projects have been selected based on their position within the wider river system, ecological condition and proximity to communities vulnerable to flooding. They are intended to improve river health while helping to reduce flood risk downstream. The first project is due to begin in October and will reconnect the River Alne with its natural floodplain, while creating a network of storage wetlands designed to slow, store and manage water across the landscape. According to Carlsberg Britvic, the project is expected to reduce flood risk for downstream communities including Henley-in-Arden and Alcester, while supporting biodiversity through new wetland and woodland habitats. The work will be delivered by Severn Rivers Trust, The Rivers Trust’s member organisation for the region. The partnership supports Carlsberg Britvic’s target to replenish 100% of the water used at its Rugby site, equivalent to approximately one million cubic metres annually, by 2032. The company has set an interim target of replenishing 400,000 cubic metres per year by 2028. Based on current assumptions, the projects are estimated to replenish more than 328,000 cubic metres of water annually, with their performance set to be monitored over time. The initiative represents Carlsberg Group’s first water replenishment project in Europe and forms part of its broader water stewardship and sustainability programme. Paul Davies, CEO of Carlsberg Britvic, said: “Water is at the heart of every drink we make, so protecting this precious resource is not only important for our business, it’s a responsibility we take seriously.” He added that the partnership would help turn the company’s water replenishment ambitions into practical action by working with experts and local stakeholders. Mark Lloyd, CEO of The Rivers Trust, said the partnership would enable “practical, nature-based solutions” to restore rivers and catchments, support biodiversity and deliver measurable water benefits. George Clark, programme manager at Severn Rivers Trust, said the investment would support work with farmers and land managers to reconnect rivers with floodplains, create ponds and wetlands and restore natural processes. The projects are intended to support river and wetland health, biodiversity and community resilience beyond the boundaries of Carlsberg Britvic’s Rugby manufacturing site.
