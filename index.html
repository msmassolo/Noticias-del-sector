El ciberataque a Asahi Group reportado en la nota de riesgos es una alerta directa para auditar la resiliencia digital de la cadena de suministro propia, especialmente en sistemas de planificación y logística.

Walmart same-store sales slowdown could portend trouble for packaged food brands

La desaceleración podría presagiar contracciones en volumen y márgenes para proveedores de bebidas, independientemente de factores de crecimiento empresarial de Walmart, indicando un posible endurecimiento de las condiciones de mercado para el sector.

Walmart had an <a href="https://stock.walmart.com/" target="_blank" rel="">outstanding Q2</a> by most metrics, beating earnings and revenue estimates and even increasing its financial outlook for the remainder of its fiscal year – but it was the sluggish same-store sales growth that sent the company’s stock value tumbling by more than 9% on Thursday. The same-store sales growth rate indicator excludes newly opened and recently closed stores to provide a more precise measurement of sales growth, and Wall Street analysts took notice when its Q2 report showed the bellwether retailer’s performance of just 2.6%. Wall Street analysts’ expectations set the growth figure at 3.5%, and a year ago, the same-store sales ratio was at 4.6%. The economic indicator is not just bad news for Walmart; as the world’s largest retailer, the news could signal a broader economic slowdown. For packaged food manufacturers and other retailers, softer growth could also mean consumers are becoming more selective about what they buy, increasing pressure on brands to demonstrate value and on retailers to compete more aggressively on price. Walmart explained on Thursday that the poor showing in same-store sales (also known as comparative or comp sales) was partly driven by a roughly 1.25% decline in its pharmacy business, due to the implementation of the Maximum Fair Price (MFP) regulation, which placed Medicare price caps on some drugs starting at the beginning of 2026. Walmart also is feeling pressure in its pharmacy business from the release of generic versions of some GLP-1 drugs, reducing the company’s revenue from the popular weight-loss drugs. Walmart CFO John David Rainey explained that Walmart’s “core categories” of groceries and general merchandise “have been extremely consistent, largely in the 3% to 4% range on a quarterly basis.” The pharmacy tailwind, particularly from GLP-1 sales, is softening, though, he said. “When looking at the Walmart US total comps, including health and wellness, during each of FY 2025 and FY 2026, we realized 100 basis points of a tailwind from sales of GLP-1 branded drugs,” he said. “In FY 2027, the benefit from GLP-1 is expected to be roughly half that amount, as script count growth is more than offset by price mix headwinds.” Although the retailer took a hit in its physical store sales, due in part to its pharmacy business, Walmart’s e-commerce business grew significantly, according to CEO John Furner. “We grew units and transactions, and membership fee revenue was at an all-time high on growth of 17%,” he said. “We delivered another quarter of strong e-commerce growth, up 23% globally, including the 10th consecutive quarter of growth over 20% for Walmart US.” The company expanded its Walmart+ loyalty program and online Marketplace platform to regions outside the US, Furner explained. “The sustained growth we have seen in e-commerce across the company over multiple years points to more than a digital success story. It is evidence that customers and members increasingly choose Walmart because they know we combine low prices across a broad assortment with speed and convenience,” he said. “The mix of e-commerce for Walmart International is now 30%, with strong growth again this quarter in China, India and Canada.” Walmart reported that it has received $2.9 billion in a tariff refund from the federal government, the result of a <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/02/20/supreme-court-strikes-down-trumps-tariff-powers-under-ieepa/" target="_blank" rel="">US Supreme Court decision</a> in February that declared illegal the Trump administration’s tariff regime under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The refund is roughly three times the size of one of <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/20/target-stock-rises-on-tariff-refund-and-q2-growth/" target="_blank" rel="">Target’s</a>, which reported a pre-tax refund of $994 million on Aug. 19. That helped Walmart’s adjusted operating income growth of 17.4% for the quarter. Furner said Walmart plans to invest the refund into price cuts. Rainey explained that Walmart is prioritizing investment in grocery and merchandising categories, but did not give details. “Looking forward, our Q3 guidance reflects the continued impact of pricing actions taken in Q2, alongside continued prioritization of tariff refunds into price investment,” Rainey said. Walmart US delivered more than 11,000 “price rollbacks” in Q2, up from 7,200 in the first quarter, according to Furner. He noted that most quarters the company rolls back prices on roughly 5,000 products. The price cuts are aimed at inflation-weary consumers who are becoming <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/17/how-brands-and-retailers-can-navigate-inflation-weary-shopping-habits/" target="_blank" rel="">increasingly price conscious</a>. “We’re investing heavily in price because customers need us to and because we believe it drives market share gains over time,” Furner said. “Our price gaps to conventional grocers here in the US are strong, and they continue to widen.”

Cayeron las ventas en supermercados, mayoristas y shoppings en junio, según el Indec

Las ventas de consumo masivo cayeron en junio según el Indec: supermercados bajaron 1%, mayoristas 1,4% y shoppings 6,2% a precios constantes. Los tres canales de distribución formales muestran deterioro tanto mensual como interanual, señalando debilidad sostenida en la demanda de productos de consumo. Esta caída impacta directamente en el desempeño de bebidas, principal categoría en estos canales, y refleja contracción del poder adquisitivo del consumidor argentino que presiona márgenes y volúmenes de la industria.

El consumo masivo volvió a mostrar signos de deterioro en junio, de acuerdo con los datos que difundió el Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos (Indec) sobre la actividad de los tres canales formales más relevantes del comercio minorista. Los supermercados, los autoservicios mayoristas y los centros de compras tuvieron bajas mensuales en sus ventas de 1%, 1,4% y 6,2% respectivamente, medidas a precios constantes, Los tres canales cayeron además en la comparación interanual y en los acumulados del primer semestre del año. En el caso de los supermercados, la comparación interanual muestra una retracción de 3,1%, mientras que el acumulado del semestre arrojó una baja de 2,8% frente a igual período de 2025. Medidas a precios corrientes, las ventas de los supermercados sumaron $2.484.975,5 millones en junio, con un incremento de 23,7% respecto al mismo mes del año pasado. Ese salto nominal responde en gran medida al efecto de la inflación sobre los precios, más que a una recuperación real de las cantidades vendidas. Los rubros que más subieron en los súpers En ese universo de precios corrientes, y con la salvedad de que se trata de variaciones nominales y no de valores reales, los rubros que mostraron los aumentos más pronunciados fueron Carnes, con una suba interanual de 39%, y Electrónicos y artículos para el hogar, con 35,1%. También se destacaron Verdulería y frutería, con 30,6%, y Alimentos preparados y rotisería, con 23,6%. En el otro extremo, Indumentaria, calzado y textiles para el hogar fue el único rubro que retrocedió, con una caída de 7,4% frente a junio de 2025. Por jurisdicción, las mayores subas en las ventas a precios corrientes se dieron en Neuquén, con 34,4%, y en La Pampa, con 34,0%. Detrás se ubicaron Río Negro, con 30,8%, y Jujuy y Formosa, ambas con 30,4 por ciento. Qué pasó en los mayoristas Los autoservicios mayoristas registraron un comportamiento parecido al de los supermercados. La serie desestacionalizada cayó 1,4% respecto de mayo, mientras que la comparación interanual arrojó una baja de 3,1 por ciento. El acumulado del primer semestre del año quedó 3,0% por debajo del mismo período de 2025. A precios corrientes, las ventas de este canal totalizaron 381.104,3 millones de pesos en junio, con un incremento nominal de 23,1% frente a igual mes del año anterior. Al igual que en los supermercados, ese número refleja principalmente el impacto de los precios. Entre los rubros, y siempre en valores corrientes, las mayores subas interanuales se dieron en Almacén, con 27,4%, y en Lácteos, con 27,1%. Le siguieron Panadería, con 25,7%, y Artículos de limpieza y perfumería, con 23,7%. Electrónicos y artículos para el hogar fue el único grupo que cayó, con una baja de 1,9 por ciento. Por jurisdicción, la Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires fue la que menos creció, con una variación de apenas 0,7% respecto a junio de 2025. En los 24 partidos del Gran Buenos Aires, la suba fue de 22,8%, mientras que en el resto del país las ventas avanzaron 24,8 por ciento. Los shoppings, la caída más marcada Los centros de compras fueron el canal que mostró el retroceso más pronunciado del mes. La serie desestacionalizada cayó 6,2% respecto de mayo, la comparación interanual arrojó una baja de 4,9% y el acumulado del semestre quedó 2,7% por debajo de igual período de 2025. A precios corrientes, las ventas de los shoppings sumaron 685.447 millones de pesos en junio, un 13,4% más que en el mismo mes del año pasado. Ese incremento nominal fue, de los tres canales relevados, el más moderado. Por rubro, y en valores corrientes, el mayor salto interanual se dio en Amoblamientos, decoración y textiles para el hogar, con 46,3%, seguido por Ropa y accesorios deportivos, con 37,9%. También crecieron Diversión y esparcimiento, con 29,2%, y Perfumería y farmacia, con 20,1%. En contraste, Indumentaria, calzado y marroquinería, el rubro con mayor peso dentro de las ventas totales de los shoppings, fue el único que cayó, con una baja de 2,6% frente a junio de 2025. Por región, se observa que CABA concentró ventas por 193.589 millones de pesos, con un alza interanual de 18,5%. En los 24 partidos del Gran Buenos Aires, las ventas llegaron a 224.091 millones de pesos, con un incremento de 8,3%. La región Pampeana, que agrupa al resto de la provincia de Buenos Aires junto con Córdoba, Entre Ríos, La Pampa y Santa Fe, sumó 145.766 millones de pesos, con una suba de 13,2%, siempre hablando de la comparación interanual en términos nominales. Cuyo fue la región con el mayor crecimiento relativo entre las agrupaciones del interior, con un avance de 18,3% en sus ventas, aunque muy por debajo de la inflación del período, que fue de 33,8 por ciento.

El eCommerce argentino creció 28% en el primer semestre: vendió 248 millones de artículos

El eCommerce argentino creció 28% en el primer semestre de 2026, facturando $20 mil millones con 181,5 millones de órdenes. El segmento de Alimentos y Bebidas lidera el ranking de facturación. Este dato es crítico para evaluar la migración del canal de distribución directo hacia plataformas digitales, impactando la estrategia omnichannel y la relación con retailers tradicionales en un contexto de consolidación del comercio online.

Texto completo del artículo
El eCommerce (o comercio electrónico) argentino facturó casi $20 mil millones durante los primeros seis meses de 2026, logrando así un crecimiento del 28% frente al mismo período del año pasado. Los datos surgen del estudio de mitad de año presentado por la Cámara Argentina de Comercio Electrónico (CACE). Durante ese período se concretaron 181,5 millones de órdenes de compra, un 21% más que en el primer semestre de 2025. Los rubros que lideraron el ranking de facturación fueron Alimentos y bebidas, Electro (Línea Blanca) y Herramientas y construcción. En total, se comercializaron 248 millones de artículos con un aumento interanual del 22%. Quiénes lideraron el ranking: “Crece como un hábito cotidiano de consumo” Al analizar el volumen por unidades, Alimentos y bebidas se posicionó primera en el ranking, seguida por Herramientas y construcción, y Productos para el cuidado personal. Además, el ticket promedio por cada orden de compra fue de $107.597 pesos a lo largo de esta primera mitad de 2026. En cuanto a las compras online internacionales, un 18% de los encuestados afirma haber concretado su primera compra al exterior durante 2026. Además, la frecuencia de compra internacional sigue siendo principalmente ocasional (64%), reforzando su rol como canal para necesidades puntuales más que para compras habituales (10%). “Estos resultados muestran que el comercio electrónico argentino sigue creciendo y, sobre todo, consolidándose como parte de los hábitos cotidianos de consumo”, declaró Andrés Zaied, presidente de la Cámara Argentina de Comercio Electrónico. Y sumó: “El crecimiento de las órdenes y de las unidades vendidas refleja un canal cada vez más presente en la vida de los argentinos”. “Para las empresas, el desafío ya no es decidir si estar o no en el mundo digital, sino cómo evolucionar para ofrecer mejores experiencias, más simples, confiables y relevantes para un consumidor que cambia permanentemente”, concluyó Zaied.

From specialty ingredients to prepared foods sold alongside beverages, producers across the CPG spectrum are taking a closer look at what vacuum packaging equipment can do for product consistency, spoilage reduction, and operational efficiency. Across the food and beverage supply chain, a familiar challenge has taken on new urgency: keeping products fresh, shelf-stable, and protected without adding artificial preservatives or compromising presentation. For a growing number of producers and co-packers, vacuum packaging has become a practical answer to that question one that applies well beyond the meat counter. The technology, long established in commercial food processing, is increasingly relevant to beverage-adjacent categories: specialty ingredients, cold-pressed juice components, ready-to-eat accompaniments, functional food add-ons, and premium perishables sold in the same retail channels as beverages. As product quality expectations rise and operational margins tighten, interest in vacuum packaging equipment has broadened considerably. At its core, vacuum packaging removes oxygen from a package before it is sealed creating a low-oxygen environment that inhibits the growth of aerobic bacteria and slows oxidation. The result is a meaningful extension of usable shelf life without the need for chemical additives. Chamber vacuum sealers, one of the most widely used formats in commercial operations, work by placing the entire product bag and all inside a sealed chamber. The air is evacuated uniformly before the bag is heat-sealed, producing a consistent, airtight result that is difficult to achieve with open-system alternatives. This method is particularly well-suited to liquid-rich foods, marinated proteins, and delicate items where uneven pressure can compromise packaging integrity all categories with direct relevance to beverage producers handling wet ingredients, infusions, or liquid-based preparations. Chamber vacuum sealers have become a preferred choice for operations that need consistency, speed, and reliability at scale. Unlike simpler sealing devices, they are built to handle liquids, irregular shapes, and high production volumes without sacrificing seal quality. The use cases for vacuum packaging within food and beverage-related operations are more varied than they might initially appear. Producers of specialty and premium ingredients herbs, botanicals, coffee, tea blends, spice mixes, and flavoring compounds increasingly rely on vacuum packaging to protect product integrity during storage and transit. The packaging solution preserves the quality, flavor, and texture of food products for an extended period, giving manufacturers a competitive edge by offering longer-lasting and fresher products to consumers. For prepared foods sold alongside beverages including grab-and-go proteins, deli items, and ready-to-eat snacks vacuum packaging supports extended display windows and reduces the spoilage losses that can erode margin in high-turnover environments. Widespread adoption of packaging solutions ensures that perishable food items can withstand longer transportation times without compromising quality, which is beneficial for exporting and importing food products across international borders. Small-batch and artisan producers are also finding chamber vacuum technology accessible. The commercial vacuum sealer market has seen significant advancements in improved performance, enhanced durability, and increased user-friendliness, including sealers with advanced sealing technologies, improved chamber sizes, and intuitive control panels. That evolution has brought equipment that was once primarily associated with large-scale industrial facilities within reach of smaller operations. For commercial operations, the practical benefits are straightforward: chamber systems reduce the variability that can lead to seal failures, spoilage, or product waste issues that carry real cost implications at any production volume. Consistent sealing also supports longer shelf life, more predictable inventory cycles, and cleaner presentation in retail or food service environments. As the range of applications has expanded, so has the equipment landscape. Manufacturers are developing vacuum packaging systems aimed at different production scales and product types, with attention to factors like hygienic design, ease of cleaning, and compatibility with various packaging materials. The company's equipment is positioned for operations where packaging consistency and durability are ongoing priorities, from food producers handling perishables to facilities managing high-volume sealing cycles across multiple shifts. Introvac Corp, based in Ontario, California, is among the equipment suppliers that have expanded their lineup to address food and beverage manufacturing specifically. Introvac Corp has developed fully automated chamber vacuum systems tailored for the food and beverage manufacturing industry, with a focus on high-performance engineering, durability, and hygienic design. The company offers equipment ranging from compact tabletop units suited to small-batch production or facility testing environments to double-chamber industrial sealers designed for continuous workflow and high-volume packaging lines. In December 2025, Promarksvac Corp and Introvac Corp announced a partnership aimed at advancing vacuum packaging solutions for the perishable food industry, with a focus on improving product freshness, extending shelf life, and enhancing operational efficiency across categories including produce, dairy, and ready-to-eat foods. Both companies represent a segment of the equipment market focused on serving food and beverage producers that require reliable, scalable sealing solutions with a particular emphasis on performance in wet, liquid-rich, or high-throughput packaging environments. Sustainability and Efficiency Are Shaping Purchasing Decisions Operational efficiency and sustainability considerations are increasingly factored into equipment procurement decisions across the food and beverage industry. Ongoing innovations in recyclable materials, high-performance films, and automation technologies are strengthening the industry's outlook. As businesses continue to prioritize freshness, efficiency, and product protection, vacuum packaging will remain a vital component of modern packaging and preservation strategies. Equipment that supports compatibility with recyclable films and lower-plastic barrier materials is a growing consideration for producers working toward packaging sustainability goals. At the same time, operational consistency the ability to produce reliable seals across a full production day without variability remains a primary driver of equipment selection. The food and beverage world keeps adjusting to new rules and concerns about sustainability, and packaging machines are getting smarter in response. Semi-automatic and fully automated systems are increasingly designed to offer programmable controls, better sanitation access, and more efficient sealing cycles reducing both material waste and downtime. For food and beverage companies evaluating their packaging operations, the practical question is whether current sealing methods are delivering the consistency, shelf life, and presentation quality that retail, foodservice, or export channels demand. Chamber vacuum technology offers a relatively well-understood solution one that has proven effective across a broad range of product categories and production volumes. The decision to invest in vacuum packaging equipment typically involves weighing throughput requirements against available floor space and budget, along with considerations around the types of products being sealed, the packaging materials in use, and the service and support available from the equipment supplier. As product protection requirements continue to evolve across the food and beverage industry, vacuum packaging is likely to remain a practical and widely applicable tool one that producers at various scales are integrating into their standard operations rather than treating as a specialty solution. La cadena de suministro global de bebidas mostró estabilidad aparente en Q3 2026 según Agrowgate, pero persisten presiones inflacionarias en commodities que impactaron a actores como Juice Burst Drinks Co con caídas de margen del 72%. Heineken lanzó un programa de agricultura regenerativa para su cadena de cebada malteada en UK, y la industria del aluminio en Brasil reportó resiliencia con producción récord, señal positiva para el envasado de bebidas en la región.

Ventas / Comercial

En Argentina, los supermercados aceleran la reconversión hacia formatos más chicos y menor costo operativo ante la caída del consumo y la mayor frecuencia de compra con menor ticket. En México, Walmart reportó crecimiento de ticket promedio pero caída en número de transacciones, mientras las ventas de ANTAD avanzaron apenas 1%, reflejando un consumidor masivo bajo presión en toda la región.
