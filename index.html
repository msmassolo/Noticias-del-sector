Why Fizz Is Falling Flat with Some Wellness-Minded Drinkers

El mercado de bebidas carbonatadas enfrenta un desplazamiento significativo hacia productos sin gas impulsado por consumidores más jóvenes orientados al bienestar. En 2025, los lanzamientos sin carbonatación representan el 38% de nuevos productos en el mercado estadounidense de bebidas premezcladas de $22 mil millones (versus 27% en 2021), marcando una tendencia estructural. Marcas consolidadas como Svedka y nuevos competidores están reposicionándose hacia opciones fizz-free, lo que redefine la estrategia de portafolio de la industria. Esta migración hacia bebidas wellness obliga a productores a repensar su mix de productos y segmentación de mercado.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
Carbonated drinks like seltzers and sparkling water have been popular for many years; however, modern – and younger – drinkers appear to be more interested in how the beverage will make them feel than how bubbly it is. In 2025, fizz-free beverages accounted for 38% of newly launched products in the $22 billion U.S. premixed drinks market, up from 27% in 2021, according to IWSR data cited by Bloomberg . Brands ranging from Surfside and Sun Cruiser to Svedka Vodka Water and Lucky One Lemonade have been focusing on noncarbonated formats, while hard seltzers have lost some ground to other ready-to-drink options. Part of what’s driven this move away from bubbles is heightening consumer scrutiny of beverages through the lens of health and wellness. And it goes beyond calories, sugar, and clean ingredients, too, as these consumers are also paying more attention to how they physically feel after drinking these beverages – a mindset shift that I suspect has largely stemmed from the sober curiosity movement and its emphasis on mindful awareness of such experiences. In terms of carbonation, high consumption of carbonated drinks can lead to bloating, belching, or abdominal pressure for some. However, Vikas Arora, a qualified pharmacist and director of British Pharmacare who specializes in upper gastrointestinal conditions, does not believe that carbonation is the only culprit. “Usually it’s not the carbonated aspect that requires limits. It’s other common ingredients like sugar and caffeine,” Arora told FI . “So, the issue isn’t that these drinks are fizzy, it’s the other ingredients which are common in them such as caffeine, sugar, and artificial sweeteners.” The research on carbonation and its impact on reflux is also quite nuanced. Though a systematic review examining carbonated beverages and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) found that carbonation can temporarily lower pressure in the lower esophageal sphincter, the researchers found no direct evidence showing that carbonated beverages consistently cause or worsen GERD. Meanwhile, a later systematic review did happen to find an association between carbonated beverage consumption and GERD risk, demonstrating how individual responses and other dietary factors can muddy the waters. While many experts say that a still beverage is not inherently healthier than a carbonated one, just as plain sparkling water and a sugary soft drink are not nutritionally equivalent, everybody is different – and consumers who experience discomfort can benefit from taking note of what triggers it. “Persistent bloating, frequent belching, abdominal pressure or worsening heartburn. These are all reasons to reduce intake immediately and check if symptoms improve,” Arora said. About 20% of people in the U.S. have GERD, according to the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, and the agency recommends that people with the condition pay attention to foods and drinks that appear to worsen their individual symptoms. “However, if reflux occurs frequently and is waking someone at night, this should not solely be blamed on carbonated drinks and must be followed by a medical assessment,” Arora told FI .

Why cask ale is targeting younger drinkers to revive sales in UK pubs

Las cervecerías británicas están reorientando estrategias de marketing hacia consumidores más jóvenes para revitalizar las ventas de cask ale (cerveza de barril), categoría que enfrenta presión por cambios demográficos y reducción de bares tradicionales. El cask ale, un producto vivo sin pasteurizar que continúa fermentando después de dejar la cervecería, requiere expertise en elaboración y servicio. Esta reconfiguración de target demográfico refleja la necesidad de la industria cervecera de adaptarse a nuevos hábitos de consumo para sostener categorías tradicionales en mercados maduros.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
But as changing consumer habits, demographics and pub numbers have declined, it has put pressure on the category and brewers are increasingly looking to younger drinkers to help secure its future. Cask beer, often referred to as real ale, is unpasteurised or filtered meaning it is a live product. The beer retains live yeast that continues to mature and develop the flavour after leaving the brewery, with fermentation continuing in the cask. Brewing and serving cask beer requires skill at every stage, from the brewery through to cellar management to the pouring the perfect pint. It is also available in a wide range of styles, from pale ales, bitters and milds to rich stouts. However, like many other alcoholic drink categories it has faced challenges. According to the latest NIQ on-trade OPM data, <a href="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/09/16/cask-beer-sales-decline-as-prices-rise-and-distribution-struggles/" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/09/16/cask-beer-sales-decline-as-prices-rise-and-distribution-struggles/">published in the Morning Advertiser</a>, total cask volumes dropped to 979,000 hectolitres over the 12 months to August 2026, down 19.7% year on year and 10.4% versus two years ago. That compares with a 3.9% year-on-year decline and 1.7% fall in two years for total draught beer. But it is not all bad news as value sales have been more resilent, with cask worth £805m, down 7.4% year-on-year and 3.1% against two years ago, because of strong price growth. Research by the Society of Independent Brewers & Associates also highlighted the challenges facing the sector, describing the current position for as “not a pretty picture.” The organisation found that only 27% of beer drinkers ever drink cask – down from 35% in its previous report. However, the report also found opportunities for growth. It found that 41% of consumers said a sample of cask ale would encourage them to try it; while 28% said they would try cask if they knew more about it and interestingly 26% said they would try it on the recommendation of bar staff. There is now a renewed focus in the UK as brewers look to the younger customer to revamp sales in the category. You only have to look at the the massive surge in the popularity of Guinness, which was transformed it from an “old man’s drink” to a choice of Gen Z and Young adults driven trendy social media trends. Consumer research by Racasse Audience Intelligence showed that one-third, 34%, of cask drinkers are aged between 18 to 39, with 17% of cask drinkers being under 30. This comes as pubs in the UK celebrate Cask Ale Week, which runs from 17-27 September 2026. The initiative aims to encourage trial and awareness of Britain’s national beer style, with 10,000 participating pubs expected to offer around one million free “Try Before You Buy” samples. The aim is to encourage younger drinkers in particular to discover cask and hopefully convert them into regular cask ale drinkers. Research conducted in the lead up to Cask Ale Week found that younger beer drinkers are increasingly ordering the cask ales they remember their parents and grandparents drinking at the pub. Gen Z and younger millennials, broadly those aged 18-to-35, are as likely to enjoy a pint of Old Peculier as they are a trendy craft beer. Bass owner Budweiser Brewing Group (BBG) worked with hospitality industry insight company The Oxford Partnership to track the changing age demographics of Bass drinkers. Warren White, Bass brand manager, said: “Bass has always been a cask ale with broad appeal to beer drinkers. What this research shows is that since 2024/25 the age mix in pubs serving Bass has changed, with more under‑35s, and particularly more in the 18-to-24 and 25-to-29 age groups, drinking in Bass pubs.” Timothy Taylor’s Landlord, famously named by pop-star Madonna as her favourite ale, is the UK’s best-selling cask ale brand by both value and volume is also seeing interest among younger drinkers. Andy Leman, head brewer at the Yorkshire-based independent family brewery, said: “We know that there’s plenty of interest amongst 18 to 24 year-olds in trying cask beer brands that are familiar fixtures on the bar, but which they may want to know more about flavour-wise. Giving these so-called cask-curious, and price-conscious, consumers a chance to try-before-they-buy could help increase the reach to a new generation of cask converts.” Meanwhile, Adam Russell, commercial director of Wiltshire-based brewer Wadworth, whose 6X ale is also seeing strong growth, said there is a trend amongst younger drinkers looking for cask ales with provenance and heritage. “They know Wadworth 6X as a brand their dad - or maybe even their granddad – drank, and they want to revive and continue that family tradition,” he said. Phillip Montgomery, business unit director, CGA by NIQ, which measures drinks sales in pubs, said that for the past five years, cask ale is a beer style that was being left behind, especially by younger drinkers. “However, in the same way that stout has seen a revival in fortunes, we’re also starting to see an upturn in interest in cask ale amongst younger drinkers, and it’s often traditional brands and well-established regional favourites that bring them into the cask category,” he said. The figures also show that cask ale retains a strong regional following, with Timothy Taylor Landord the top seller in Scotland and the North, Greene King IPA in East Anglia, and Fuller’s London Pride in London and the South East.

Danone expands specialist dairy ingredient capabilities at Steenvoorde site | FoodBev Media

Danone invirtió €70 millones en una nueva línea de producción de nutrición médica en su planta de Steenvoorde (Francia), posicionándola como hub europeo para nutrición especializada. La expansión fortalece la capacidad de la compañía en segmentos de alto valor agregado (nutrición médica, fórmulas infantiles y nutrición especializada), mercados estratégicos con crecimiento impulsado por envejecimiento poblacional y demanda de productos premium. Esta consolidación operativa refuerza la posición competitiva de Danone en categorías de mayor rentabilidad y diferenciación versus bebidas convencionales.

Danone has inaugurated a new medical nutrition production line at its Steenvoorde site in northern France, as part of a €70 million investment designed to expand the facility’s capabilities in specialised nutrition. The investment positions Steenvoorde as a European hub combining medical nutrition, infant formula and speciality dairy ingredient production, with the site using advanced filtration technologies to manufacture ingredients for specialised nutrition applications. The new production line, which was announced in 2024, is Danone’s largest medical nutrition production line in France. It will have the capacity to produce nearly 20 million litres of oral nutritional supplements annually, including almost 150 Fortimel product references. Alongside finished specialised nutrition products, Steenvoorde has developed expertise in producing dairy ingredients through advanced filtration technologies. Danone said the site has a “unique industrial expertise” in producing ingredients used in specialised nutrition products, with many of these ingredients also manufactured at Steenvoorde. The combination of ingredient production and finished-product manufacturing gives the facility a broader role within Danone’s specialised nutrition network, linking dairy processing technologies with applications designed for specific nutritional requirements. The site brings together three strategic manufacturing activities: infant formula, medical nutrition and specialty dairy ingredients. Its capabilities are intended to support the development of specialised nutrition products across different stages of life. The expansion will also see Steenvoorde produce part of Danone’s Fortimel range of oral nutritional supplements. The range includes different formats, textures and recipes, including plant-based alternatives, to address varying nutritional needs and consumer preferences. Danone’s investment comes as the company seeks to expand its medical nutrition activities against a backdrop of population ageing and increasing chronic disease. According to the company, the number of people aged over 60 is expected to double by 2050, while disease-related malnutrition currently affects between 30% and 50% of hospitalised patients, citing WHO Europe. Danone said medical nutrition is a key pillar of its Renew Danone strategy, with the group aiming to strengthen production capabilities and scientific expertise as nutritional requirements evolve. Danone described the new medical nutrition line as the largest medical nutrition production line in France in terms of both production capacity and size. The €70 million project also included the installation of a biomass boiler, inaugurated in February 2026, which Danone said is helping to reduce the carbon footprint of the Steenvoorde site. The investment is part of a wider programme of more than €600 million that Danone plans to invest across its French sites between 2021 and 2027. Steenvoorde is located in the Hauts-de-France region, an established dairy-producing area in France. Danone said the site works with more than 400 partner farms, while regional milk collection has increased sixfold over the past four years. Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 8 días · Actualizado 2026-09-11

La semana estuvo dominada por una oleada de inversiones estratégicas de los grandes jugadores globales en infraestructura productiva y logística en América Latina, con Coca-Cola y PepsiCo liderando el despliegue de capital en la región. A nivel financiero, los resultados trimestrales mixtos de National Beverage, Brown-Forman y Monster Beverage marcaron la agenda bursátil, con señales de presión en volúmenes en categorías maduras como cerveza y spirits. Las tendencias de consumo siguieron orientadas hacia bebidas funcionales, sin alcohol y RTD premium, con movimientos de M&A alineados a esa dirección. En Argentina, el foco estuvo en la crisis de la cerveza artesanal, la caída de exportaciones vitivinícolas y la histórica inversión de Grupo Peñaflor.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Coca-Cola comprometió USD 770M en Chile hasta 2030 e inauguró una nueva planta en México tras 30 años de ausencia, marcando su mayor ciclo de inversión en Latinoamérica en décadas.
  • PepsiCo inauguró un mega centro logístico de 24.000 m² en el Callao, Perú, con inversión de USD 59M, posicionándolo como hub de distribución regional.
  • Grupo Peñaflor anunció la mayor inversión en una nueva bodega argentina en 30 años, hito estratégico para la industria vitivinícola local en un contexto de caída de exportaciones.
  • National Beverage Corp. reportó caída del 16% en utilidad neta del Q1 FY2027 e ingresos por debajo de estimaciones, reflejando la presión que enfrenta el segmento de bebidas carbonatadas premium en EE.UU.
  • Danone adquirió Huel, líder en nutrición funcional, consolidando la convergencia entre bebidas y nutrición de alto desempeño como el movimiento de M&A más significativo de la semana a nivel global.
Finanzas

National Beverage Corp. reportó caída del 16% en ganancia neta del Q1 FY2027 alcanzando USD 47M, incumpliendo estimaciones de mercado en ingresos por aproximadamente USD 11M; en paralelo, Brown-Forman y Molson Coors mantuvieron cotizaciones estables tras resultados que cumplieron o moderadamente decepcionaron expectativas. Monster Beverage registró un Q2 2026 récord pero su acción retrocedió tras anunciar un split, mientras Campari y Constellation Brands también enfrentaron presión bajista en sus papeles durante la semana.

Marketing

PepsiCo lidera la tendencia de colaboraciones cross-category con marcas fuera del sector bebidas, ejemplificada por Pepsi y Mountain Dew en alianzas con marcas de cuidado personal, en una estrategia de 'odd-couple marketing' que gana terreno en EE.UU. Poppi, la marca viral de bebidas funcionales adquirida por PepsiCo, avanza en su expansión global con lanzamiento en el Reino Unido, consolidando la apuesta de la compañía por el segmento funcional como vector de crecimiento y relevancia cultural.

Supply Chain

Heineken UK, Molson Coors y Diageo aceleraron sus programas de descarbonización en planta mediante bombas de calor industriales, reduciendo consumos de gas entre 45% y 50%; simultáneamente, Diageo inauguró su planta de USD 415M en Alabama para fortalecer la cadena de suministro del sur de EE.UU. En el frente regulatorio, México proyecta recaudar 264 mil millones de pesos vía IEPS sobre bebidas azucaradas y alcohólicas en 2027, mientras los trabajadores de la destilería Cameronbridge de Diageo en Escocia votaron a favor de huelga, abriendo un riesgo operacional relevante.

Ventas / Comercial

El canal retail en EE.UU. sigue bajo presión para la cerveza convencional, con caídas del 4,5% en valor y superiores en volumen, mientras las bebidas sin alcohol aceleraron un 4,9% interanual en el mismo período, confirmando el trasvase de consumo. En distribución, la expansión del centro logístico de PepsiCo en Callao (USD 59M) y la ampliación del acuerdo de embotellamiento Carlsberg-PepsiCo en nórdicos y bálticos refuerzan la apuesta por eficiencia en la última milla; en sentido opuesto, un fabricante de bebidas en EE.UU. presentó Chapter 11 tras perder su acuerdo de distribución con Costco.
