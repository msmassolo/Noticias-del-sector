Briefing para Finanzas

El sector de bebidas enfrenta presión de márgenes a nivel global, con el crecimiento de exportaciones desacelerándose según reportes internacionales. Briefing para Marketing

El mercado interno de vino cayó en julio, consolidando una tendencia bajista en el semestre, mientras que la cerveza artesanal enfrenta simultáneamente caída de consumo y cambio de hábitos, señales de recomposición del consumidor local. A nivel global, las bebidas funcionales avanzan hacia propuestas más específicas y creativas —incluyendo infusiones ayurvédicas y salud intestinal—, una tendencia a monitorear para anticipar demanda en segmentos premium y sin alcohol. Red Bull suma un nuevo sabor permanente (Fuji Apple & Ginger) y Bacardí refuerza el posicionamiento de legacy del ron, indicando que las grandes marcas apuestan a innovación incremental y storytelling de origen.

Briefing para Supply Chain

Los sistemas de edulcorantes están evolucionando activamente para adaptarse a preferencias del consumidor, lo que implica potencial revisión de formulaciones y contratos de materias primas en el corto plazo. El packaging compostable enfrenta barreras de adopción que no son de material sino de casos de uso, lo que exige reevaluar estrategias de sustentabilidad antes de comprometer inversión. Las exportaciones de vino a granel crecen 46,5% en volumen, con foco en Malbec y sustentabilidad, generando presión sobre disponibilidad de granel y logística de exportación en origen.

Briefing para Ventas / Comercial

PepsiCo capacitará a más de 4.500 tenderos en 2026 en la región, reforzando la ejecución en canal tradicional como palanca de crecimiento comercial —movimiento relevante para benchmarking en trade marketing. El modelo de tiendas de descuento duro (Tiendas 3B) gana tracción y es replicado por grandes cadenas como Walmart y Soriana, evidenciando una reconfiguración del canal moderno hacia propuestas de precio agresivo que impacta la estrategia de precio-canal. Starbucks migra a pantallas digitales para impulsar ventas en el horario tarde, una señal de optimización de punto de venta que puede inspirar acciones en canales on-premise y foodservice local.

Raising the functional beverage bar: Drinks are becoming more specific and creative

El mercado de bebidas funcionales evoluciona desde claims simples de hidratación hacia propuestas más sofisticadas enfocadas en beneficios específicos para cuerpo, cerebro y estado de ánimo. Los consumidores rechazan calorías vacías y exigen que cada bebida aporte valor funcional, convirtiendo estos atributos en requisito básico competitivo. Esta tendencia obliga a reformulaciones y posicionamientos diferenciados en el portafolio de marcas, abriendo oportunidades en segmentos de mayor margen pero demandando inversión en I+D e ingredientes especializados.

Texto completo del artículo
From simple “functional” and “hydration” claims to more complicated body-, brain- and mood-boosting callouts, added-benefits have become table stakes for beverages as consumers increasingly eschew empty calories and look to make every sip count. According to new <a href="https://www.spate.nyc/reports/functional-beverage-trends-2026-trends-ingredients-white-space?utm_medium=email&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-9bwG85u6rP_AoyC6BXqFC8IhqXJuxVlL3xPQjxN6rsxnzTlo_qHl6YxySwtCf7qlUKx9IuL3LsmvA5sUQNb1pVq8qmx_mPh8iZZ3qs_CzaCE2PZ8Y&_hsmi=430471429&utm_content=430471429&utm_source=hs_email" target="_blank" rel="">research</a> from the trend tracking and market research firm Spate, the popularity of the term “functional beverage” across Google, TikTok and Instagram grew a staggering 491.8% year-over-year, qualifying the trend as having “medium popularity.” More important, though, is what the trend signals. According to Spate, the uptick in use of the phrase shows consumer adoption of an industry term at scale and underscores an important shift in how they evaluate beverages. Specifically, Spate says it emphasizes how “shoppers increasingly think in terms of what a drink does, rather than what it is.” But as shoppers become more sophisticated and selective about their drink choices, the added benefits, ingredients and formats they want are shifting – as is the marketing, branding and packaging that best resonates given beverages’ double-duty as status symbols for many shoppers. In this episode of <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Multimedia/Podcasts/" target="_blank" rel="">FoodNavigator-USA’s Soup-To-Nuts podcast</a>, the founders of four emerging brands share where they see challenges and opportunities within the functional beverage category, including where there is white space for innovation, market saturation, how they are cutting through the competition and explaining to consumers the benefits their products proffer. This includes a deep dive into the potential for protein in drinks with insights from the entrepreneur behind the sparkling bone broth beverage marrō<b> </b>and the nutritionist who created H2Pro+ Protein Water. The founder of BrainFood shares insights about the emerging demand for cognitive support and the power of her sparkling nootropic brain health drink to support focus, mental clarity and stress management. And finally, an insider with the supplement drink mix company Avro Life shares how the functional category is fragmenting into occasions and needs. According to Spate, energy is one of the most popular functional benefits across Google, TikTok and Instagram with 818,500 mentions from June 2025 to May 2026, representing an increase of 18.7% year over year. Energy drinks also are the top functional beverage by popularity increase with a year-over-year spike of 40.3%, according to the report. But more energy doesn’t necessarily mean more caffeine. It might mean more of the vitamins and minerals needed to boost focus, brain wellness and stress balance, which when not adequately supported can feel like burnout, exhaustion and brain fog. Megan Burton, the CEO and co-founder of the sparkling water startup BrainFood, saw this distinction as an opportunity to break free from competitors in the traditional energy drink category and offer consumers something more than stimulation from caffeine, which can cause jitters and result in a subsequent crash. “BrainFood is a lightly sparkling water with nutrients to support brain wellness. It is infused with vitamins and minerals to support things like focus, stress balance and overall brain health,” said Burton, who has a background in biomedicine and whose co-founder is a psychiatrist. The drinks include B12, zinc and vitamin D along with adaptogens, antioxidants and amino acids to support the body and brain. Notably missing from that list is caffeine. And Burton says that is on purpose “Everything is caffeinated at this point,” said Burton, who admits she loves a “good French press black coffee,” but added caffeine too late in the day can negatively effect REM and deep sleep, potentially making people more tired the next day. Burton says her decision to skip the caffeine in BrainFood also follows a subtle, but important shift in how consumers are thinking about what ‘energy’ means. Burton explains consumers are breaking the concept down to understand if by energy they mean being able to focus on a task or run a marathon. They are then tailoring their solutions based on their needs. The consumer shift towards specific outcomes rather than a generic promise of energy or wellness also is showing up in one of the biggest functional-beverage trends of all – protein. According to Spate, the popularity of “protein packed” as a functional beverage benefit is up 1,700% year over year, and the popularity of “protein water” is up a whopping 155.8% year over year. Among the many protein waters hitting the market to meet this demand is H2Pro+ Protein Water, which co-founder and certified nutritionist, fitness coach and wellness creator Sloane Davis says stands out because it actually tastes like water and offers more than protein. She explains the beverage has 15 grams of protein plus 4 grams of fiber and electrolytes in each bottle. “You’re already drinking water. Why not make it work smarter for you?” she said. “The protein satiates you, and the electrolytes are going to keep you hydrated.” H2Pro+ also is designed to eliminate some of the friction associated with other protein beverages – so, no small scoop lost in a tub of powder, no shaker and no chalky texture. Consumers are increasingly interested in how that function fits into a particular moment in their day – which is opening the door for beverages that do less, but better. This is the premise behind Avro Life’s line of functional supplement drink mixes made with PharmaGABA, according to Marianne Lyles, who is the growth and marketing director for Avro Life Co. “The category that we are building is calm performance. So, we have a lot of energy and caffeine culture, which can lead to spikes and crashes, and we are building a platform that says you can also start from a calm, balanced place and perform really well from there,” she said. She explained that PharmaGAMA is the foundation for the company’s entire line, which includes options with added magnesium in the ‘calm’ blend and the ‘focus’ blend features a dose of Cognigrape, which is a standardized red grape polyphenol extract that has been studied for improving attention, memory and mental clarity. That kind of benefit specificity that Avro and others offer raises the bar for brands. As the category gets more crowded, consumers have more choices and opportunities to decide whether a product actually delivers on its promise. “People are looking for a product that actually delivers on the functional claim,” Lyles said. For example, can they feel the effect? She adds Avro has science to back up its claims and a variety of flavors so it can deliver on two fronts. The tension between increasingly sophisticated functional claims and the very basic need to actually enjoy drinking the product is the foundation for marrō – a bone broth unlike any other, according to founder Nick Foreste. “We are the first ready-to-drink bone broth designed to be enjoyed cold and out of the can. So we are lightly carbonated, and the kicker is we’re fruit flavored. So it’s so wild. It’s a very, very different take on traditional broth, but we’re 98% bone broth,” he said. He explained that that idea for marrō came to him while he was training as an ultra runner. Traditional <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/02/bone-broth-breaks-out-of-the-soup-bowl/" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/02/bone-broth-breaks-out-of-the-soup-bowl/">bone broth</a> offered benefits his body needed to recover, such as collagen, but he didn’t want to drink it hot when he was sweating after or during a run, and cold soup was “disgusting.” His solution: Swapping fruit flavors for bone broth’s more traditional savory notes, adding a hint of carbonation and chilling it for a refreshing beverages that quenches thirst and delivers benefits. Marrō is taking something consumers already associate with nutrition and wellness – bone broth – and completely rethinking what the drinking experience can look like. That requires retailers to rethink where the beverage should be. Rather than in the soup section, Foreste says the beverages is best placed next to the kombuchas, cold pressed juices and turmeric shots. Once a product can be defined by the benefit it delivers, rather than by a key ingredient or traditional beverage segment, the boundaries around that category start to get pretty blurry. Taken together, these four brands illustrate just how broad the definition of a functional beverage has become. Brain Food is focused on brain wellness, focus and stress. H2 Pro+ is essentially turning water into a vehicle for protein, fiber and electrolytes. Avro is building around different mental and physical states — calm, focus and energy. And Marro is taking the nutritional proposition of bone broth and putting it into a cold, carbonated, fruit-flavored format. They’re very different products. But they share one important characteristic: They’re all starting with a consumer need or desired benefit and building the beverage around it. And as consumers become more familiar with the language of functional beverages, the brands that win will be the ones that most clearly answer communicate the benefits – and give consumers a reason to come back for another sip.

Problemas comunes incluyen ablandamiento de bases en contenedores calientes, deformación de tapas y fugas en bebidas frías, que generan rechazo del consumidor y erosionan la credibilidad de iniciativas de sustentabilidad. La industria necesita alineación entre especificaciones de materiales compostables y requerimientos funcionales de cada producto para evitar fracasos estratégicos en la transición hacia packaging sustentable.

A brand switches its hot-food container to a compostable version, and within weeks the complaints arrive: the base softens under a saucy dish, a lid warps, a chilled drink cup sweats and leaks. The usual conclusion is that 'compostable doesn't work.' Nearly always, the material was capable. It was simply matched to the wrong job. That matters more in 2026 than it did even two years ago, because the switch is no longer optional. The EU's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40) came into force on 12 August 2026; in the United States, more than a dozen states have enacted restrictions on PFAS 'forever chemicals' in food packaging, with Maine's ban on nine categories of plant fibre food-contact packaging taking effect in May 2026. For food and beverage brands, compostable and PFAS-free packaging has moved from a marketing choice to a documented compliance requirement. Getting the specification right the first time is now a commercial issue as well as an environmental one. The first misconception to drop is that 'compostable' describes a single material. It is a category that includes moulded sugarcane fibre (bagasse), bamboo and wheat-straw pulp, PLA and CPLA and various coated and uncoated papers, each behaving very differently under heat, moisture, grease and time. Specifying 'the compostable option' without matching the format to the food is the root of most disappointing switches. From the production side, we see brands run into the same three mismatches again and again. Each maps to a question worth asking before a switch, not after. The variables that decide performance are temperature, moisture, fat content and dwell time – for example, how long the food sits in the pack before it is eaten. A dry pastry and a hot biryani make entirely different demands. An uncoated fibre bowl that is perfect for a sandwich can fail under a curry left for twenty minutes; chilled and frozen lines add condensation; microwave reheating adds another stress. The useful question is never 'is this compostable?' but 'will this format hold this food, at this temperature, for this long?' A compostable product only delivers its benefit where the infrastructure to process it exists, and that infrastructure is uneven. BioCycle's national survey found composting facilities accepting compostable packaging rising from 58% in 2018 to 71% more recently – real progress, but far from universal – while 45 of the 145 facilities surveyed run a zero-tolerance policy for contamination. Industrial-compostable is also not the same as home-compostable. Ship an industrially compostable pack into a region without industrial composting and it behaves like ordinary waste, and the environmental claim quietly collapses. This is where brands carry the most reputational and regulatory risk, and where the data is sobering. When organisations Consumer Reports and The Counter, tested moulded fibre foodware, they found fluorine, an indicator of PFAS, in effectively every moulded fibre bowl tested, at levels several times higher than in many other packaging types. A product can be genuinely compostable and still carry a fluorochemical coating that makes it neither safe nor truly compostable. 'Compostable' and 'PFAS-free' are separate properties that must be separately verified: the actual certificate and its scope (not a supplier's logo), a total organic fluorine or PFAS test report, and evidence that the end-of-life claim holds in the destination market. None of this makes compostable packaging hard. It makes it a packaging engineering decision rather than a marketing swap. The brands that switch successfully put compostable options through the same rigour they apply to any spec: grease and temperature resistance, service life, transport and storage and documentation for every environmental claim. The ones that struggle tend to have ordered 'the compostable version' and expected it to behave exactly like the plastic it replaced. The encouraging part is that the material science has largely caught up. For most foodservice applications, there is now a compostable format that genuinely performs, and PFAS-free fibre, driven partly by the very testing and bans above, is increasingly the norm rather than the exception. The failures still circulating are rarely material failures. They are specification failures: a pack chosen for its label instead of its fit. As compostable packaging shifts from gesture to default, successful brands will stop asking 'is it compostable?' and start asking 'is it the right compostable for this product, this market and this claim I can prove?' Get that right, and the switch works the first time. Get it wrong, and the material takes the blame for a decision made before it ever entered the picture. El mercado interno de vino argentino registró 649.674 hectolitros en julio de 2026, con caída del 1,4% interanual según el INV. En el acumulado del semestre se observa recuperación marginal con empate técnico respecto a 2025. La comercialización de vino en el mercado interno alcanzó en julio de 2026 un volumen de 649.674 hectolitros (hl), lo que representó una caída del 1,4% respecto de igual mes del año pasado, según el informe difundido por el Instituto Nacional de Vitivinicultura (INV). Sin embargo, el reporte acumulado mostró una mínima recuperación que mostró empate técnico con relación al 2025. La mayor parte de las ventas correspondió a vinos sin mención varietal, que concentraron el 66,8% del volumen comercializado. Los vinos varietales representaron el 28,9%, mientras que los espumosos alcanzaron el 3,8% y otros vinos —entre ellos cóctel de vino, gasificados, especiales y rituales— explicaron el 0,5%. El comportamiento de las distintas categorías fue dispar. En julio, los despachos de vinos sin mención varietal retrocedieron 1,1%, mientras que los varietales registraron una caída más pronunciada, del 4,5%. En contrapartida, los vinos espumosos mostraron un fuerte crecimiento del 20,3%, mientras que la categoría de otros vinos avanzó 7,5%. Por color, los vinos blancos, que representaron el 24,1% del volumen total comercializado, registraron un incremento marginal del 0,1%. Los vinos de color, que concentran el 75,9% restante, tuvieron una disminución del 1,8%. Botella, lata y bag in box Los datos del INV también mostraron cambios en los formatos utilizados para la comercialización del vino. En la comparación interanual de julio, los despachos en botella aumentaron 3,6%, mientras que el bag in box tuvo un salto de 372,7%. También crecieron las ventas en lata, con una suba del 7,4%. En cambio, los envases tradicionales registraron retrocesos. Los despachos en tetra brik disminuyeron 9,9%, mientras que la damajuana cayó 28,7% y otros tipos de envases tuvieron una baja del 14,9%. La evolución de los formatos reflejó una mayor dinámica de las presentaciones alternativas, especialmente el bag in box y la lata, frente a la pérdida de participación de algunos envases tradicionales. Estos, no obstante, no tienen todavía un volumen que haga mover la aguja en las ventas totales. Empate técnico A pesar de la caída registrada en julio, el balance de los primeros siete meses de 2026 tuvo una evolución positiva. Entre enero y julio se comercializaron 4.093.144 hectolitros de vino en el mercado interno, un 0,6% más que en igual período de 2025. En este acumulado, los vinos sin mención varietal representan el 70,8% del total, mientras que los varietales concentran el 25,9%. Los espumosos participan con el 2,9% y otros vinos con el 0,4%. El comportamiento por categorías también presentó diferencias importantes. En los primeros siete meses del año, los despachos de vinos sin mención varietal crecieron 11,4%, mientras que los espumosos avanzaron 10,6% y la categoría de otros vinos aumentó 5,6%. El dato más negativo se encontró entre los vinos varietales, cuyos despachos acumulados cayeron 21,2% frente al mismo período del año anterior. Por color, los vinos blancos acumulan una mejora del 3,3% entre enero y julio, mientras que los vinos de color presentan una disminución del 0,3%. De esta manera, el mercado interno vitivinícola llega al segundo semestre con una evolución prácticamente estable en términos de volumen: julio marcó una contracción interanual de 1,4%, pero el acumulado anual todavía se mantiene en terreno positivo, con una suba de 0,6%. Al mismo tiempo, los datos mostraron un cambio en la composición de las ventas, con mayor crecimiento de los espumosos, los vinos sin mención varietal y formatos alternativos.

  • Heineken anunció el despido de 6.000 empleados y el cierre de producción en una planta, en la reestructuración más significativa de la semana a nivel operativo global.
Finanzas

Pernod Ricard fue el gran perdedor de la semana, reportando una caída de ventas del 14,2% en FY26 (de €10.959M a €9.404M) y un beneficio operativo recurrente 5,2% menor, con su acción cayendo en bolsa ante perspectivas cautelosas para EE.UU. y China. En el lado opuesto, Coca-Cola alcanzó máximos de 52 semanas y Monster Beverage aceleró su crecimiento con un alza de ventas netas del 20,8% en Q2 2026 excluyendo su segmento alcohólico, mientras Keurig Dr Pepper y PepsiCo reafirmaron su guidance anual sin cambios.

Marketing

PepsiCo apostó fuerte al marketing estacional lanzando productos de Halloween bajo 'drop culture' junto a Coca-Cola y Hershey, y activó a la tenista Amanda Anisimova para posicionar Pure Leaf Iced Tea en el segmento de bienestar y pausa. Monster Beverage cerró un acuerdo con el fútbol profesional mientras BuzzBallz capitalizó la nostalgia con una colaboración limitada con Pop-Tarts, reflejando la tendencia global de co-branding como palanca de relevancia cultural.

Supply Chain

Diageo enfrentó una amenaza de huelga en su destilería escocesa de Cameronbridge con ~100 trabajadores movilizados por recortes de personal, mientras Warsteiner consolidó toda su producción en una única planta tras reducir capacidad en un 30%. Constellation Brands anunció una inversión de USD 100M en agricultores estadounidenses proveedores de la industria cervecera, apostando a la resiliencia de su cadena de suministro doméstica ante la incertidumbre arancelaria.

Ventas / Comercial

En Argentina, las ventas en supermercados, mayoristas y shoppings cayeron en junio según el INDEC (-1%, -1,4% y -6,2% respectivamente), con la cerveza artesanal como segmento bajo mayor presión por cambio de hábitos y caída de consumo. A nivel regional, Walmart reportó desaceleración en same-store sales aunque sus usuarios de IA (Sparky) gastan 40% más por compra, evidenciando que la digitalización del canal masivo empieza a segmentar fuertemente el desempeño comercial.
