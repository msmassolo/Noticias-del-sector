Allison and Stephen Ellsworth, the founders behind prebiotic soda brand Poppi, launched jetty, a hydration brand built for the modern consumer and real everyday life, the company shares. Following the sale of Poppi to PepsiCo for $1.95 billion in 2025, the Ellsworths are building their next legacy brand, bringing a better-for-you electrolyte drink with five flavors in ready-to-drink bottles and powders. “Similar to Poppi, the idea behind jetty was born organically in solving a personal health issue that many people experience, but often overlook,” said Allison Ellsworth, chief brand officer and co-founder of jetty, in a statement. “Like many Americans, I was suffering from chronic dehydration. I went to the grocery store and noticed every electrolyte drink I found was made for high-performance athletes, high in sodium, and full of artificial ingredients, and there was no brand I related to. My husband, Stephen, and I saw a white space, and together, we created jetty: a product that’s made for everyday hydration.” Built on taste, culture and community first, jetty is made for the modern consumer looking for a more approachable way to wellness, the company says. The brand launches in both ready-to-drink bottles and ready-to-mix powders from day one, available online at Target and Amazon, and nationwide at Whole Foods Market. • Hydration for Everyday: jetty is designed for everybody and any occasion, delivering 100 mg of sodium, 310 mg of potassium, 100 mg of magnesium, B vitamins and prebiotics, alongside 6 grams of sugar and 45 calories in each bottle • Five hydrating flavors: The ready-to-drink bottles and ready-to-mix powder lineup includes Watermelon, Orange Mango, Fruit Punch, Black Raspberry and White Cherry • No artificial stuff: jetty is formulated without artificial colors, dyes or sweeteners. The ready-to-drink bottles are even made with real fruit juice • Low-carbon-footprint bottle: Made with co-Friendly PET packaging and designed to have a lower carbon footprint “Hydration is a $13 billion category, but consumers are leaving traditional sports drinks and looking for better-for-you ways to hydrate,” said Stephen Ellsworth, CEO and co-founder of jetty. “These brands have long been engineered for men, athletes, exercise and performance. That’s why retailers are so excited about jetty. We are finally delivering a hydration brand for everyday consumers — people navigating work, travel, social plans, parenting, late nights, early mornings and everything in between.” jetty marks the next chapter for Allison Ellsworth, the entrepreneur and brand builder who grew Poppi from an emerging beverage brand into a household name. As Chief Brand Officer, Ellsworth is bringing the playbook she created at Poppi, where she pioneered the brand’s cultural cachet and digital-first approach, creating a loyal community and more than 3 billion views on TikTok. Stephen Ellsworth will helm the company as CEO, overseeing the company’s day-to-day business operations, finance and research and development, which he led at Poppi and was responsible for creating and developing all the flavors. Huel’s RTD complete meals are now stocked in over 120,000 distribution points Huel has surpassed £100m retail sales value in the UK, driven by surging demand for its plant-based ready-to-drink meal replacements. The brand, snapped up by French food and beverage giant Danone in September for €1bn (£800m), has grown value sales of its products by 29% over the past year, to £114m, NIQ data shows [52 w/e 3 October 2026]. The majority of sales came from Huel’s RTD complete meals, now stocked in over 120,000 distribution points including major grocers Tesco , Sainsbury’s and Waitrose , alongside health-focused retailers Boots and Holland & Barrett . Sales are up by 25%, to £102.3m. Meanwhile, Huel’s high-protein Black Edition range has more than doubled its volume sales, adding £42.6m in RSV. Huel Lite, added last October to tap demand for products with reduced calories, is now worth £7.7m. Huel was seeing “increased demand for healthy, convenient meals and snacks across all the categories we play in”, chief sales officer Brian Hopkins-Parkinson told The Grocer. “When we launched into retail in 2019, retailers wanted to range us in the ambient pharmacy aisles with legacy slimming brands, but we knew that wasn’t the right location to build this category,” Hopkins-Parkinson said. “We launched with Sainsbury’s as they bought into our vision of selling our RTD complete meals in the fridges and alongside their food-to-go range.” Launching in the chilled section of stores had been “a huge driver in educating the consumer that this is a credible meal-on-the-go solution when you want something fast but healthy”, he added. “Being chilled also meant we were much better suited for instant consumption, which represents over 50% of our sales.” Subsequent distribution gains and more retailers electing to range meal drinks and protein shakes in their chillers had helped Huel amass a 73% value share of the category, Hopkins-Parkinson said. Meanwhile, NPD such as Huel Instant Ramen and further innovation in formats was also playing helping Huel reach more shoppers and play in more occasions, he noted. “In the last three years we have tripled our household penetration as more shoppers discover us in a new store or find a flavour or format that works for them. Our time-of-day sales show that lunch is our most common usage occasion but breakfast and snacking are also important.” Danone agreed to buy Huel in March, citing “exciting opportunities in the new and fast-growing nutritionally complete space”. The deal was cleared by the UK’s Competition & Markets Authority in August, greenlighting the Actimel owner to add Huel to its existing portfolio of dairy , water, and specialised nutrition products. Texto completo del artículo
As beverage producers prepare for 2027, the supply chain outlook is showing signs of stabilization, but significant cost and availability risks remain. Agrowgate ’s Q4 2026 Supply Chain Snapshot reveals a market where easing commodity prices are not necessarily translating into lower production costs . Aluminum remains expensive despite calmer market conditions, corrugated packaging prices continue to rise even as demand remains relatively flat, and tightening trucking capacity is creating renewed pressure on freight costs. Meanwhile, shifting trade policies, geopolitical uncertainty and constrained production capacity are complicating sourcing decisions across key beverage inputs. The outlook varies considerably by commodity, creating both challenges and opportunities for producers. Sugar supplies are tightening, with U.S. production projected to decline in the coming crop year, while Canadian oat production is expected to fall. At the same time, comfortable corn inventories are not necessarily translating into improved availability of corn-derived ingredients, and regional differences in glass and hop supplies are creating additional sourcing considerations.
