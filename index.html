Stress-relief drinks fuel next functional beverage boom

Las bebidas funcionales para alivio del estrés lideran el crecimiento del sector, impulsadas por la creciente demanda de salud y bienestar. El estrés se posiciona como la principal preocupación de wellness entre consumidores globales, generando una oportunidad significativa para innovación en formulaciones dirigidas. Este segmento refleja la evolución del mercado hacia bebidas con beneficios específicos y nutricionales convenientes, acelerando la expansión del portafolio funcional más allá de categorías tradicionales.

Texto completo del artículo
The functional beverage sector is thriving. Driven by rising consumer interest in health, wellness and convenient nutrition, functional beverages have evolved from a niche category into a major success story. And demand shows no signs of slowing. In fact, it’s speeding up, with consumers seeking increasingly targeted health benefits from their beverages. One area benefiting most from this shift is the stress-relief. Why? Because stress is now the top wellbeing concern amongst consumers globally. According to Innova Market Insights (IMI), 43% of consumers cite stress as a key issue, placing it well ahead of anxiety (36%) and sleep difficulties (32%). Stress-relief, mood-support and relaxation beverages are rapidly emerging as one of functional drinks’ most exciting growth opportunities. According to IMI, the segment is expanding at a similar pace to brain health beverages, posting annual growth of around 19%, and is already approaching the market share currently held by gut health drinks. Moreover, as immunity products begin to mature following their pandemic-fuelled boom, many industry observers see relaxation-focused beverages as the category’s next major growth driver. The trend reflects a broader shift in consumer priorities. While physical health remains important, consumers are increasingly looking for products that support emotional wellbeing, stress management and everyday balance. In response, brands are developing beverages formulated with functional ingredients, positioning them as convenient tools for relaxation and mood support. From start-ups to CPGs, manufacturers are investing heavily in the space, incorporating functional ingredients into sparkling beverages, ready-to-drink teas and wellness shots, as they look to capitalise on the trend. But which ingredients are driving the category’s growth? Chaga mushroom extract and guarana are among the fastest-growing ingredients in the stress-relief and mood-support beverage space. Turkey tail mushroom is also gaining momentum – the number of stress-relief and relaxation beverage launches featuring the ingredient rose from just two in 2023 to 13 in 2025, reflecting increasing innovation among manufacturers and growing consumer curiosity around new functional ingredients. While emerging ingredients are helping brands differentiate their offerings, more established botanicals and adaptogens continue to form the backbone of many formulations. Ashwagandha, L-theanine, lemon balm and chamomile remain popular among formulators due to their strong consumer recognition and association with relaxation, stress management and mood support. Their continued popularity reflects consumer preference for ingredients perceived as both natural and functional. Innovation is also accelerating across a wide range of product formats. From sparkling wellness beverages and functional waters to ready-to-drink teas and concentrated wellness shots, brands are increasingly targeting specific consumption occasions and consumer needs. This diversification is helping stress-relief beverages move beyond specialist wellness channels and into the mainstream. However, as the category grows, brands face increasing pressure to demonstrate the effectiveness of their products. Consumers are becoming more discerning and looking beyond marketing claims, placing greater importance on transparency, ingredient quality and scientific credibility. The regulatory environment is also becoming increasingly important, with health and wellbeing claims requiring robust scientific substantiation. As a result, evidence-backed formulations are likely to become an increasingly important point of differentiation. According to IMI consumer data, more than a third (36%) of consumers want functional beverages that help them relax and unwind, while a further 33% actively seek stress-relief benefits, and 22% search for mood-supporting products. The occasions for consumption are equally revealing. Nearly half (45%) of consumers enjoy stress-relief drinks at home, positioning them as part of everyday wellness routines rather than occasional purchases. Around 30% consume them as a dedicated “moment to myself”, suggesting they’re now being used as tools for self-care and relaxation. And a further 23% drink them during work or study, underlining demand for products that can help consumers navigate the pressures of busy, often stressful, daily lives. These consumption patterns point to a broader shift in how consumers view wellbeing and the role beverages can play in supporting it. Consumers are increasingly prioritising emotional resilience, mental wellness and everyday balance, creating opportunities for brands to develop products that address a broader range of functional needs. With stress ranked as the world’s leading wellbeing concern, the long-term outlook for relaxation-focused beverages remains strong. Continued innovation in ingredients, formats and functionality is expected to drive further growth, while consumers’ increasing focus on holistic wellness is likely to expand the category’s appeal. For manufacturers, the opportunity extends beyond simply helping consumers unwind. The next generation of stress-relief beverages will need to deliver credible functional benefits, fit naturally into daily routines and appeal to consumers seeking proactive approaches to wellbeing. As the functional beverage market continues to evolve, relaxation drinks look set to become one of its defining success stories.

The new beverage battleground is in AI recommendations vs. shelf space

Las bebidas funcionales están ganando visibilidad en las recomendaciones de IA (ChatGPT) independientemente de volúmenes de venta o precio, según estudio de 5W AI Communications. El posicionamiento estratégico como solución para necesidades del consumidor determinará la aparición en motores generativos, representando un nuevo canal de distribución paralelo al retail tradicional. Para marcas de bebidas, la optimización de IA se convierte en factor crítico de competitividad junto al espacio en estante, redefiniendo las estrategias de visibilidad y acceso al consumidor.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
In the exponentially expanding world of generative engine optimization, brands that position themselves as a solution to consumers’ needs are the ones that will increasingly show up in AI recommendations. Within the beverage category, functional beverages continuously showed up as the top recommendation in AI answers, regardless of whether the product moves volumes or is cheaper, according to recent research from 5W AI Communications. The AI communications firm looked across 60 prompts in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI for its 5W AI Visibility Index <a href="https://www.5wpr.com/ai-visibility-index/functional-beverages-ai-visibility-index-2026/" target="_blank" rel="">report</a> and found that functional beverages surfaced more than brands positioned around flavor, taste or sales volume. AI prompts from users are function-focused, according to Ronn Torossian, founder of 5W AI Communications. Within energy drinks, AI recommendations consistently favored Celsius, Liquid Death, Olipop and Poppi, whereas category giants Red Bull and Monster, which have moved significant unit sales over the last several decades, ranked lower. “What surprised me most was how completely uncorrelated sales and citation share turned out to be,” Torossian said. “Red Bull and Monster have owned the energy category for 20 years by unit sales. They’re the brands everyone thinks of first at the store, yet they’re sitting at second and seventh place in how often AI names them.” Why? With health as the main theme across consumer AI beverage prompts, AI agents are scraping the digital landscape and pulling up brands that tell a clear benefit narrative around their product, along with updated earned media and independent coverage, according to Torossian. Traditionally brands relied on search engine optimization (SEO) to show higher in search engine rankings. Now, brands are competing in <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/18/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo/" target="_blank" rel="">GEO</a> to show up as the first answers in AI searches. Ultimately, a brand’s relevance, or citation share, in an AI recommendation is increasingly based on two factors: what a consumer is searching for and how a brand answers those questions across their media presence. “Nobody asks a chatbot for the best-selling energy drink,” Torossian said. “They ask for the healthy one, the gut-healthy one, the clean one, the one with electrolytes. The brand that has clearly and repeatedly answered that specific question in the public record is the brand that gets named back.” In Celsius’ case, the brand expanded its citation share into the female and fitness energy drink space when it <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/08/celsius-dominates-the-us-energy-drink-market-while-setting-its-sights-on-europe/" target="_blank" rel="">acquired Alani Nu</a> in February 2025. “The Alani Nu acquisition matters more than people realize,” Torossian said. “It’s not just added revenue. It’s added citation surface.” Another example is PepsiCo’s<a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/07/22/is-pepsi-prebiotic-cola-a-game-changer-or-just-late-to-the-game/" target="_blank" rel=""> Poppi</a> acquisition last year, where the CPG giant is playing in the prebiotic soda space – a category Poppi and Olipop created, Torossian says. Both Poppi and Olipop consistently show up as leading gut-health soda recommendations in AI search, according to 5W. When it comes to AI, market leadership is not enough. Consumers are not searching for the best-selling energy drink, Torossian argues. They are looking for the one that supports a specific need, whether that’s energy, hydration or gut health. “Market leadership built through decades of distribution deals and shelf placement doesn’t automatically transfer into that answer,” he said. “It’s an entirely different kind of leadership that AI is rewarding.” For flavor-forward brands, they may win on retail shelves because consumers build a relationship with the product over time, Torossian argues, whereas citation is earned through a public record. “Loyalty and citation are earned in two completely different places, through two completely different mechanisms,” he said. “Flavor wins at the shelf, through taste, habit and repeat purchase.” By contrast, AI recommendations are built on documented information. “AI doesn’t experience taste. It reads,” Torossian said. “A brand can have won over thousands of loyal repeat customers on flavor alone and still be functionally invisible to an AI engine, simply because nobody’s written down what the product does beyond ‘tastes good.’” He emphasized the contrast: “The two forms of success just don’t overlap the way people assume they would.” One flavor-forward brand, Spindrift, which focuses on natural flavors, is still picked up by AI, but largely as a clean-label and low-sugar sparkling water. “Even Spindrift’s citation success is quietly borrowing a small functional claim to get in the door,” Torossian said. For AI visibility, flavor has a shrinking shelf life, Torossian said. “Flavor is what turns that first-time buyer into a repeat customer once they’ve actually tried it,” he said. “Function is what gets a startup discovered in the first place.” While flavor-centric brands can still compete today, Torossian says functional positioning will become increasingly important as AI-driven discovery grows. “As more competitors attach a real function to their brand story, the AI engines will have more well-documented functional options to choose from for every query,” he said. “The purely flavor-driven brands will simply get crowded out of the answer.” Torrossian adds: “It’s not that flavor becomes worthless. It stays essential for retention. But as a standalone strategy for AI visibility, it has a shrinking shelf life.”

Texto completo del artículo
UK sales of Bacardi-owned St-Germain more than doubled in the 12 months to March 2026 as the Hugo Spritz surged in popularity. The elderflower liqueur claims to have more than doubled its UK sales between 1 April 2026 and 31 March 2026, compared to the same period in 2024/25. Ahead of National Spritz Day (1 August), the brand revealed that summer Google searches for the Hugo Spritz have slightly nudged ahead of its orange-coloured competitor, the Aperol Spritz. According to analysis from the brand’s PR agency, Ogilvy, UK Google search trends for the 13 weeks ending 12 July 2026 show that Hugo Spritz recorded an average indexed interest of 55.8, compared with 53.9 for the Aperol version – an increase of approximately 3.6%. The Hugo Spritz recipe is similar, swapping Aperol for elderflower liqueur and the orange slice for fresh mint. Meanwhile, Google search interest for the three months to 16 July 2026 showed a 100% increase for the terms ‘Spritz recipe’ and ‘Hugo Spritz’ compared with the same period in 2025. The brand also noted growing consumer interest in other types of Spritz such as elderflower, rose, peach, frozen and alcohol-free versions, based on Google search trends. The boom in Spritz serves also follows the wider trend of consumers moving away from late-night consumption during the summer, the brand noted. It said ‘lighter-tasting, longer serves’ are better suited to occasions such as long lunches, garden gatherings, and relaxing early-evening moments. Franck Dedieu, global brand ambassador for St-Germain, said: “The Hugo Spritz has become the drink of the summer because it captures exactly how people want to socialise now.” This is backed by a UK consumer survey from the 2026 Bacardi Cocktail Trends Report , which found 34% of drinkers aged 18 to 29 selected the Spritz as their number-one cocktail choice for summer 2026. The research also found that 37% of 18- to 29-year-olds and 42% of 30- to 44-year-olds said they now socialise earlier in the evening than they did a year ago. In May, St-Germain called on Game of Thrones actor Sophie Turner to front its latest campaign , Delight in Life. According to The Brand Champions 2026 report, Campari Group-owned Aperol sold 10 million nine-litre cases in 2025, an increase of 1%. The Italian company’s sales in the UK rose by 7% last year , driven by Aperol. The brand also recorded ‘solid growth’ in Europe during the first quarter of 2026 , supported by the introduction of new formats, such as its canned Aperol Spritz cocktail. The company’s new portfolio strategy in the past year has been focused on “fewer, bigger bets” and a push for Aperol in the US , where the brand grew by 15% in the on-trade last year. Texto completo del artículo
French spirits group Rémy Cointreau has delivered better-than-expected first quarter results, as a recovery in cognac sales helped offset declines in liqueurs and spirits. Sales at the Rémy Martin owner rose 1.3% organically to €223.2m, ahead of the 0.2% rise forecast by analysts. Cognac sales climbed 7.7% organically, with strong growth in APAC offsetting declines in the Americas. Spirits and liqueur sales, meanwhile, fell 6.6% organically, compared to analyst forecasts of a 1.5% decline. Rémy blamed “unfavourable phasing” of shipments in the US for the miss. Despite the beat, the Cointreau brand owner maintained its full-year forecasts for the year. It expects to return to “sustainable” organic sales growth, with sales improving “progressively over the year”. Full-year sales at Rémy Cointreau declined 5% to €935.3m in the year to 31 March , with the majority of the decline coming from currency movements. The group registered organic sales growth of 0.2% during the year. Texto completo del artículo
Moët Hennessy se ha incorporado como nuevo miembro de pleno derecho de The Portman Group, la entidad de autorregulación del sector del alcohol en Reino Unido. La entrada de la división de vinos y espirituosos de LVMH eleva a 20 el número de miembros de pleno derecho y sitúa a la organización en su mayor tamaño hasta la fecha. La incorporación coincide con el 30 aniversario del Código de Prácticas de The Portman Group, una norma publicada por primera vez en 1996 para regular el nombre, el envase y la promoción de las bebidas alcohólicas. La entidad sostiene que ese código busca que el alcohol se promocione de forma socialmente responsable, solo entre mayores de 18 años y sin un atractivo particular para personas vulnerables. El consejero delegado de The Portman Group, Matt Lambert, afirmó que la llegada de un nuevo miembro refuerza el papel de la autorregulación dentro del marco de responsabilidad del sector productor en Reino Unido. Lambert añadió que la trayectoria y la experiencia de Moët Hennessy serán útiles para orientar el trabajo del grupo en los próximos años, con el objetivo de mantener la protección del consumidor y preservar una comercialización responsable dentro del sector. Por parte de la compañía francesa, la directora general de Moët Hennessy UKI, Julie Nollet, señaló que el éxito a largo plazo de sus maisons depende de conservar la confianza de consumidores, responsables políticos y sociedad. Nollet explicó que por ese motivo la empresa se suma a The Portman Group y respalda su compromiso con el marketing responsable. A su juicio, una autorregulación sólida y creíble es esencial para que innovación, creatividad y responsabilidad avancen juntas. The Portman Group fue creada en 1989 y actúa como organismo regulador del sector del alcohol y como entidad centrada en responsabilidad social. Además de sus miembros de pleno derecho y asociados, cuenta con más de 170 firmantes de su código entre productores, minoristas y organizaciones sectoriales. La organización informa de que está financiada por 23 empresas miembros y asociadas. Entre ellas figuran Asahi UK Ltd, Aston Manor Cider, Bacardi, Suntory, Brown-Forman, Budweiser Brewing Group UK&I, Campari, C&C, Coca-Cola GB, Damm UK, Diageo GB, Edrington UK, Heineken UK, Lucky Saint, Mark Anthony Brands International, Mast-Jägermeister UK, Moët Hennessy, Molson Coors Beverage Company, Pernod Ricard UK, Punch Pubs & Co, SHS Drinks, Thatchers y Treasury Wine Estates. Sobre el funcionamiento del código, The Portman Group asegura que más de 200 productos han sido modificados o retirados del mercado tras su aplicación. Añade también que cientos más pudieron ajustarse a las normas antes de llegar a los lineales gracias al servicio de asesoramiento previo. La entidad precisa además que el texto ha sido actualizado en seis ocasiones a lo largo de estos 30 años para adaptarse a cambios en las actitudes públicas y ampliar su alcance. Moët Hennessy es la división de vinos y espirituosos del grupo LVMH. También agrupa viñedos bajo la entidad LVMH Vins d’Exceptions. La empresa reúne 25 maisons con presencia internacional y vincula su actividad a un programa ambiental y social denominado Living Soils Living Together. Con esta adhesión, The Portman Group suma un nuevo respaldo empresarial en un momento en el que la industria británica del alcohol quiere reforzar su mensaje sobre la utilidad del modelo de autorregulación para ordenar la promoción comercial del sector. Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 8 días · Actualizado 2026-07-24

La semana estuvo dominada por reportes de resultados Q2 2026 de los grandes jugadores globales, con un panorama mixto: mientras Coca-Cola, Monster Beverage y KDP mostraron resiliencia vía poder de precios, Boston Beer decepcionó al mercado con caídas en volumen y EPS muy por debajo de expectativas. La tendencia hacia bebidas sin azúcar y sin alcohol ganó protagonismo como vector de crecimiento en portafolios de LATAM y Europa. A nivel regional, Arca Continental defendió márgenes ante el nuevo impuesto a bebidas en México, y Walmart de México reconoció que el Mundial FIFA 2026 no generó el impulso de consumo esperado. Un ciberataque confirmado sobre la subsidiaria Fairlife de Coca-Cola y la expiración del decreto de consentimiento DOJ sobre AB InBev-SABMiller marcaron el tono en riesgos regulatorios y reputacionales.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Boston Beer reportó resultados Q2 2026 muy por debajo de expectativas con EPS ajustado de $3.65 vs. $4.83 estimado y depletions cayendo 6%, evidenciando una crisis de portafolio más allá de sus marcas en crecimiento.
  • Coca-Cola confirmó un ciberataque de ransomware sobre Fairlife que paralizó operaciones de la subsidiaria, representando el mayor evento de riesgo reputacional y operativo de la semana para el sector.
  • Arca Continental sostuvo un margen EBITDA del 20.7% e ingresos de 63,489 mdp pese a la implementación del nuevo impuesto a bebidas en México, con Coca-Cola Zero creciendo 19% en volumen durante Q2.
  • La Comisión de Competencia de Sudáfrica aprobó la adquisición de Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA) por parte de Coca-Cola HBC, consolidando la mayor operación de embotellado del continente africano.
  • Expiración del decreto de consentimiento DOJ que regulaba la fusión AB InBev-SABMiller abre un nuevo escenario competitivo en distribución en EE.UU., con implicancias directas para el acceso al canal de marcas independientes.
Finanzas

Boston Beer fue el gran perdedor de la semana con EPS ajustado de $3.65 vs. estimado de $4.83, ingresos netos cayendo 3.3% a $568M y depletions en -6%, lo que refleja una contracción estructural en su portafolio más allá de Twisted Tea. En contraste, United Spirits (Diageo India) reportó un salto de 52% en ganancias netas y Arca Continental sostuvo un EBITDA del 20.7% pese al nuevo impuesto mexicano a bebidas, demostrando mayor resiliencia operativa en mercados emergentes.

Marketing

La premiumización y las bebidas sin alcohol continuaron siendo los ejes creativos de la semana: Heineken posicionó su tecnología SmartDispense como motor de márgenes en pubs británicos y su línea 0.0 como palanca de crecimiento global, mientras Diageo India incrementó su inversión publicitaria a Rs 312 crores y tomó una participación del 10% en la startup de craft spirits Nuvola. El boom de marcas de celebrities spirits mostró señales claras de agotamiento, con Casamigos entre los casos de declive citados por analistas del sector.

Supply Chain

Bacardi formalizó un acuerdo global con EcoSpirits para packaging de circuito cerrado, sumándose a Pernod Ricard en una apuesta por logística sostenible que podría redefinir estándares de la industria de destilados. Suntory PepsiCo inauguró una planta de $300M en Vietnam orientada a bebidas más saludables, señalando una reconfiguración de capacidad productiva en Asia-Pacífico hacia categorías de bajo contenido calórico.

Ventas / Comercial

Coca-Cola sellò una alianza estratégica con Marriott International para expandir distribución de su portafolio en el canal hotelero global, un movimiento que refuerza el on-premise premium. Heineken Costa Rica adquirió la marca de agua artesanal RainForest Water, ampliando su presencia en el canal de bebidas premium no alcohólicas en Centroamérica y marcando un movimiento relevante en la estrategia de diversificación de portafolio en la región.
