Texto completo del artículo
BrauBeviale 2026, running from November 10 to 12, brings together beer, mineral water, soft drinks, juice, wine, sparkling wine and spirits makers, alongside the ingredients, processing and packaging technologies supporting them. But perhaps the clearest trend at this year’s show is drinks without alcohol. Non-alcoholic beer continues to command attention in western markets, but the opportunity now stretches far beyond traditional alcohol alternatives. BrauBeviale’s conference programme and exhibitors, for example, put the likes of RTD mocktails, non-alcoholic wines and other emerging drinks formats in the spotlight as manufacturers look to build broader portfolios for changing drinking occasions. That evolution is also visible in beer itself. The show looks beyond simply removing alcohol, asking where brewers can take the category next, while a dedicated session on dispensing non-alcoholic beer points to the practical considerations emerging as the category matures. Functionality is another theme moving rapidly into the mainstream. Functional beverages, shots, bitters and kombucha brands feature among the many other drinks at the show, reflecting the increasingly blurred lines between refreshment and products designed around wider wellness needs. A dedicated Discovery Bar tasting brings those categories together, giving visitors the chance to explore emerging beverage concepts alongside more established categories. And innovation is spreading into unexpected parts of the drinks market. Malt-based soft drinks feature on the conference agenda, illustrating how producers are exploring ingredients and processes traditionally associated with brewing to create products outside conventional beer occasions. Then there is sugar. Reducing sugar without sacrificing the experience consumers expect remains a key formulation challenge, with the programme exploring routes towards lower-sugar beverages alongside wider innovation in ingredients and production. But some of the biggest changes are happening behind the bottle. AI, automation, energy efficiency and smarter production form a substantial part of this year’s conversation. Conference sessions will examine how robotics and AI could help reduce operating and energy costs, how manufacturers can extract greater value from brewery data, and how electrification could reshape heat-intensive beverage production. The Brewery 2030+ Forum will push that thinking further, asking what tomorrow’s brewery should look like, while discussions around energy and resource efficiency bring sustainability firmly into the production conversation. Together, these trends point towards a beverage industry becoming more flexible in what it produces, more adventurous in what it puts into the bottle, and much smarter about how it makes it. For the beverage industry, that means plenty to watch: from the continuing evolution of no-and-low and functional drinks, all the way to the technologies promising to change how beverages are formulated, processed and produced.
