Walmart’s promise on ‘surveillance pricing’ sets the tone for retail

Walmart se comprometió públicamente a no implementar 'surveillance pricing' (precios dinámicos basados en datos individuales), posicionándose como líder en confianza del consumidor. Esta decisión impacta directamente la estrategia de pricing de bebidas y alimentos en retail, estableciendo un precedente que competidores y marcas deben considerar. En un contexto de inflación persistente y desconfianza post-pandemia, la política de Walmart sobre precios transparentes influye en las dinámicas de negociación entre fabricantes de bebidas y canales de distribución, y refuerza la importancia de la confianza como diferencial competitivo en mercados con sensibilidad al precio.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
Trust lies at the center of the relationship among grocers, food and beverage manufacturers and shoppers. More than half a decade of upheaval – from <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/21/grocery-inflation-is-expected-to-rise-later-this-year/" target="_blank" rel="">inflation</a> and pandemic-driven shifts to online shopping and <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/15/doj-egg-price-fixing-settlement-criticized-as-too-small/" target="_blank" rel="">price-fixing</a> scandals involving major commodities – has put that trust to the test. That became clear in late September, when Walmart President and CEO John Furner penned a letter to the retail giant’s customers promising that the company was not using its technology to overcharge them with its rapidly growing fleet of electronic shelf labels (ESL). It was a surprising move for a company that has spent the last decade investing heavily in tech as a <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/06/24/walmart-amazon-escalate-grocery-delivery-war-as-same-day-demand-surges/" target="_blank" rel="">challenge</a> to its biggest competitor, Amazon. Walmart’s announcement follows a wave of <a href="https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/9/algorithmic-pricing-under-fire-state-restrictions-on-personalized-and-surveillance-pricing" target="_blank" rel="">state laws</a> and city ordinances restricting the use of personal data to set grocery prices. Maryland’s <a href="https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS" target="_blank" rel="">law</a> took effect Oct. 1, but it is not the most recently enacted measure: <a href="https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/2026/20260723a.shtml" target="_blank" rel="">New Jersey enacted</a> a related law in July, and <a href="https://wilson.seattle.gov/2026/09/23/seattle-becomes-first-city-to-stop-grocery-stores-from-using-ai-based-personal-data-to-set-prices/" target="_blank" rel="">Seattle’s mayor signed</a> a grocery-pricing ordinance Oct. 5. The important distinction is that most of these measures target personalized “surveillance pricing,” rather than banning all price changes driven by demand, inventory or time of day. It’s not just states that are giving a skeptical side-eye to the industry. Grocers are also taking heat from the Trump Administration, which launched an <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/12/09/trump-orders-probe-into-rising-food-prices/" target="_blank" rel="">investigation</a> into high grocery prices last December, noting at the time that, “in recent years, major players in America’s food supply chain have paid tens of millions to settle price-fixing lawsuits.” Trump talked big in his <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/addressing-security-risks-from-price-fixing-and-anti-competitive-behavior-in-the-food-supply-chain/" target="_blank" rel="">Executive Order 14364</a>, noting that some sectors of the food supply, “including meat processing, seed, fertilizer, and equipment” are vulnerable to price-fixing. But when it came time to punish companies, particularly in the egg industry, that settled on antitrust cases brought by the US Department of Justice, the DOJ gave what critics called a <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/15/doj-egg-price-fixing-settlement-criticized-as-too-small/" target="_blank" rel="">slap on the wrist</a>. Furner wrote in his letter that Walmart doesn’t “set different prices based on who you are or the time of day, and we won’t.” Irrespective of the customer’s “income, shopping history, urgency or what we think you could pay” will never have an impact on the price, <a href="https://corporate.walmart.com/about/everyday-affordability/letter-from-our-ceo" target="_blank" rel="">Furner promised</a>. In the era of artificial intelligence, where retailers’ use of information remains hidden from consumers, it might come as no surprise that they would question the integrity of grocery executives, but Furner addressed the issue as well. “We want to offer that same level of personalized care when our customers shop online with Sparky, our AI shopping assistant. When you engage with Sparky, that’s an invitation to serve you better, not to use your personal information to set a personalized price,” he explained. What did Walmart do to require such a public declaration? In a word, nothing. Much of the criticism on electronic shelf labels is being spread by the United Food and Commercial Workers International Union (UFCW), which represents 1.2 million workers, more than 800,000 of whom are grocery workers. In March, UFCW <a href="https://www.ufcw.org/press-releases/ufcw-slams-walmarts-decision-to-roll-out-electronic-shelf-labels-chainwide-urges-lawmakers-to-act-quickly/" target="_blank" rel="">condemned</a> Walmart’s rollout of ESL, also referred to as digital price tags, basing much of its argument on a study released by the Federal Trade Commission on Jan. 20, 2025, just three days before Trump’s inauguration. The <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer" target="_blank" rel="">FTC report</a>, however, never makes a single statement or appears to have ever studied digital price tags. Rather, the investigation into “surveillance pricing” aimed “to better understand the shadowy market that third-party intermediaries use to set individualized prices for products and services based on consumers’ characteristics and behaviors, like location, demographics, browsing patterns and shopping history,” according to the FTC. It did reveal that online shopping could be an opportunity for retailers to engage in dynamic pricing. The report noted: “Staff found that consumer behaviors ranging from mouse movements on a webpage to the type of products that consumers leave unpurchased in an online shopping cart can be tracked and used by retailers to tailor consumer pricing.” Tucked away at the tail end of the UFCW statement appears to be the primary motivation for its opposition to the ESLs: jobs. The union noted the threat these systems pose to skilled grocery clerks. “UFCW members are essential to keeping our communities fed, and they know how disruptive ESLs could be for workers and shoppers alike,” the union said. The absence of a smoking gun doesn’t mean grocery giants and affiliated companies are off the hook. In December 2025, Consumer Reports and Groundwork Collaborative published an <a href="https://www.consumerreports.org/money/questionable-business-practices/instacart-ai-pricing-experiment-inflating-grocery-bills-a1142182490/" target="_blank" rel="">investigative report</a> showing that Instacart had used artificial intelligence by Eversight to charge customers different prices. “The investigation found that Instacart’s algorithmic pricing experiments could result in price differences as high as 23% for certain products and could cost families more than $1,200 a year at checkout,” <a href="https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/consumer-reports-statement-on-new-personalized-pricing-proposal-from-the-ftc/" target="_blank" rel="">Consumer Reports said</a> in August. Instacart quickly ended the practice of charging customers different prices for the same products. Consumer Reports’ comment in August was in response to an announcement by FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson that the commission <a href="https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/20/ftc-warns-businesses-on-surveillance-pricing/" target="_blank" rel="">sought public comment</a> on an enforcement statement it will use to enforce deceptive use of personalized pricing. FTC said that while personalized pricing is not prohibited in all circumstances – rideshare at different times of the day and in certain situations, for instance – deceptive pricing violates Section 5 of the FTC Act. “When consumers see a listed price, they expect it to be the same price that everyone else sees, not the retailer’s estimate of how much they are willing to pay based on their personal data,” Ferguson said in August. What does this all mean for retailers and CPG? It’s clear that it comes back to that central tenet of trust. Perception is reality in the world of grocery, and if consumers believe those tiny electronic price tags are collecting their data and jacking up prices, then that’s the reality retailers must address, whether real or fabricated. Walmart made the right decision by addressing shoppers’ concerns directly through its CEO, rather than leaving the response to a company spokesperson. Furner’s commitment puts the retail giant’s credibility on the line if it adopts surveillance pricing – and sets a standard shoppers, as well as food and beverage manufacturers, can demand from the rest of the industry.

The German government has approved a draft plan to introduce a tax on sugary drinks, with the proposed levy set to take effect on July 1, 2027, subject to parliamentary approval. According to the finance ministry, the tax aims to reduce sugar consumption, generate revenue for the healthcare system and protect public health, “particularly of children and adolescents”. The proposed tax would apply to drinks containing at least 5g of sugar per 100ml, with a rate of €0.26 per litre for drinks containing at least 5g and up to 8g of sugar per 100ml, rising to €0.32 per litre for those containing more than 8g. The measures would cover soft drinks, energy drinks, lemonades, iced teas and sweetened fruit drinks. Pure fruit and vegetable juices, non-alcoholic beers and wines, and alcoholic drinks would be exempt. Smaller manufacturers producing up to 70,000 litres annually would be exempt from the duty. A further exemption for businesses producing up to one million litres a year would require approval from the European Commission under state aid rules. For manufacturers subject to the levy, the additional duty could affect production costs and pricing strategies. Businesses will need to assess whether to absorb the tax, pass some of the cost on to retailers and consumers, or adjust their product portfolios in response. The German government expects the levy to generate around €690 million in 2027, rising to €925 million in 2028. The revenue is intended to help stabilise contributions to the statutory health insurance system. The proposal has faced criticism over its potential economic impact. German public broadcaster ARD’s Tagesschau news service reported that Hessian Minister-President Boris Rhein of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) argued that the tax would make drinks more expensive and could threaten jobs in the beverage industry. The tiered structure could give manufacturers a financial incentive to reduce sugar content in products, particularly those close to the proposed thresholds. Companies could review existing recipes to assess whether reformulation would move products into a lower tax band or below the taxable threshold. The UK’s Soft Drinks Industry Levy, introduced in 2018, provides an example of how a tiered tax can influence product formulation. The UK government reported that the average sugar content per 100ml in affected soft drinks fell by 28.8% between 2015 and 2018. By early 2019, only around 15% of eligible soft drinks still contained enough sugar to be liable for the levy, down from 52% when the policy was first announced in 2016, according to the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR). Germany would join several European countries that already tax sugary drinks, including France, Portugal and Hungary. Hungary introduced a broader public health tax in 2011 covering certain products with high levels of sugar and other ingredients. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends taxing sugar-sweetened beverages as a public health measure, citing evidence that such taxes can increase prices and reduce purchases of taxed drinks. Depending on how a tax is designed, it can also encourage manufacturers to reformulate products to reduce sugar content. However, the effectiveness of these measures remains contested. The US-based Tax Foundation questioned the evidence for improved health outcomes in a 2025 report, arguing that higher prices do not necessarily translate into significant improvements in health. Germany’s proposal follows months of political debate. Earlier proposals reportedly considered extending the tax to a wider range of drinks, including some ready-made coffee products and beverages containing artificial sweeteners, prompting opposition from conservative politicians. The coalition ultimately settled on a two-tier structure, with the draft legislation now set to go before parliament. If approved, the tax would give manufacturers until the proposed July 2027 implementation date to assess its potential impact on costs, pricing and product formulations.

  • Constellation Brands adquirió SpikedAde por USD 75 millones con earn-out de hasta USD 278 millones, apostando al boom del segmento de bebidas alcohólicas deportivas justo cuando los RTD de spirits crecen +21% en escaneos semanales.
  • Coca-Cola lanzó versiones prebióticas de sus marcas principales y anunció una agenda completa de funcionales en NACS 2026, marcando su mayor movimiento estratégico hacia el segmento de salud y bienestar.
  • PepsiCo retiró más de 122 mil cajas de Gatorade en 37 estados de EE.UU. por colorantes no declarados en el etiquetado, generando un impacto reputacional y operativo significativo.
  • En Argentina, el consumo en supermercados de CABA cayó 1,2% interanual y los mayoristas acumularon diez trimestres en baja, mientras la cerveza artesanal local enfrenta caída de demanda y ajuste de costos.
Finanzas

PepsiCo redujo su proyección de crecimiento de ganancias para 2026 de 4-6% a 1-2%, reflejando que la recuperación en Norteamérica tomará más tiempo del previsto, mientras el crecimiento internacional sostiene los números globales. Constellation Brands superó estimaciones respaldada por cerveza y destilados y concretó la adquisición de SpikedAde por USD 75 millones, con earn-out potencial de hasta USD 278 millones adicionales.

Marketing

Coca-Cola presentó en NACS una agenda de innovación 2027 que incluye versiones prebióticas de Coca-Cola Zero Sugar y Sprite Zero, nuevos sabores y bebidas funcionales, consolidando su apuesta por el segmento better-for-you. Nestlé cerró su mayor acuerdo global de marketing al convertir a Milo, Nescau y Nesquik en Socios Oficiales de la NBA, mientras AriZona ingresó al segmento alcohólico en México con AriZona Hard, una línea de hard seltzers con base de tequila.

Supply Chain

PepsiCo retiró más de 122 mil cajas de Gatorade en 37 estados de EE.UU. por declaración incorrecta de colorantes en el etiquetado, un golpe reputacional y operativo relevante para la marca líder en hidratación deportiva. El precio del café sigue presionando los márgenes de Nestlé y el alza de costos de materias primas fue citada explícitamente por PepsiCo al justificar nuevos aumentos de precios en snacks y bebidas.

Ventas / Comercial

El hard discount gana terreno en México: Tiendas 3B creció ventas 20% frente al 1,8% de Walmart, señal de una migración del consumidor hacia formatos de precio en contexto de menor poder adquisitivo. En Argentina, el consumo en supermercados de CABA cayó 1,2% interanual en Q2 2026 y los mayoristas acumularon diez trimestres consecutivos en baja, consolidando un escenario de contracción sostenida en el canal tradicional.
