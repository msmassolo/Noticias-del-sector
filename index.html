Why relaxation could be the next functional beverage frontier

El mercado de bebidas funcionales está evolucionando hacia categorías enfocadas en relajación y bienestar mental, alejándose del tradicional énfasis en energía y rendimiento. Retailers como Walmart están capitalizando esta tendencia con categorías dedicadas como 'Mood and Mindset', reconociendo una oportunidad de crecimiento. Esta shift representa una segmentación de mercado potencialmente significativa para portafolios de bebidas funcionales en Argentina, donde el consumidor también busca productos wellness más holísticos.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
After years of consumers reaching for beverages promising an energy boost, improved performance or an extra dose of protein, the functional drinks market could be entering a new phase: one focused on helping people slow down. In the US, relaxation beverages are beginning to attract greater mainstream attention, with retailers increasingly recognising the opportunity. Walmart is among those taking notice, introducing a dedicated ‘Mood and Mindset’ category. One of the brands looking to capitalise on this opportunity is Recess, founded in 2017 by Benjamin Witte. Initially built around the idea of providing consumers with a way to unwind, the US brand has developed a range of relaxation-focused beverages and mood-supporting products. In October 2025, Kyle Thomas joined Witte as president and co-CEO, coinciding with the company’s $30m Series B funding round. Thomas brings experience across some of the beverage industry’s established and emerging brands, having previously held senior commercial roles at Coca-Cola and Nutrabolt, the maker of C4 Energy, as well as working on Bloom’s energy and better-for-you soda businesses. With Recess looking to expand its retail presence and establish relaxation as a mainstream beverage category, Thomas believes the opportunity could follow a similar trajectory to energy drinks, which evolved from a relatively unfamiliar proposition into one of the industry’s biggest segments. In the late 1990s and early 2000s, brands such as Red Bull and Monster were helping establish a category that was still unfamiliar to many consumers. Since then, energy drinks have evolved beyond their original proposition, expanding into performance-led products and becoming an established part of the beverage landscape. Thomas believes relaxation could follow a similar trajectory, as consumers increasingly look for ways to balance stimulation with unwinding. “At some point you can only stimulate so much, where there has to be kind of the opposite end of the spectrum,” he says. He sees an opportunity for relaxation beverages to meet this emerging need, with greater consumer awareness and understanding of their benefits helping to unlock wider adoption. “I think you’ll see the same thing happen with relaxation, where it become an increased benefit,” he adds. For Thomas, the opportunity is underpinned by a wider shift towards functional nutrition, with consumers increasingly incorporating products designed to support specific health and wellness goals into their everyday routines. He points to the rise of protein as an example of how an ingredient once associated with specialist nutrition has become part of mainstream consumer habits. “I saw it in my previous life with how much protein jumped up in the last couple of years. Protein’s been around forever. It’s something that became a part of the everyday lifestyle journey,” he says. He sees a similar shift across active nutrition, with products containing ingredients such as creatine and colostrum becoming increasingly familiar to consumers. This growing engagement with functional products also reflects a greater understanding of the importance of mental as well as physical health. “The consumer is already there in so many different platforms, from stick packs and beverage to supplements that they buy online,” he says. However, while consumers are becoming more familiar with functional products, Thomas believes there is still an opportunity to establish how relaxation beverages fit into their everyday lives. Rather than focusing solely on what goes into a relaxation beverage, Thomas believes brands need to communicate why consumers might want to reach for one in the first place. For Recess, that proposition is built around the idea of taking a break. Its positioning encourages consumers to step away from the noise of everyday life, whether that’s during a busy working day or when they simply need a moment to reset. He argues that the opportunity is less about making people feel sleepy and more about helping them feel calm, focused and composed. “It was never so much about the energy. I think the consumer shift that’s happening and what Recess does is sort of give permission to the consumer to say, ‘Hey, it’s okay to ring the bell and take a recess’,” he explains. “That’s the big education part that we have to continue to push and communicate.” That focus on everyday occasions is also shaping Recess’s product development strategy, with the brand looking to extend its proposition beyond relaxation drinks and into new social settings, including the growing no/low-alcohol market. As consumers become more conscious of their drinking habits, Thomas sees an opportunity for beverages that allow people to socialise and unwind without alcohol. Recess is exploring this through its zero-proof range, which includes mini margarita packs, party packs and non-alcoholic martinis. For Thomas, the opportunity is not about replacing alcohol altogether, but offering consumers greater flexibility. “I don’t believe alcohol is dead by any stretch of the imagination,” he says. “There’s just a different awareness of the right time and fit for alcohol in your daily life.” He believes alcohol-free alternatives can help consumers feel part of social occasions without the effects of drinking, particularly the following morning. “I want to feel like I belong when I’m out,” he explains. However, expanding into new occasions and formats does not mean compromising on the fundamentals of beverage development. For Thomas, taste remains the biggest driver of repeat purchases, regardless of a product’s functional proposition. “Flavour and taste will always be number one,” he says. “If it doesn’t taste good, consumers ultimately don’t come back. So, no matter what you’re doing, if you don’t lead with taste and flavour, then you’re going to have a pretty long road.” For manufacturers, the message is clear: functional ingredients and health benefits may attract initial interest, but the drinking experience ultimately determines whether consumers return. “I’ve said this for a long time: products live on the shelf, but brands live in the hearts and minds of consumers, and you can’t get there unless you have a phenomenal taste.” As relaxation beverages move towards the mainstream, Thomas expects competition to intensify. Having witnessed the evolution of energy drinks and better-for-you soda, he believes established manufacturers will soon look towards relaxation as another opportunity for growth. His prediction comes amid a wider shift among major beverage groups towards becoming ‘total beverage companies’, expanding beyond their traditional categories to capture a broader range of consumer preferences and drinking occasions. Molson Coors, for example, has been building its portfolio beyond beer through investments in energy drink ZOA, non-alcoholic cocktail brand Naked Life and a US partnership with mixer brand Fever-Tree. Carlsberg has also expanded its soft drinks exposure through the acquisition of Britvic, while groups such as Suntory and Asahi operate across alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, including soft drinks, energy drinks and ready-to-drink products. Diageo, meanwhile, has extended its portfolio with alcohol-free offerings including Guinness 0.0, Gordon’s 0.0 and Seedlip. Thomas expects established players such as Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP) to take an interest in relaxation, while also pointing to the potential involvement of alcohol businesses looking to diversify their portfolios. “The next kind of consumer wave is already here, and it starts with relaxation. So, there’s no doubt in my mind that you’re going to see a very similar race to the finish,” he says. That said, he doesn’t necessarily see this as a threat to emerging brands. Instead, he believes the arrival of larger players could help legitimise the category and accelerate its development. “You haven’t really made it until you’ve shown up on a PowerPoint inside the four walls of Atlanta or Coca-Cola or a KDP or a Pepsi type business,” he says. “The more competitors that jump in is really good for the category.” Reaching this level of growth brings new challenges, however. “Once you get to that $100 million mark, it creates a lot of complexity, not just internally in your organisation, but also as you scale into more mainstream,” Thomas says. For Recess, the priority is to build the infrastructure, retail relationships and innovation pipeline needed to support its next phase of growth, while establishing itself as a leading name in the relaxation category. Looking three to five years ahead, Thomas expects relaxation beverages to have become a much more established part of the beverage landscape, with Recess aiming to remain at the forefront of that development. “Recess will be synonymous with this category,” he says.

Gen Z’s Sobering Shift Reshapes Bar Menus

Gen Z reduce su consumo de alcohol en un 13 puntos porcentuales (de 60.5% a 47.5% en consumo mensual) según datos de SAMHSA 2002-2024. Esta tendencia está impulsando cambios en las cartas de bares y restaurantes hacia opciones sin alcohol. Para la industria de bebidas, representa una amenaza a volúmenes tradicionales pero una oportunidad en segmentos de bebidas no alcohólicas, aguas funcionales e innovación en mocktails.

Texto completo del artículo
It appears getting hammered may no longer be high on the priority list for Gen Z, and experts say that’s a sign restaurants and bars need to make sure their menus provide attractive alternatives. Data from the U.S. Substance Abuse and mental Health Services Administration collected between 2002 and 2024 was analyzed by wellness firm Tmrw. The analysis found past-month drinking among 18- to 25-year-olds fell 13 percentage points, from 60.5% to 47.5%. Among the younger cohort, those 18-20, consumption declined from 51.4% to 27.2%. “The pattern suggests this is not a generational identity,” the survey said. “It’s a repeating structural shift: Each new cohort of underage Americans drinks less than the one before it. When they cross 21, the rate of decline slows. Then the next wave of under-21s picks up where they left off. If that holds, Gen Alpha, now entering their late teens, may take it further than any generation before them.” Benno Nelson, co-founder of Five Corners Beverage Co., an alcohol-free cocktail company based in Chicago, told The Food Institute that Gen Z isn’t feeling the same pressure as millennials to drink alcohol as part of a night out, partly because of growing access to cannabis. “They also have a much more nuanced and intentional understanding around nutrition and wellness than millennials did 15-20 years ago,” Nelson said. “This illustrates why the best bar owners ensure their menu has a diverse set of offerings for today’s diverse drinking culture. “Beer, wine, spirits, cocktails and NA offerings are all a key component of the modern bar — and that will only continue.” The decline in alcohol consumption was not spread evenly across the country. In the past three years, Arizona posted the biggest decline, 11.8 percentage points, followed by Hawaii, 10.2 percentage points. Alcohol consumption also went down rapidly in Iowa, Wisconsin, Nebraska, Montana, the Dakotas, Massachusetts and Alaska. The percentage drop between 2021 to 2024 ranged between 8.4% and 5.6% in those states. “The trajectory of these states raises a question that the longevity science community is beginning to ask seriously: If the youngest residents of these states continue drinking less than every cohort before them, and if that pattern compounds over the next 20 to 30 years, what might the long-term health profile of those communities look like?” Tmrw asked. Kyle Harder, CEO of HTH Imports, said he’s not convinced Gen Z consumption is in a tailspin. “These consumers are looking for interesting brands that deliver high quality for a fair price. I would tend to agree with the Tmrw research, in that Gen Z is drinking different across occasions, products, prices, types and this is going to lead to even more segmentation,” Harder said. “Can you imagine 20 years ago someone telling you that Vodkade or Spiked Ade would be a thing? “Add in THC options, discretionary income challenges, inflation, digital/social media, dating behavior, etc. and you get to where we are today.” Not all states registered declines. Drinking increased in multiple states – most notably Alabama, 5.8 percentage points; Tennessee, 5.2 percentage points; and North Carolina, 3.5 percentage points. Several industry trends are driving increased interest in high-performance food-grade lubricants. Arnie Santa Ana, business development manager for GIT Food at Klüber Lubrication, Londonderry, N.H., notes that among the most significant trends impacting demand is heightened food safety expectations, increased automation, and the continued pressure on beverage manufacturers to improve operational efficiency while minimizing downtime. “There is also a growing trend with adoption of preventative and predictive maintenance,” he explains. “Thus, more companies are seeing the benefit of selecting high-performance food-grade lubricants to reduce wear, extend component life, reduce unplanned downtime, as well as moving toward condition-based oil change intervals versus calendar-based intervals via oil analysis programs on critical assets.” Ellie Girard, regional manager for Lubriplate Lubricants Co., Newark, N.J., considers worldwide food-safety regulations to be the largest factor impacting the demand for NSF/H1 registered food-grade lubricants. “For example, we are seeing bans on lubricants that are formulated with Mineral Oil Saturated Hydrocarbons (MOSH) and Mineral Oil Aromatic Hydrocarbons (MOAH) in food and beverage production and packaging,” Girard says. “MOAH containing NSF/H1 food grade lubricants are the major concern. This is forcing a shift from mineral oil free lubricants to 100% synthetic based lubricants. “There is global alignment on phasing out PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) in finished lubricants,” she continues. “This shift is pushing lubricant manufacturers toward offering safer synthetic and bio-based options. All these regulatory changes impact the use and demand for food-grade lubricants.” Girard adds that beyond regulatory trends, original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) are continuing to recommend synthetic food-grade lubricants for their machines due to the performance benefits, such as increased lubrication intervals, better oxidation resistance, and wider operating temperature tolerances. Ben Gamier, vice president of Renewable Lubricants, Hartville, Ohio, says that from a supplier’s perspective, supply availability and equipment performance are two important factors impacting the demand for food-grade lubricants. “Disruptions in the Middle East and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz have put pressure on base-oil availability, including certain lower-viscosity synthetic base stocks,” he says. “That makes planning ahead and communicating with suppliers increasingly important. Customers still need the appropriate food-grade products and reliable performance, even when raw materials become harder to source.” As for the impact sustainability trends are having on the market, Gamier notes that the company is seeing customers approaching these trends from different directions. “Some are primarily interested in environmentally friendly products, particularly biodegradability,” he says. “Larger customers are also looking more closely at the broader sustainability of what they purchase, including renewable content, lubricant consumption and waste. Those are separate considerations. A lubricant’s biodegradability, renewable content and service life each tell a different part of the story. The right choice depends on the application and the customer’s goals.” Lubriplate’s Girard says that sustainability plays a “huge role” when it comes to choosing food-grade lubricants. “Facilities want to use lubricants [that] keep their machinery running as efficiently as possible without sacrificing performance. Because of this, facilities are focusing on choosing synthetic food-grade lubricants,” she explains. “They offer extended service intervals, which significantly decreases overall lubricant waste volume and disposal costs. Some facilities are also choosing to go 100% food-grade because many food-grade lubricants are biodegradable.” As for what is on the horizon for the lubricants market, Girard says that lubricant manufacturers will continue to develop high-performing synthetic lubricants to meet the demands of OEMs and end-users. “Look for more food-grade replacements to MOSH and MOAH mineral oil-based products as well as the elimination of PFAS in lubricants,” she notes. Stonegate Agrees to Sell 300 British Pubs to Heineken for £300 Million

Stonegate vende 300 pubs británicos a Heineken por £300 millones, posicionando a Heineken como el segundo grupo de pubs más grande del Reino Unido. La operación representa una estrategia de integración vertical de Heineken en distribución y punto de venta directo, mercado crítico para bebidas. Para Stonegate, la venta reduce su endeudamiento significativamente. Esta M&A refleja consolidación en el canal on-premise europeo y refuerza la posición competitiva de Heineken en uno de sus mercados clave.

Sazerac raises its Berentzen takeover offer to €6.65 a share

Sazerac elevar su oferta de adquisición por Berentzen a €6,65 por acción, representando un incremento del 19,8% sobre la propuesta original tras eliminar el umbral de aceptación mínima. La oferta permanece abierta hasta el 4 de diciembre. Este movimiento refleja la estrategia de consolidación en la industria de bebidas alcohólicas, fortaleciendo la posición de Sazerac en el mercado europeo de licores destilados, particularmente en el segmento de aguardientes y licores tradicionales alemanes.

Stonegate agrees £300m sale of 300 pubs to Heineken, reports claim

Stonegate vende 300 pubs a Heineken por £300 millones, operación que se completaría en noviembre. Heineken expande su cartera de puntos de venta en Reino Unido mientras Stonegate reduce deuda. Este movimiento refleja una consolidación estratégica en distribución de bebidas alcohólicas a través del canal on-trade, que es crítico para volumen de consumo y visibilidad de marca en el mercado británico.

Texto completo del artículo
According to <i>The Times</i>, the deal could complete as early as Monday (23 November), with the pubs drawn from Stonegate’s 1,034-strong Platinum portfolio of higher-value properties. The sale would leave Stonegate with approximately 4,000 pubs, while proceeds would be used to reduce its outstanding debt and associated interest costs. The reported agreement follows <a href="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/09/21/heineken-in-talks-to-buy-300-stonegate-pubs-for-300m/" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/09/21/heineken-in-talks-to-buy-300-stonegate-pubs-for-300m/">reports in September that Heineken was in talks to acquire around 300 pubs from Stonegate for £300m</a>. At the time, Stonegate said it was exploring options for its Platinum portfolio, including refinancing, a partial sale or a full disposal, but stressed that no decisions had been made. Stonegate, which is owned by private equity firm TDR Capital, has been reviewing options for its Platinum estate as part of a wider strategy to <a href="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/03/02/phil-dixon-explains-why-stonegate-is-seeking-a-cccd/" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/03/02/phil-dixon-explains-why-stonegate-is-seeking-a-cccd/">reduce debt and reshape the business.</a> Other potential buyers, including Bain Capital and Terra Firma, are reportedly continuing to consider acquisitions from the remaining portfolio. The group has also been converting managed pubs into leased and tenanted operations, reducing its exposure to rising operating costs. In its <a href="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2026/09/22/stonegate-profit-grows-as-transformation-strategy-accelerates/" target="_blank" rel="">latest trading update</a>, Stonegate reported adjusted profit of £304m for the 40 weeks to 5 July 2026, compared with £279m in the previous year, while pre-tax losses narrowed from £73m to £53m. For Heineken, the acquisition would significantly expand its Star Pubs & Bars estate, which currently comprises around 2,350 pubs. The addition of approximately 300 sites would take the portfolio to around 2,650, potentially making it the UK’s second-largest pub group ahead of Greene King. The deal would represent a further expansion of Heineken’s pub ownership business, <a href="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2017/08/30/Heineken-completes-acquisition-of-1-900-Punch-pubs/" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2017/08/30/Heineken-completes-acquisition-of-1-900-Punch-pubs/">following its acquisition of around 1,900 Punch pubs in 2017.</a> The reported transaction has yet to be formally confirmed by either company. Heineken declined to comment when approached by <i>The Morning Advertiser (MA)</i>. Stonegate has also been contacted for comment.

Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 9 días · Actualizado 2026-11-20

La semana estuvo dominada por movimientos de M&A y consolidación de portafolios en las grandes multinacionales, con Coca-Cola HBC avanzando en la adquisición de CCBA por USD 2.600 millones, Danone cerrando la compra de Made Group en Australia y Heineken expandiéndose en Brasil. El segmento de bebidas sin alcohol y funcionales marcó la agenda de consumo, con Total Wine reportando crecimiento del 125% en ventas no alcohólicas y múltiples mercados europeos proyectando aceleración del segmento. En Argentina, el contexto macro presentó una dicotomía crítica: pesimismo industrial con expectativas negativas para 2027 frente a un optimismo relativo en el canal supermercadista, mientras Tetra Pak completó una inversión de €30,2 millones en su planta riojana. La presión regulatoria y fiscal sobre bebidas alcohólicas avanzó en Polonia, Francia y Brasil, configurando un entorno de mayor costo regulatorio para la industria a nivel global.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Coca-Cola HBC avanza en la adquisición de CCBA por USD 2.600 millones, la mayor operación de M&A de la semana en el sector de bebidas.
  • Molson Coors emitió un recall de Coors Light, Molson Canadian y Blue Moon en Canadá por contaminación con fragmentos metálicos, afectando tres de sus marcas masivas.
  • PepsiCo cerró un acuerdo de 10 años como proveedor exclusivo de bebidas en más de 700 hoteles Hyatt en América, asegurando volumen institucional a largo plazo.
  • Tetra Pak completó una inversión de €30,2 millones en su planta de La Rioja, Argentina, la señal de inversión en infraestructura local más relevante de la semana.
  • Total Wine reportó ventas de bebidas sin alcohol para adultos superiores a USD 75 millones con crecimiento del 125% entre FY2022 y FY2026, confirmando la consolidación estructural del segmento no alcohólico en retail.
Finanzas

Monster Beverage y Celsius Holdings concentraron el debate de valuación de la semana, con ventas récord en ambas compañías pero presión creciente en márgenes por el alza en costos de aluminio, que ya llevó a Monster a implementar aumentos de precios en EE.UU. Molson Coors enfrentó un recall de Coors Light, Molson Canadian y Blue Moon en Canadá por fragmentos metálicos, evento de reputación con potencial impacto financiero, mientras PepsiCo profundizó su reestructuración con 1.000 despidos en América del Norte en respuesta a la caída sostenida en volúmenes.

Marketing

PepsiCo lanzó etiquetas animadas como iniciativa de innovación en packaging y amplió el alcance de Doritos mediante alianzas con KFC y Subway, buscando diversificar puntos de contacto con el consumidor más allá del canal tradicional. El reporte de tendencias de Bacardi y la edición limitada de Johnnie Walker Blue Label para el Año del Chivo consolidaron la apuesta de las grandes licorerías por experiencias culturales y personalizadas como eje de diferenciación frente al avance de las bebidas sin alcohol.

Supply Chain

El alza en los costos de aluminio se convirtió en el principal driver de ajuste de precios de la semana, con Monster Beverage ejecutando aumentos y Celsius evaluando cambios para 2027, una señal que podría contagiarse a otras categorías de bebidas enlatadas en la región. En el plano regulatorio, Brasil anunció un impuesto a vinos importados desde febrero de 2027 de hasta 11,6%, Polonia firmó aumentos de accisas para el mismo año y Francia aprobó mayor carga fiscal sobre premixes, incrementando la presión sobre la cadena de costos en múltiples mercados.

Ventas / Comercial

Mercado Libre y Carrefour lanzaron entregas de supermercado en 45 minutos en Argentina, intensificando la competencia en el canal de entrega rápida donde Rappi proyecta quintuplicar su volumen en diez años, lo que redefine la ejecución en el último kilómetro para las marcas de consumo masivo. En Brasil, Black Friday desplazó al invierno como la mayor temporada de ventas de vino, mientras que en EE.UU. las ventas de cerveza moderaron su caída en noviembre temprano y los cócteles RTD crecieron 4,1%, señalando resiliencia en segmentos premium y de conveniencia.
