Beyond the health halo: Why functional claims aren’t enough

Las bebidas funcionales enfrentan un desafío crítico: mientras los fabricantes promocionan beneficios como energía, hidratación y salud intestinal, la investigación de NielsenIQ revela que el sabor y la relación precio-valor siguen siendo los factores decisivos para los consumidores. Esta brecha entre promesas funcionales y criterios de compra reales cuestiona la viabilidad de premium pricing en el segmento, forzando a los players a repensar sus estrategias de posicionamiento y justificación de márgenes en un mercado cada vez más competitivo.

Texto completo del artículo
Functional drinks are built around the promise of doing more than simply quenching thirst, with brands increasingly highlighting benefits ranging from energy and hydration to gut health and immunity. But while these added benefits may attract consumer interest, they aren’t necessarily what seals the deal. NielsenIQ (NIQ) research suggests taste and value for money remain the leading considerations when consumers choose functional beverages, raising questions about how brands can justify a premium without compromising on the fundamentals. Despite the growing focus on functional ingredients, consumers’ priorities when choosing these products remain rooted in the fundamentals of a good drink. NIQ data shows that taste and value for money top consumers’ priorities, both cited by 50.1% of respondents, while health and wellness benefits rank lower at 29%. “It’s about price and value for money. That’s what consumers feel are important factors for choosing a functional drink. It’s joint top with taste and flavour options,” says Cerys Littlejohn of NIQ. “The fundamentals need to win first.” While health claims might differentiate a product, the findings suggest they don’t replace the basic expectations consumers have around taste and price. Taste and value remain central to purchasing decisions, but NIQ research suggests consumers also recognise the added value of functional benefits. It found that 51% of consumers would pay more for a soft drink with additional health benefits. “Value doesn’t necessarily translate as cheap. Consumers are happy to pay more because there’s this added benefit or addition into their drink that means it’s worth that cost,” Littlejohn explains. For manufacturers, the opportunity isn’t necessarily to make functional drinks cheaper, but to demonstrate why their additional benefits justify a higher price point. For brands looking to command a premium, convincing consumers to pay more is only part of the challenge. Functional drinks must also deliver on taste if they are to secure repeat purchases. That can be easier said than done. Adding functional ingredients can bring formulation challenges, particularly when it comes to flavour and texture. Whey protein, for example, can contribute to astringency in beverages – a drying or puckering sensation in the mouth, particularly in acidic formulations. Research published in the<a href="https://www.journalofdairyscience.org/article/S0022-0302(20)30391-X/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="" title="https://www.journalofdairyscience.org/article/S0022-0302(20)30391-X/fulltext"> Journal of Dairy Science</a> has examined how whey proteins can interact with saliva to create this sensation, highlighting one of the sensory challenges manufacturers face when developing protein-fortified drinks. For brands, this creates a balancing act: delivering the desired nutritional benefits without compromising the drinking experience. “If it doesn’t taste good, the repeat purchasing won’t happen,” Littlejohn says. “We can’t override the importance of functionality and let that hinder the taste profile of the drink. It needs to still taste good.” Ultimately, functional benefits may give consumers a reason to try a product, but taste is what gives them a reason to come back.

The 3rd Time’s a Charm: Nelson Brothers Reacquire Nelson’s Green Brier Distillery from Constellation Brands

Los hermanos Nelson reacquirieron Nelson's Green Brier Distillery de Constellation Brands, marcando un cambio estratégico en la industria de destilados artesanales. Constellation Brands, durante el boom de bebidas espirituosas post-COVID de principios de los 2020s, había adquirido agresivamente destilerías craft, pero ahora se retira de esta marca. Esta reacquisición refleja la corrección del mercado de bebidas premium después del ciclo especulativo COVID y el retorno de operadores independientes, con implicaciones en la consolidación/desconsolidación de portfolios de bebidas alcohólicas de grandes corporaciones.

Brothers Andy and Charlie Nelson restarted the family’s distilling business that dates back to the 1800s. The original Nelson Greenbrier Distillery business was started by their great-great-great grandfather in 1860. That business was shut down in 1909 when Tennessee voted in Prohibition, a decade before national Prohibition. In 2012, the Nelson brothers rekindled the family business when they started Nelson’s Green Brier Distillery in Nashville, Tennessee . In 2016, Constellation’s venture capital group liked what they saw in the Tennessee based distillery and made a minority investment in the brand. Brothers Andy and Charlie remained active in the business until Charlie departed in 2023 followed by Andy in 2024 after Constellation obtained full ownership. When they departed they were able to purchase the rights to Belle Meade Bourbon and Louisa’s Liqueur under their new umbrella company Belle Meade Spirits. Belle Meade Bourbon is a brand the brothers created when they first started their distillery using sourced bourbon from companies like MGP and Bardstown Bourbon Co. The Nelson brothers have acquired full ownership of the distillery and inventory of Nelson’s Green Brier Tennessee Whiskey and Nelson Brothers Bourbon. The agreement also includes taking over the lease agreement of the 50,000 square-foot distillery, event space and production facility at 1414 Clinton St. in Nashville. “This has been our family business dating back to the 1800s, so to have the opportunity to once again have full ownership of the brand and its storied history is extraordinarily meaningful to us,” said Andy Nelson, co-founder of Nelson’s Green Brier Distillery. “There will certainly be a transition period where we determine next steps for the brand, our portfolio of products and the distillery itself, but our intention is to get us back to our roots of honoring our rich family heritage as quickly as possible by producing premium spirits right here in our own hometown that reflect the craftsmanship, history and passion of the original distillery our great-great-great grandfather started.” Charlie Nelson, co-founder of Nelson’s Green Brier Distillery, echoed his brother’s excitement for the next chapter in the brand’s storied past, adding, “In a city where we continue to see more and more outside companies choose Nashville as part of their expansion plans, it’s so fun to be able to have this opportunity to once again operate Green Brier as a locally owned family business. Iconic bourbon brand continues to bring its “Perfectly Unreasonable” campaign to life through new visual world LORETTO, Ky., November 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ – Maker’s Mark®, the iconic Kentucky bourbon, today revealed its refreshed global brand identity with an updated color palette, typography, photography and graphic elements that take Maker’s Mark’s unmistakable red wax center stage. Beginning today with the launch of the brand’s new website, consumers will experience the new design across digital, social, advertising and retail channels, as well as events and experiences around the world. “My grandparents weren’t thinking about ‘branding’ when they created Maker’s Mark. They cared about a hand-made process in service to flavor and design that honors that vision,” said Rob Samuels, eighth-generation whisky maker and managing director. “That deeply personal approach created some of the most recognizable symbols in bourbon. This new and exceptional work brings that same intention forward honoring what they created and ensuring Maker’s Mark feels as personal and distinctive today as it did from the start.” The new brand identity brings to life the remarkable lengths Maker’s Mark has gone to make its whisky since the beginning. Founders Margie and Bill Samuels Sr. built Maker’s Mark by doing things differently—burning the 170-year-old family recipe to create a more balanced bourbon; choosing their homeplace in outlying Loretto for its water source and proximity to grain growers; and hand-dipping every bottle in signature red wax. That challenger spirit serves as inspiration for the new visual world. Custom typography inspired by Margie’s handwriting illustrates her wit and wisdom, while tactile wax marks created from natural elements found across Star Hill Farm—wheat stalks, oak rings and native flora—connect the design to the people, land and craft behind Maker’s Mark. Developed in partnership with creative agency Turner Duckworth London®, the resulting visual system reimagines the thoughtful, handcrafted sensibility behind the bourbon, bottle and brand in a modern, more minimal way that remains unmistakably Maker’s Mark. “In a world increasingly driven by speed and automation, we still believe some things are worth making slowly, by hand,” said Chloe Lloyd-Jones, vice president of global brand marketing, Maker’s Mark. “We returned to Star Hill Farm, working with natural materials from the land and our signature red wax to create a visual identity that reflects the ‘Perfectly Unreasonable’ spirit behind our bourbon. The result gives us a powerful way to tell our story around the world, while creating experiences that feel personal and relevant in each market.” Elements of the brand identity have already come to life through experiences in the United States and China and out-of-home advertising in Australia and the UK.
