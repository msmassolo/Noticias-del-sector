Vista por área

Briefing para Finanzas

Los principales players globales de bebidas (Coca-Cola, Monster, Constellation Brands, Molson Coors, Keurig Dr Pepper, Pernod Ricard) muestran cotizaciones estables respaldadas por resultados resilientes y dividendos, señal de que el sector mantiene valuaciones defensivas pese al entorno macro global. Deutsche Bank degradó Monster Beverage de Buy a Hold ajustando el precio objetivo a $98, lo que sugiere que el mercado de energy drinks empieza a mostrar señales de techo de valuación tras un rally prolongado. A nivel de riesgo operacional, un ataque cibernético forzó el cierre de la marca láctea de Coca-Cola valuada en USD 4.000 millones, exponiendo vulnerabilidades críticas de ciberseguridad en la industria que deben ser monitoreadas como precedente.

Briefing para Marketing

PepsiCo lanzó Lipton Sparkling, una línea de tés listos para beber con burbujas y sabores cítricos, consolidando la tendencia de bebidas sin alcohol funcionales y con baja graduación en el segmento RTD. Localmente, la cerveza artesanal enfrenta caída de consumo y cambio de hábitos en Argentina, lo que abre una oportunidad para posicionar propuestas de valor más accesibles o bebidas alternativas en ese espacio de consumidor. Pernod Ricard refuerza su apuesta por premiumización y generación de caja como eje estratégico de marca, tendencia que marca el rumbo del segmento de espirituosas premium a nivel global.

Briefing para Supply Chain

Brown-Forman cerró su cooperage histórico en Alabama, lo que podría impactar la disponibilidad de barricas de roble para el segmento de whiskies y espirituosas a mediano plazo, con potencial efecto en costos de importación de estas categorías. El ataque hacker que paralizó una unidad de Coca-Cola por USD 4.000 millones es una alerta directa sobre resiliencia en sistemas de gestión de supply chain y ERP: la interrupción operativa por ciberseguridad es hoy un riesgo tangible de continuidad. La crisis en la cerveza artesanal local implica probable exceso de stock de insumos específicos (lúpulo, malta especial, packaging premium) en proveedores, lo que puede generar oportunidades de negociación de precios.

Briefing para Ventas / Comercial

La contracción del consumo de cerveza artesanal en Argentina refleja un ajuste del shopper hacia opciones de mayor accesibilidad económica, lo que exige revisar la estrategia de precio-valor en el canal on-trade y off-trade para capturar ese trasvase. El crecimiento sostenido de Twisted Tea (Boston Beer) y el dinamismo del segmento RTD a nivel global confirman que estos formatos ganan espacio en góndola y deben ser priorizados en la negociación de espacios con cadenas. La estabilidad de demanda evidenciada en Keurig Dr Pepper y Coca-Cola a nivel internacional sugiere que el canal moderno mantiene volumen, pero la variable precio sigue siendo el principal palanca de ejecución en mercados con presión inflacionaria como Argentina.

3 Key Trends on Display at IFT FIRST 2026

IFT FIRST 2026 en Chicago identificó tres tendencias clave para la industria de alimentos y bebidas: formulaciones que combinan proteína con fibra, proteínas en formatos más claros y ligeros, y reformulaciones clean-label. El evento refleja drivers críticos del mercado incluyendo demanda de usuarios de GLP-1, expectativas de etiquetado limpio y presión regulatoria. Estas tendencias impactan directamente en desarrollo de productos y reformulación de portafolio para competir en segmentos de salud y bienestar en crecimiento.

Texto completo del artículo
CHICAGO – The Institute of Food Technologists recently hosted IFT FIRST 2026, bringing together the global food science community to discuss innovation in an environment shaped by GLP-1 users, protein demand, clean-label expectations, regulatory scrutiny, and the ongoing need to deliver taste, texture, and fulfillment. Three themes stood out on the show floor in the Windy City: protein and fiber being formulated together, protein moving into clearer and lighter formats, and clean-label reformulation shifting from simple ingredient replacement to performance, cost, and scale. Protein remained one of the most visible nutrition callouts at IFT FIRST, but this year, it was often paired with fiber. The combination reflects growing interest in nutrient density, especially as GLP-1 users eat less and seek foods and beverages that can help support satiety, muscle maintenance, hydration, and digestive health. At Ingredion’s booth, the company showcased concepts that combined protein, fiber, texture, and taste. Its Miso Tomato Pie Pasta Crisp delivered 6 grams of protein from VITESSENCE Pea & Rice Isolate 1780 and 5 grams of fiber from NOVELOSE 3490 tapioca-derived modified RS4 food starch per 28-gram serving. The formulation team also incorporated NOVATION Indulge 2940 functional native corn starch as a clean-label co-texturizer to address the brittleness and breakage that can occur in high-protein chips. The Manildra Group highlighted the high-protein, high-fiber message in a Pistachio Swirl Breakfast Bread made with GemPro Line wheat proteins and FiberGem resistant wheat starch with 90% dietary fiber. Sammi Meyers, product application manager at Manildra, said that “fiber is the new protein” and described the protein-fiber pairing as being driven by GLP-1 users, who need more nutrition in everyday eating occasions. Protein has also moved far beyond sports nutrition into mainstream CPG, “with many brands that historically had nothing to do with protein now leveraging protein as the leading claim in some of their innovation efforts,” said Justin Shimek, CEO of Mattson, during an IFT FIRST presentation. Yet consumers remain confused about how much protein they need and often care more about benefits such as fullness, strength, energy, weight management, and healthy aging than technical protein terminology. Protein innovation is also moving into lighter, clearer formats. Instead of only showing up in bars, shakes, powders, and thick dairy drinks, protein appeared in beverages, frozen treats, and translucent snacks. PURIS highlighted ClearP Protein, a hydrolyzed pea protein designed for clear beverage applications, in a strawberry lemonade RTM (ready-to-mix) with 15 grams of protein. Misen Luu, technical marketing manager at PURIS, said the ingredient is plant-based, non-GMO, not a top-nine allergen, and offers functionality similar to whey protein. Other examples reinforced the same direction while also incorporating reduced-sugar positioning. Nura showed Clear Protein Freezer Pops with 8 grams of vegan protein (CLEARTEIN clear rice & pea protein peptides) and 4 grams of fiber (FiberEssence organic prebiotic fiber blend of soluble tapioca fiber, guar fiber, acacia fiber) sweetened with SWEETZEN stevia & Brazzein blend. Foodology by Univar Solutions showcased a high-protein FlavorFuse Fortified Gelatin Shots concept using Leprino Nutrition’s Clear PRO 90 whey protein isolate and Cargill EverSweet Stevia. Cargill offered a Protein + Prebiotic Soda concept combining 5 grams of whey protein isolate and 5 grams of Cargill soluble corn fiber, sweetened with Cargill allulose and ViaTech stevia leaf extract. The trend fits the GLP-1 era, as consumers eating less may look for lighter ways to add protein, fiber, and hydration without the heaviness often associated with traditional protein formats. For brands, the opportunity is to make protein feel more refreshing, convenient, and enjoyable. Clean-label reformulation was another major theme, but the conversation has moved beyond simply removing ingredients. Companies are now focused on whether natural and clean-label solutions can perform at scale while preserving safety, shelf life, taste, texture, color, and cost. Natural colors remained highly visible following increased regulatory and consumer scrutiny of synthetic dyes. Julia Streuli, CEO and co-founder of FUL Foods, said the natural color conversation has evolved since last year’s show. “Last year, most companies were still in discovery and testing mode – exploring which natural ingredients were the right fit for their formulations,” Streuli said. “Now, many have locked in their formulations, so the conversation has shifted from discovery to price and volume.” EXBERRY by GNT also highlighted natural color innovation with “Graze the Gradient: Chef-Created Bites,” a sampling experience featuring plant-based colors in globally inspired snack pairings. Phytolon highlighted precision-fermented natural food colors, including Beetroot Red, with an emphasis on heat and pH stability, supply-chain continuity, and year-round availability. The clean-label conversation also extended beyond colors. A&B Ingredients framed GLP-1 medications as a new pressure point for better-for-you reformulation, highlighting natural preservation systems such as Rowanberry Extract extracted from rowan tree berries as an alternative to synthetic preservatives. WTI emphasized natural, performance-driven preservation systems such as vinegar antimicrobials, rosemary antioxidants, and lemon-based phosphate alternatives. During a Hot Topics Studio panel on natural emulsifiers and preservatives, Amber Beckett, vice president of product management at WTI, noted that replacing synthetic preservatives with natural solutions requires understanding the function those ingredients serve, since they may contribute not only shelf-life protection but also yield, flavor, and quality. Across the show floor, the message was clear: the next phase of better-for-you innovation is not just about adding protein, cutting sugar, or removing artificial ingredients. It is about solving multiple formulation challenges at once.
