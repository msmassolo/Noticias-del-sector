Amante 1530 seeks to take on Aperol in UK

Amante 1530, un amaro italiano con 25% menos azúcar que Aperol, ingresa al mercado UK a través de un acuerdo de distribución con Maverick Drinks. El producto busca posicionarse como alternativa premium en el segmento de aperitivos amargos, capitalizando la tendencia de consumidores hacia bebidas con menor contenido calórico. La expansión sigue a su lanzamiento en EE.UU. en septiembre 2023, indicando una estrategia de crecimiento geográfico en mercados desarrollados clave para la categoría de licores de aperitivo.

Bitter liqueur Amante 1530 seeks to challenge leading apéritif brands in the UK under a new distribution deal with Maverick Drinks. Amante 1530 aperitivo, is described as a ‘new’ take on the classic Italian amaro, that’s ‘less sweet, but not too bitter’. It is produced using all-natural Italian ingredients at Pallini Distillery in Rome, and is said to contain 25% less sugar than the category leader, Aperol. Following its US debut in September 2023, Amante 1530 will now be distributed in the UK by Kent-based Maverick Drinks who will spearhead a hospitality-led entry into the country. The liqueur is made with botanicals including Italian citrus, ginger and honeysuckle, bottled at 15% ABV with a UK RRP of £20.95 (US$28) per 700ml. Amante 1530 is available now from online retailer Master of Malt and in select on-trade locations, including Nobu London and Gold in Notting Hill, where it is the core component in the restaurant’s house Spritz. Hannah Burden-Teh, brand manager at Maverick Drinks, said: “The Spritz occasion has exploded in Britain, and the category has diversified with it. Hugo, limoncello, vermouth Spritzes, they’ve all found an audience. “But the classic Italian bitter Spritz, the one most people actually order, has never been seriously challenged. Amante 1530 is the first brand we’ve seen with the credentials to change that. It’s made by one of the most respected winemakers on the planet, bottled in one of the most historic distilleries in Italy, and the liquid speaks for itself the moment it hits the glass.” Amante 1530 founder and CEO Ana Rosenstein left a career in venture capital to develop her product in collaboration with Italian oenologist Riccardo Cotarella. Rosenstein told The Spirits Business : “You have this behemoth, Campari group, and they’ve built two extraordinary brands but I believed there was room for something else. I wanted to create something that sat in between Aperol and Campari, and was maybe the thing that you graduated to when you had outgrown the Aperol Spritz.” The concept of Amante 1530 was born when Rosenstein was on a trip to Tuscany. “I was sitting there in Tuscany, with all of the people who ended up becoming my original investors, and we looked around to see four or five Spritzes on every table around us. It was this ‘eureka’ moment; the world didn’t look like this 10 years ago. This category has just exploded and there’s one player. “This was just at the moment that Casamigos had come in and transformed Tequila. Aviation had come in and done the same for gin. The aperitivo category was kind of the last man standing, and it was only growing in popularity.” Maverick Drinks will focus first on growing Amante 1530’s presence in the UK on-trade, and Rosenstein confirmed plans to expand into chain retail in the coming years. “In terms of strategy, I spend a lot of time in market,” said Rosenstein. “We hire our own salespeople in every market that we’re in. We’re actually looking to expand the team in London and most of the people we hire are former bartenders. “We don’t really work with a lot of traditional influencers, because that’s not really the brand. There are some influencers who have a lot of value, but in our category it’s the people with expertise that drive the consumption pattern. It’s the bartenders who know this space better than I ever will who are the people I want on board.” This bartender-first approach influenced the design of the Amante 1530 bottle, cap and label, all of which is made in Italy. “I wanted the bottle to look fresh and modern, but also like it had sat on the back bar for generations. Everything about the bottle was designed for both consumers and for bartenders to love it,” Rosenstein added. “The circumference is the size of the average hand, so that makes it really easy for bartenders to hold. Right at happy hour when you’re rushing, the bottle doesn’t fall out of your hand. It has a long neck so that it can be down in the well, so that we can be the house Spritz.” Rosenstein has confirmed the brand will focus on its flagship product for the foreseeable future, with a planned 50ml format due to debut later this year. She has not ruled out new expressions and limited edition seasonal releases in the future. While she has been clear about her intention to take on market leader Campari, Rosenstein is quick to pay tribute to the brands that have helped make the aperitivo category a global success. “I have so much respect for the incumbent. I don’t want to bash their products because they’re beloved for a reason,” she continued. “I normally just ask people to try the liquid on its own, try it on the rocks, maybe add a lemon, see how the flavour opens up. Try it against a whole set of other aperitivos and see what you like best. We find that when people try an Amante Spritz, they never go back.” Amante 1530 isn’t the only brand seeking to challenge Campari Group in the marketplace. Last year, Allora Aperitivo al Limone debuted an out-of-home campaign depicting its signature limoncello Spritz in a sea of recognisable orange Spritzes with text reading ‘Non Per Tutti’ – meaning ‘not for everyone’ in Italian. In the past 12 months, established names including De Kuyper , Cointreau and Gordon’s have all launched expressions aimed at capturing a piece of the lucrative aperitivo market. In May this year, Martini Vermouth published the results of a OnePoll online survey, which found two thirds of respondents across seven European countries preferred Spritz to beer in the summer months. Aperol took the number one spot in the speciality spirits segment of The Brand Champions 2026 , having sold 10 million nine-litre cases in the preceeding year. Its stablemate Campari held the number four position on the list with sales of 4.9m nine-litre cases.

How RTDs are winning over bars

Las bebidas ready-to-drink (RTD) están expandiendo su presencia del retail hacia el canal on-trade (bares y locales gastronómicos), impulsadas por un crecimiento sostenido en un mercado de spirits en contracción. Este movimiento estratégico representa una oportunidad de captura de volumen en canales premium y de experiencia que históricamente fueron dominados por bebidas servidas directamente. Para empresas argentinas de bebidas, entender esta tendencia de penetración on-trade es crítico para evaluar modelos de distribución y portfolio de RTDs, particularmente en el segmento de cócteles pre-elaborados y bebidas spirits premezcladas.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
After conquering supermarket shelves, ready-to-drink brands are making their mark behind the bar. We explore why brands are increasingly being poured in on-trade establishments. *This feature was originally published in the June 2026 issue of The Spirits Business magazine. In the midst of an industry in a contractionary phase, the ready-to-drink (RTD) category is doing something many spirits segments aspire to right now: growing. The sector is no longer riding the coattails of pandemic-induced at-home consumption or the hard seltzer boom in the US. It is establishing itself as a serious contender in the global spirits industry – and demand continues to grow. In IWSR’s RTDs Strategic Study 2025, RTDs were singled out as the “growth category to watch in beverage alcohol”, with RTD volume sales up by 2% in 2024 in 10 of the world’s leading markets (Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, Germany, Japan, Mexico, South Africa, the UK and the US), and by 4% in value. Much of the interest in RTDs has stemmed from its off-trade growth and potential. Convenience, affordability and variety are a key part of the category’s appeal. While overall sales of spirits in the US off-trade fell by volume and value in January 2026, according to NielsenIQ’s (NIQ) Retail Measurement, ‘prepared cocktails’, including RTDs, offset the trend to report 8.8% value growth and 3% volume growth. By comparison, in the UK, the latest data from the Wine and Spirit Trade Association, showed RTDs generated £704 million (US$948m) in the country’s off-trade in 2025. However, growth is not solely coming from retailers. RTDs are increasingly finding their way into the on-trade. So where, and why, is the category finding favour there? Australians are thirsty for RTDs. The category generated AU$2.5 billion (US$1.7bn) in Australia’s bars in 2025 after increasing sales by 12% by value compared with 2024 – up by 35% on 2023, NIQ data showed. RTDs now attract 15 cents in every Australian dollar spent on drinks in the country’s on-trade, up from 12 cents in 2023. “RTDs, especially those on draught/tap, offer an attractive proposition to consumers looking to find value from their drinks when out,” says Tom Graham, NIQ, BevAI Industry, industry insights lead in Australia. “Compared with traditional glass-spirits serves, they are more sessionable and refreshing, and offer an alternative that is closer to the need states’ beer provides. Several factors may continue to support category growth: one, not all venues offer RTDs on tap, so physical availability is lagging behind preference/behaviour, which means increased distribution could drive further growth for the category; two, innovation is continuing.” In Australia, Graham notes how vodka- and neutral-based RTDs are well-positioned to tap into the ongoing consumer preference for lighter and more sessionable drinks. “RTDs continue to resonate strongly with legal-age younger-adult consumers, who are the primary drinkers of the category,” he adds. “Growth has been driven by vodka-based RTDs, including brands such as Hard Rated, Brookvale Union, and Kirin Hyoketsu. Simple, light, refreshing drinks.” Younger legal-drinking-age consumers have also been driving sales of Sazerac-owned Buzzballz , which grew its sales in the UK by 800% in the year ended 6 September 2025, with off-trade sales of £24.6m (US$35.5m). Its popularity looks certain to continue – and the on-trade could play a key role in the spherical brand’s future success. “We see strong opportunity in casual dining, eater-tainment concepts, music venues, and other high-energy accounts where convenience, portability and bold branding resonate with consumers,” says Jess Scheerhorn, president of BuzzBallz. “Operators value the speed of service, consistency and ease of execution the format provides, while the brand’s distinctive packaging and social visibility help generate interest and trial.” British RTD Moth echoes Scheerhorn’s insights, and also notes the eco-friendly appeal Moth RTDs offer. “Sustainability is another growing driver, with aluminium being infinitely recyclable and increasingly important to both venue owners and consumers who are paying closer attention to the environmental impact of their choices,” says Ali Mather, Moth’s head of on-trade. “In the UK, younger LDA [legal-drinking-age] adults plan to drink more RTDs or canned cocktails in the next 12 months, with around two-fifths of 18- to 29-year-olds (39%) and 37% of 30- to 44-year-olds planning to do so ( Bacardi Cocktail Trends Report 2026 ).” Speed, consistency, and sustainability were all contributing factors in Moth’s recent listing with Yo! restaurants throughout the UK. It’s the latest addition to a growing roster of eateries where Moth has secured positions for three of its canned cocktails. “Yo! represented a genuinely exciting opportunity because it sits in a different culinary space to our existing on-trade partners such as Honest Burger and Franco Manca,” explains Mather. “The pairings are a simple way to explore new flavour combinations across the menu, from a classic Negroni alongside nigiri to an Espresso Martini paired with chocolate mochi, there’s a serve for every mood and every course. It’s a high-footfall, consumer-facing environment that puts the brand in front of a broad audience, reinforcing that Moth works across the full spectrum of the on-trade.” While that’s all well and good for food-led venues, what about drinks-led establishments? Why should bars embrace RTDs? How do they compete with existing cocktail menus? For Ingrid Smith, founder of the first coconut water-based RTD cocktail brand Nirvana , demand for RTDs is more selective in bars than restaurants. “Bars still care deeply about craft and presentation, but many are starting to view premium RTDs as a strategic complement rather than a replacement for cocktails,” Smith says. “The best-use cases are high-volume moments, outdoor service, concerts, hotel minibars, rooftops, and event bars. “The data supports what we’re seeing in the market. Beverage Information Group reported that nearly 19% of on-premise operators said RTDs were the category they would expand on menus, just behind Tequila, and RTDs tied with non-alcoholic cocktails and alcohol-free ‘spirits’ as the category generating the most on-premise buzz.” It isn’t just pre-mixed, canned products driving RTD growth and opportunities in the on-trade, however. Ready-to-serve (RTS), draught cocktails are also becoming increasingly common, and popular. At Bar Convent Berlin 2025, De Kuyper unveiled two draught cocktails for the on-trade : Amaretto Sour and Piña Colada, which joined its existing Espresso Martini and Pornstar Martini options. “The on-trade remains very important to us; our De Kuyper Batched and Draught solutions are specifically designed to address speed, consistency and waste reduction needs within hospitality,” notes Marieke Liesting, global marketing director, De Kuyper Royal Distillers. “Our pre-mixed cocktails play a dual role: they unlock new opportunities in the on-trade while also democratising cocktails for at-home consumption via off-trade ready-to-serve and RTD cans. CGA data from the Netherlands shows a clear link here – around 70% of cocktail drinkers enjoy cocktails at home that they first discovered in Horeca [hotels/restaurants/catering] (CGA 2025).” De Kuyper singles out its homeland, the Netherlands, Spain, and the UK as the markets where it is seeing the biggest momentum for its RTD, RTS, and draught cocktails. In the US, vodka-based hard tea Surfside was the fastest-growing alcohol brand in the US off-trade in 2025 after sales soared by 362% on 2024. In March this year, Surfside’s parent company, Stateside Brands, unveiled Super Lyte . The new brand was inspired by classic sports drinks flavours. The non-carbonated, vodka-based beverage is aimed towards Gen Z and Millennial drinkers looking for an RTD that could fit an active lifestyle thanks to its 4.5% ABV strength, zero-sugar recipe and fewer than 90 calories per can. “Innovation is one of the core drivers of our success, and our team works extremely hard to make the best-tasting beverages in the industry,” says Clement Pappas, CEO of Surfside and Super Lyte. “The on-trade RTD space in the US is highly competitive and becoming more crowded as demand continues to grow. Even so, there is significant opportunity for brands that offer true differentiation and strong consumer pull. Success in the on-premise channel today requires more than distribution. Brands need strong velocity, recognisable branding, and authentic cultural relevance. We understand the value of tapping into fandoms and meeting consumers where they are to build local connections and elevate the occasion. Our focus is on investing in marketing, partnerships and cultural moments that deepen consumer connection and keep our brands competitive.” Looking to the next 12-18 months, Nirvana’s Smith anticipates five key developments regarding RTDs in the on-trade. She envisions the category being more widely accepted in the channel, particularly in high-volume and outdoor service settings, while premium, spirits-based RTDs will continue to distance themselves from malt-based products. “Third, we will see more menu integration. RTDs will not just sit in a cooler; they will be positioned as part of a bunch of programmes, patio menus, poolside service, golf carts, hotel amenities, and event packages,” Smith adds. “Fourth, innovation will become more occasion-led. Products will be developed for specific use cases: daytime socialising, lower-ABV sessions, travel, wellness-adjacent occasions and premium convenience. Fifth, the category will face a credibility test.” Ultimately, Smith says RTDs are not going to replace bars’ cocktail menus. If done right and done well, they will expand what a bar can offer. “The mistake is treating RTDs as less than cocktails. The opportunity is positioning them as a modern format for moments where convenience and quality need to work together,” she concludes.

Nichols eyes functional drinks growth with £64m purchase of VITHIT

La adquisición refuerza la estrategia de Nichols (dueña de Vimto) de expandirse en el segmento de bebidas diferenciadas y funcionales, respondiendo a la tendencia de consumo hacia productos de mayor valor nutricional. Esta operación posiciona al grupo para competir en el segmento de bebidas saludables en crecimiento en Europa, particularmente en el mercado UK e Irlanda.

Nichols, which has iconic brand Vimto as part of its portfolio, said that VITHIT is an “excellent strategic fit” and represents a “significant strategic milestone” in its plans to invest in differentiated soft drinks brands. Founded in Dublin in 2001, VITHIT has a portfolio of low-calorie, low-sugar beverages fortified with vitamins and functional ingredients. Its products offer 100% of the Recommended Daily Allowance of eight essential vitamins through a portfolio that spans bottled ready-to-drink beverages, sparkling cans and effervescent products. New York-based importer Helmsman Imports has teamed up with Ankaa Global Logistics, the third-party logistics arm of Southern Glazer’s, to provide warehousing and fulfilment services for its clients. Southern Glazer’s, the largest drinks distributor in the US, launched Ankaa in 2023 with the aim of establishing a premier logistic service provider for the drinks industry. Under the agreement, Helmsman Imports will gain access to Ankaa’s warehouse network, which currently include facilities in Shepherdsville, Kentucky, and Fairfield, California, to store and manage inventory for alcoholic brands in both the domestic and international market. Ankaa brings extensive beverage alcohol supply chain experience to the partnership, including storage and value-added packaging capabilities. Its infrastructure is designed to address the regulatory and operational requirements involved in moving beverage alcohol through the supply chain after products clear customs. The partnership is also intended to address post-production logistics challenges faced by domestic brands expanding across multiple states or moving toward national distribution, particularly once finished products leave co-packers and require storage and fulfilment. Through the arrangement, Helmsman clients will have access to managed warehouse storage, inventory visibility and outbound fulfilment services. The companies are also integrating their systems, allowing inventory data from Ankaa’s facilities to flow into Helmsman’s proprietary business intelligence platform, The Helm. “We built Helmsman because founders need an importer that understands their challenges at every step, not just the paperwork,” said Jeff Diego, founder and CEO of Helmsman Imports. “Fulfilment, storage, and how efficiently a distributor pickup happens are part of that same problem. Those pickups have to be seamless; that’s what actually gets a brand’s product to market on time.” Diego added that Helmman chose to partner with Ankaa as the company understands Helmsman Imports’ storage and fulfilment needs, and it has the facilities and expertise to deliver it at scale. Helmsman operates as a full-service importer of record, handling compliance filings, inventory management, accounts receivable and payable, chargeback processing and Certificate of Label Approval (COLA) submissions through its Helmsman App platform. The Ankaa agreement adds physical warehouse capacity to that infrastructure, combining Helmsman’s compliance and financial services with Ankaa’s logistics and fulfilment capabilities for brands entering or expanding in the US market. “Our mission is to provide seamless vertical integration solutions for beverage alcohol suppliers that are efficient, transparent and differentiated,” added Drew Vermilion, senior vice president of supply chain at Southern Glazer’s Wine and Spirits. “Helmsman’s model as a full-service importer of record is a natural fit for what we’re building at Ankaa.” Southern Glazer’s operates in 47 markets with projected revenue of approximately US$25.5 billion. But weak demand is continuing to impact the US distribution market. Just last month, Southern Glazer’s announced 250 job cuts, alongside an expansion of its use of artificial intelligence, as rival RNDC began major restructuring following the loss of several high-profile supplier contracts including Brown-Forman, Treasury Wine Estates and Pernod Ricard. SGWS said that it is implementing a new way of doing business through a hybrid approach that will combine field sales, inside sales and digital commerce under the same roof. Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 8 días · Actualizado 2026-08-07

La semana estuvo dominada por la avalancha de resultados del Q2 2026, con un panorama mixto: ganadores claros como Monster Beverage, Coca-Cola FEMSA y Ambev, frente a perdedores evidentes como Celsius Holdings, Diageo y Heineken USA. Las presiones en costos de materias primas —especialmente aluminio— y la debilidad en categorías maduras como cerveza mainstream y espirituosas de alta gama marcaron el tono global. A nivel local, el hito de la semana fue la activación del joint venture Arcor-Danone con La Serenísima, que reconfiguró el mapa del negocio lácteo en Argentina. En el plano estratégico, la preocupación por El Niño 2026/27 comenzó a instalarse en la agenda de cadena de suministro de las grandes compañías globales.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Arcor y Danone activaron el 1° de agosto el joint venture de La Serenísima, el movimiento corporativo más relevante del sector de bebidas y lácteos en Argentina en años recientes.
  • Celsius Holdings se desplomó 18% en bolsa tras un miss en Q2, con el quiebre de su modelo de distribución vía PepsiCo como factor central de la caída.
  • Diageo proyectó ventas planas para FY2027 y lanzó un plan de ahorro de USD 1.000M, confirmando la profundidad de la crisis en espirituosas premium globales.
  • Monster Beverage reportó ventas récord en Q2 2026 superando estimaciones, consolidándose como el gran ganador de la semana en energéticas.
  • Molson Coors sufrió un impacto de USD 40M en costos de aluminio por el shock energético en Irán, evidenciando la vulnerabilidad geopolítica de la cadena de packaging del sector cervecero.
Finanzas

Celsius Holdings se desplomó 18% en bolsa tras un miss en Q2 atribuido al deterioro de su relación con PepsiCo como canal de distribución, mientras Diageo recortó guidance a crecimiento plano para FY2027 y anunció un plan de ahorro de USD 1.000 millones ante una caída del 27% en su ganancia operativa. En contraste, Monster Beverage superó previsiones con ventas récord en Q2, Coca-Cola FEMSA creció 4,7% en ingresos con volúmenes históricos en Brasil, y Ambev expandió su lucro neto 24,5% aunque decepcionó en ingresos y EBITDA absolutos.

Marketing

AB InBev consolidó su pivote estratégico en EE.UU. al redirigir inversión publicitaria desde Bud Light hacia Michelob Ultra, reflejando el avance irreversible del segmento premium-light sobre las marcas masivas tradicionales. Hugo Spritz superó a Aperol Spritz en búsquedas de Google UK durante el verano, señal temprana de un posible cambio en la preferencia de cocktails RTD que Campari deberá monitorear de cerca.

Supply Chain

Molson Coors absorbió un impacto de USD 40 millones en costos de aluminio derivado de un shock energético en Irán, contribuyendo a una caída de ganancias del 27,8% en Q2 y poniendo en foco la exposición del sector a la volatilidad geopolítica en insumos de packaging. Las empresas alimentarias globales —con Danone a la cabeza— comenzaron a activar estrategias defensivas ante las proyecciones del fenómeno de El Niño 2026/27, mientras Heineken UK lanzó uno de los programas de cebada regenerativa más grandes de Reino Unido para asegurar su cadena de malta a largo plazo.

Ventas / Comercial

Heineken reportó caídas de volumen en rango de altos dígitos simples en EE.UU. y entre 4%-6% en Brasil durante H1, con la compañía expresando insatisfacción explícita con su desempeño en las Américas; en respuesta, anunció la eliminación de 3.000 posiciones a nivel global para defender márgenes. Ambev discontinuó canales de bajo retorno en no alcohólicos, lo que generó una caída de 4,4% en volumen de esa categoría en Brasil, evidenciando una racionalización deliberada del portafolio de distribución en favor de rentabilidad.
