Modus Brewery’s non-alcoholic brand NORT has announced the release of Australia’s first non-alcoholic bottled stout, launching 13 August. More than nine months in the making, the NORT stout is set to deliver on the flavour, aroma and mouthfeel consumers expect from a full-strength stout, without the alcohol. “Being Australia’s first bottled non-alcoholic stout is pretty exciting,” said Jaz Wearin, Co-Founder and CEO of Modus Brewery. “There are some great non-alcoholic stouts in cans, but we wanted to create an Australian-made option that was available nationally, year-round, in a premium glass format.” At only 83 calories per bottle and zero sugar, the NORT stout leans into consumers’ desire for drinks that are better-for-you. Wearin said, the challenge in the brewery was ensuring the final product still delivered on flavour. “Getting all that body, richness and mouthfeel you expect from a stout without the alcohol doing the heavy lifting is a clever art, particularly when you’re also keeping the sugar at zero and the calories down. “Our Head Brewer, Ian Jones, has done an amazing job with this beer,” she said. A blend of dark specialty malts builds the brew’s coffee, cocoa and roasted flavours, while using a precise technique when working with the mash retains the body and mouthfeel. Control of the brewing and fermentation process then keeps the alcohol, sugar and calories down. According to Endeavour Group’s State of the Hops Report 2026 , stout is now the fastest-growing beer style in the country, while non-alcoholic beer just notched its 11th consecutive year of growth. Speaking with Beer & Brewer , Wearin said the choice to create a non-alcoholic stout was fuelled by the current boom of the beer style, and consumers leaning further into moderation. “Stout is having an incredible renaissance, largely driven by one very famous Irish brewery. They’re an iconic brand and what they’ve done for the category has been phenomenal. But there was a clear gap for an Aussie-made non-alc stout in a 330ml glass bottle that feels premium.” She added: “I’m incredibly proud that we’re Australian-owned, independent and still manufacturing right here in our own brewery. I’m passionate about keeping brewing and manufacturing in Australia, so this felt like a space we absolutely should be in.” Positioned as Australia’s first bottled non-alcoholic stout, Wearin said the bottle itself felt as important as what is inside. “Most of our alcoholic beer goes into cans, but we love non-alcoholic in glass. Cracking the top, the cold glass in your hand, the rim on your lips… those little ceremonial moments make it feel special and like a proper beer occasion. “We’ve always loved to innovate. It’s what makes business and brewing exciting. Having the team and capability to keep creating new things and putting them out into the world is quite the privilege,” she said. Looking ahead to the next 12-18 months, Wearin said she sees Australian consumers continuing to think about what and when they drink, without compromising on flavour or experience. “Moderation is clearly here to stay, but what I find really interesting is that almost half of non-alcoholic beer baskets also contain alcohol. It’s not one or the other. Sometimes I want a beer, sometimes I want a non-alcoholic and sometimes I’ll have both in the same night. “At the same time, the love for stout will continue, but it’s not just about one brand anymore. People genuinely like stout. They’re rediscovering darker, fuller-flavoured beers and wanting a bit more flavour and interest in what they drink.” Six years into the NORT brand, Wearin said she is as excited about the brand today as she was when it first launched. “We started with a pretty simple idea: make non-alcoholic beer that beer drinkers actually want to drink. And that hasn’t changed.” NORT stout is available from 13 August in 330ml bottles, with six-packs available for $19.99 and a case of 24, $59.99.

Berlucchi, the oldest producer of Franciacorta, which is made using the traditional method of secondary fermentation in the bottle, began harvesting Aug. 2 as part of an industrywide effort to preserve grape quality in the face of increasingly extreme weather. Experts say several factors combined to push Franciacorta’s harvest so early this year. A relatively mild winter meant the vines produced buds earlier, while hailstorms damaged some of the yield, lightening the fruit and bringing it to earlier maturation. Even in late July, Berlucchi was targeting an Aug. 10 harvest date, but it quickly became clear that Pinot Nero used for sparkling blends and rosé was ready to be picked, signaled by rapidly rising sugar levels.
