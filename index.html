New beverage launches: From premium whisky to alcohol-free RTDs

El mercado de bebidas responde a tendencias de moderación y consumo premium en casa con nuevos lanzamientos de bebidas alcohólicas y sin alcohol en formato RTD. Tanto players establecidos como startups buscan diferenciación mediante premiumización, enfatizando artesanía, métodos de producción elevados y ediciones limitadas en spirits, con foco en herencia y proveniencia. Esta estrategia refleja la bifurcación del consumo hacia opciones premium e inclusión de alternativas sin alcohol, redefiniendo ocasiones de consumo y segmentación de mercado.

Leer nota original Ver en español
Texto completo del artículo
The latest product launches showcase how brands are responding to evolving consumer occasions, from moderation and alternation trends to premium at-home consumption. Both established players and start-ups are seeking new ways to differentiate in increasingly competitive categories. Premiumisation remains a major theme, particularly in spirits, where producers continue to emphasise craftsmanship, elevated production methods and limited-edition releases. Heritage, provenance and cask innovation are also helping brands justify higher price points. At the same time, flavour remains a key driver of consumer engagement. Seasonal ingredients, dessert-inspired profiles and cocktail influences are appearing across categories as brands look to create excitement and encourage trial. Clapton Craft has partnered with Brighton brewery UnBarred to launch Choco Fix, a milk chocolate stout (4.8% ABV) now available across its retail and hospitality estate. Designed for colder drinking occasions, Choco Fix is brewed with chocolate malts and specialty grains and finished with an in-house cocoa tincture. The result is a stout featuring milk chocolate sweetness balanced with deeper cocoa notes. According to co-founder Will Jack, the launch reflects continued consumer demand for premium craft beer despite ongoing industry challenges, with the retailer’s hybrid off-trade and bar model helping it gather direct customer feedback and identify gaps in its range. Lanzero has entered the US non-alcoholic RTD market with a range of 0.0% canned cocktails designed to be consumed alongside alcoholic drinks rather than as a substitute for them. Founded by former Pernod Ricard executives Juan Manuel Gomez and Jean Del Pino, the New York-based company launched in October with three flavours: Spicy Margarita, Passionfruit Mojito and Smoky Pineapple Fizz. The drinks are available nationwide through the brand’s website and Amazon, as well as selected retail and on-premise accounts in New York City. The launch is built around the growing trend of “zebra striping”, where consumers alternate between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks during a social occasion. According to NIQ data cited by the company, 94% of non-alcoholic beverage buyers also purchase full-strength beer, wine or spirits. Lanzero’s products contain no alcohol, no sugar and between 15 and 20 calories per can, while providing approximately 500mg of electrolytes from sodium, potassium and calcium. Angry Orchard has expanded its seasonal cider offering with the launch of the Mad Chill Variety Pack, a winter-themed mixed pack featuring four flavour variants inspired by cocktail and dessert profiles. The pack combines returning favourite Baked Apple Pie with three other flavours: Stoked Sangria, Angry Old Fashioned and Rad Plum. The launch reflects continued innovation in the flavoured cider segment, with Angry Orchard using familiar cocktail serves and seasonal flavour cues to appeal to consumers seeking variety during the winter months. LALO Tequila has added a new product to their limited-edition High Proof Blanco series with the release of its 2026 expression. The new tequila marks the third annual iteration of the range and is bottled at 48% ABV (96 proof). The 2026 edition uses bagazo fermentation, a traditional technique in which cooked agave fibres remain in the fermentation tanks for 72 hours. LALO says this process allows natural sugars and microbes from the agave to contribute additional texture and flavour development, resulting in a more viscous mouthfeel and a deeper agave character. The finished tequila is described as the sweetest High Proof release to date, with notes of cooked agave, warm spice and subtle buttery flavours alongside an extended finish. The Dalmore has unveiled the fourth and final release in its Cask Curation Series, partnering with Portuguese wine producer Blandy’s Madeira for a collection of three ultra-premium single malt whiskies. The Madeira Cask Edition follows previous releases inspired by sherry, port and French red wine casks, highlighting the distiller’s long-standing focus on multi-cask maturation and exclusive relationships with wine producers. Limited to just 150 sets globally, the collection comprises 30, 34 and 45-year-old whiskies, each initially matured in American white oak ex-bourbon barrels before being finished in different Madeira wine casks. The release underscores the growing role of cask innovation and luxury positioning within the super-premium Scotch whisky sector. Priced at $44,450 per set, The Madeira Cask Edition will be available in selected global markets, including the US.

Se profundiza la caída del consumo masivo: retrocedió un 7,5% anual y todos los canales de venta registraron bajas

El consumo masivo en Argentina registró una caída de 7,5% en volumen durante septiembre-octubre 2026, la peor performance del último año, acelerando la contracción respecto a períodos previos. Todos los canales de venta fueron impactados negativamente según datos de NielsenIQ. Esta contracción profunda afecta directamente la demanda de bebidas y requiere revisión urgente de estrategias comerciales, pricing y portafolio para enfrentar el deterioro del poder adquisitivo del consumidor argentino.

Leer nota original
Texto completo del artículo
El consumo masivo volvió a mostrar señales de deterioro y profundizó su tendencia contractiva en el inicio del último trimestre del año. Durante el bimestre septiembre-octubre de 2026, las ventas en volumen retrocedieron un 7,5% interanual, la mayor caída de los últimos doce meses relevados. El resultado, según el último informe de la Canasta de Consumo elaborado por NielsenIQ (NIQ), representó una aceleración de la contracción respecto del período agosto-septiembre, cuando el indicador había registrado una baja del 5,8%. De esta manera, en el acumulado del año, el consumo exhibió una disminución del 1,8% frente al mismo período de 2025. El deterioro se produjo pese a una leve desaceleración en la variación interanual del precio promedio ponderado de los productos relevados, que pasó del 27,5% al 27,3%. En paralelo, la facturación nominal creció un 16,9%, por debajo del 19,6% registrado en el bimestre anterior. CANASTA NIELSEN medios- OCT-2026.pdf Los datos reflejan un escenario de creciente debilidad de la demanda, con una reducción generalizada de las cantidades vendidas tanto en las distintas familias de productos como en los canales de comercialización. Consumo masivo: las bebidas lideraron la caída y los alimentos básicos tampoco escaparon al deterioro El segmento de bebidas fue el más golpeado durante el último período relevado, con una contracción interanual del 13,8%. A su interior, las ventas de bebidas alcohólicas disminuyeron un 14,5%, mientras que las no alcohólicas retrocedieron un 13,6%. Las aguas minerales saborizadas, las cervezas y las gaseosas se ubicaron entre los productos que más contribuyeron al resultado negativo del rubro. La magnitud de la caída explica buena parte del deterioro general del consumo: de los 7,5 puntos porcentuales de contracción de la canasta, 5,1 puntos correspondieron al segmento de bebidas. Por su parte, el consumo de alimentos disminuyó un 3,2% interanual, con retrocesos del 5% en los productos no básicos, del 2,6% en los básicos y del 0,5% en las golosinas. El segmento de cuidado personal y limpieza también registró una baja del 5%, explicada por una contracción del 4,9% en cosmética y tocador y del 5,3% en los artículos de limpieza del hogar y de la ropa. CANASTA NIELSEN medios- OCT-2026.pdf En el acumulado de 2026, las bebidas presentan una caída del 3,2%, mientras que los productos de cuidado personal y limpieza registran una disminución del 3%. Los alimentos, en tanto, se mantienen sin variaciones respecto del mismo período del año pasado. Kioscos y comercios tradicionales, los más afectados por la retracción de las ventas Uno de los datos más relevantes del informe es que todos los canales de comercialización registraron caídas interanuales durante el bimestre septiembre-octubre, incluso aquellos que habían mostrado una mayor resistencia a lo largo del año. Los kioscos y minimercados encabezaron las bajas, con una contracción del 12,3%, seguidos por los comercios tradicionales, con un retroceso del 11,3%. En las cadenas de supermercados, la disminución alcanzó el 4,8%, mientras que las farmacias y perfumerías registraron una merma del 0,9% y los autoservicios independientes, del 0,3%. Este último dato resulta particularmente significativo, dado que los autoservicios independientes constituyen el único canal que todavía exhibe un balance positivo en el acumulado del año, con un crecimiento del 3,5%. En contraste, las cadenas de supermercados acumulan una caída del 3,4%; los kioscos y minimercados, del 3,3%; los comercios tradicionales, del 1,7%; y las farmacias y perfumerías, del 1,3%. Qué productos registraron el peor desempeño El relevamiento de NielsenIQ también identificó las categorías de productos con mayor y menor dinamismo durante los últimos dos meses. Dentro del segmento de alimentos, las galletitas de cereales, los postres, las gelatinas, las leches vegetales y los preparados en polvo para helados se ubicaron entre las categorías de peor desempeño. En contrapartida, los alimentos infantiles, las sopas, las leches en polvo y las harinas figuraron entre los productos que mostraron una mejor evolución relativa. En bebidas, los jugos isotónicos, las aguas saborizadas, los jugos en polvo, los aperitivos y el whisky estuvieron entre las categorías más rezagadas. Por el contrario, la yerba mate, el té, las aguas minerales puras y las bebidas energizantes exhibieron un comportamiento relativamente más favorable. En materia de higiene personal, los desodorantes corporales y los tratamientos capilares se encontraron entre los productos de menor dinamismo.

The fourth quarter is the busiest production period of the year for food and beverage manufacturers, and one of the hardest to plan. Brand loyalty keeps declining, so commercial teams turn to limited-time offers (LTOs), product variations, seasonal packaging and promotional campaigns to win consumer attention. These tactics lift short-term sales. They also put heavy pressure on the supply chain behind them. For teams running food processing, packaging and supply chain operations, the holiday rush adds complexity at every step. When promotions ignore what the plant floor can deliver, manufacturers can end up with good product in event-specific packaging that has no buyer once the season ends. Repackaging or destroying it is costly, especially at distribution centres without packaging lines and it cuts into margin. The root cause of these margin leaks is a fundamental disconnect between commercial ambition and supply chain reality. Food and beverage planning has to account for fluctuating agricultural yields, packaging line changeovers, cold-chain temperature limits and shelf-life constraints. If commercial planning assumes unlimited capacity or easy replenishment, the result is usually excess safety stock, waste, and weaker service levels. Additionally, retailers also set many of the conditions manufacturers work under. A retailer may push its own private-label products when they earn better margins. Replenishment then becomes a balancing act: oversupplying stores with perishable promotional products brings financial penalties, while undersupplying leads to stockouts, lost sales, and a weaker brand image with retailers and consumers alike. Finally, the proliferation of SKUs makes forecasting harder still. Regional preferences, labelling requirements, allergen declarations and new recycling programs all multiply the variants to plan. If a promotion succeeds beyond expectations, manufacturers may have to pay a premium for outside sourcing or subcontracting, which can erase much of the campaign’s profit. Without a shared view across commercial and operations, teams end up adjusting schedules by hand and expediting freight to keep production running. To eliminate post-holiday waste and protect margins, forward-thinking companies are moving to AI-enabled supply chain orchestration. At the centre of it all is the supply chain 'digital twin.' Think of a digital twin as a living mirror of your end-to-end operations, one that continuously reflects real-world constraints and conditions. That includes the nitty-gritty, SKU-level details: bills of materials, bulk production capacities, packaging line availability and exact shelf-life expiration tracking. When planning runs through this single, reality-based view, your sales and supply teams are finally looking at the same current picture of what the business can actually do. With this digital twin technology in place, planners can run 'what-if' scenarios before putting capital behind a seasonal launch or a major LTO. The simulations show right away how a promotion would affect raw material needs, factory capacity, transportation logistics, and inventory holding costs. Say a demand scenario looks profitable on paper, but it would bottleneck a particular packaging line or break a minimum remaining shelf-life requirement. The twin flags the risk, so your team can adjust the timing or volume of the promotion before it ever hits the floor. Modern supply chain planning tools use continuous AI sensing to help supply chains shift from reactive to predictive. Decision-centric AI agents keep watch around the clock. They catch emerging risks, like a sudden surge in demand for a seasonal product or a supply crunch on key ingredients and then hand planners a focused set of optimised options. This kind of optimisation takes a lot of the daily complexity off a retailer’s plate. It cuts changeover frequency and asset idle time during manufacturing campaigns, optimises inventory across multi-level distribution networks, and schedules replenishment to make the most of capacity while hitting target service levels. Environmental costs and greenhouse gas emission rates can also be built into the optimisation, so companies can meet sustainability goals without sacrificing profitability. And because the AI handles those intricate calculations on its own, planners can spend their time on strategic, high-value decisions instead of manual data entry. There are several examples of major food and beverage brands that are actively utilising AI-enabled supply chain planning solutions to stabilise operations, manage volatility, and standardise complex multi-site networks that can be learned from: • Preventing waste and stockouts (Kraft Heinz): Kraft Heinz set out to build an intelligent, sustainable value chain, and brought its data and processes together from farm to table. With AI optimisation, the company cut physical waste by roughly five percent and reduced total inventory by about $90 million year-over-year. Today, more than 80% of its inventory stays within defined target limits, which means much more reliable replenishment for retail and foodservice partners. • Managing agricultural volatility (JDE Peet’s): C offee roasters deal with very long lead times that stretch all the way back to origin, which leaves them exposed to commodity price swings and global harvest disruptions. JDE Peet’s put a fully integrated planning solution in place to manage that complex sourcing. The result: lower storage costs and service levels protected against upstream supply shocks. • Standardising multi-site operations (Duvel Moortgat): Brewers face tight seasonal capacity and excise-driven regional differences. To tackle that, Duvel Moortgat recently standardised its demand and operational planning across three Belgian breweries using advanced digital planning solutions. By moving away from fragmented, site-specific practices, the brewer has optimised its capacity utilisation and significantly reduced changeovers on the floor, all while keeping its market-leading service levels. For F&B manufacturers, the Q4 promotional push doesn’t have to mean squeezed margins and chaos on the production floor. The old approach, where plans fall apart the moment they run into the physical limits of the shelf or the packaging line, just doesn’t hold up anymore in a market defined by tight margins, complex product portfolios, and shifting consumer behaviour. By embracing AI orchestration and building a centralised, reality-based digital twin, F&B manufacturers can bring their commercial ambitions and operational capabilities into lasting alignment. The payoff is simple: when the holiday rush arrives, your supply chain isn’t scrambling to react. It’s executing a highly optimised, highly profitable plan.
