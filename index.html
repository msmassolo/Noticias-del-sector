New beverage launches: From premium whisky to alcohol-free RTDs

Marcas de bebidas alcohólicas y sin alcohol lanzan nuevos productos dirigidos a ocasiones de consumo en evolución, enfatizando premiumización y diferenciación. Los productores destacan artesanía, métodos de producción elevados y ediciones limitadas en spirits, mientras crece la demanda de RTDs sin alcohol y opciones de moderación. Esta tendencia refleja cómo la industria se adapta a cambios en patrones de consumo del hogar y captura segmentos de mayor valor agregado en un mercado cada vez más competitivo.

Texto completo del artículo
The latest product launches showcase how brands are responding to evolving consumer occasions, from moderation and alternation trends to premium at-home consumption. Both established players and start-ups are seeking new ways to differentiate in increasingly competitive categories. Premiumisation remains a major theme, particularly in spirits, where producers continue to emphasise craftsmanship, elevated production methods and limited-edition releases. Heritage, provenance and cask innovation are also helping brands justify higher price points. At the same time, flavour remains a key driver of consumer engagement. Seasonal ingredients, dessert-inspired profiles and cocktail influences are appearing across categories as brands look to create excitement and encourage trial. Clapton Craft has partnered with Brighton brewery UnBarred to launch Choco Fix, a milk chocolate stout (4.8% ABV) now available across its retail and hospitality estate. Designed for colder drinking occasions, Choco Fix is brewed with chocolate malts and specialty grains and finished with an in-house cocoa tincture. The result is a stout featuring milk chocolate sweetness balanced with deeper cocoa notes. According to co-founder Will Jack, the launch reflects continued consumer demand for premium craft beer despite ongoing industry challenges, with the retailer’s hybrid off-trade and bar model helping it gather direct customer feedback and identify gaps in its range. Lanzero has entered the US non-alcoholic RTD market with a range of 0.0% canned cocktails designed to be consumed alongside alcoholic drinks rather than as a substitute for them. Founded by former Pernod Ricard executives Juan Manuel Gomez and Jean Del Pino, the New York-based company launched in October with three flavours: Spicy Margarita, Passionfruit Mojito and Smoky Pineapple Fizz. The drinks are available nationwide through the brand’s website and Amazon, as well as selected retail and on-premise accounts in New York City. The launch is built around the growing trend of “zebra striping”, where consumers alternate between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks during a social occasion. According to NIQ data cited by the company, 94% of non-alcoholic beverage buyers also purchase full-strength beer, wine or spirits. Lanzero’s products contain no alcohol, no sugar and between 15 and 20 calories per can, while providing approximately 500mg of electrolytes from sodium, potassium and calcium. Angry Orchard has expanded its seasonal cider offering with the launch of the Mad Chill Variety Pack, a winter-themed mixed pack featuring four flavour variants inspired by cocktail and dessert profiles. The pack combines returning favourite Baked Apple Pie with three other flavours: Stoked Sangria, Angry Old Fashioned and Rad Plum. The launch reflects continued innovation in the flavoured cider segment, with Angry Orchard using familiar cocktail serves and seasonal flavour cues to appeal to consumers seeking variety during the winter months. LALO Tequila has added a new product to their limited-edition High Proof Blanco series with the release of its 2026 expression. The new tequila marks the third annual iteration of the range and is bottled at 48% ABV (96 proof). The 2026 edition uses bagazo fermentation, a traditional technique in which cooked agave fibres remain in the fermentation tanks for 72 hours. LALO says this process allows natural sugars and microbes from the agave to contribute additional texture and flavour development, resulting in a more viscous mouthfeel and a deeper agave character. The finished tequila is described as the sweetest High Proof release to date, with notes of cooked agave, warm spice and subtle buttery flavours alongside an extended finish. The Dalmore has unveiled the fourth and final release in its Cask Curation Series, partnering with Portuguese wine producer Blandy’s Madeira for a collection of three ultra-premium single malt whiskies. The Madeira Cask Edition follows previous releases inspired by sherry, port and French red wine casks, highlighting the distiller’s long-standing focus on multi-cask maturation and exclusive relationships with wine producers. Limited to just 150 sets globally, the collection comprises 30, 34 and 45-year-old whiskies, each initially matured in American white oak ex-bourbon barrels before being finished in different Madeira wine casks. The release underscores the growing role of cask innovation and luxury positioning within the super-premium Scotch whisky sector. Priced at $44,450 per set, The Madeira Cask Edition will be available in selected global markets, including the US.

Texto completo del artículo
Sign up to FoodBev and unlock more insights from the international food and beverage industry. Subscribers have access to webinars, newsletters, publications and more... The fourth quarter is the busiest production period of the year for food and beverage manufacturers, and one of the hardest to plan. Brand loyalty keeps declining, so commercial teams turn to limited-time offers (LTOs), product variations, seasonal packaging and promotional campaigns to win consumer attention. These tactics lift short-term sales. They also put heavy pressure on the supply chain behind them. For teams running food processing, packaging and supply chain operations, the holiday rush adds complexity at every step. When promotions ignore what the plant floor can deliver, manufacturers can end up with good product in event-specific packaging that has no buyer once the season ends. Repackaging or destroying it is costly, especially at distribution centres without packaging lines and it cuts into margin. The root cause of these margin leaks is a fundamental disconnect between commercial ambition and supply chain reality. Food and beverage planning has to account for fluctuating agricultural yields, packaging line changeovers, cold-chain temperature limits and shelf-life constraints. If commercial planning assumes unlimited capacity or easy replenishment, the result is usually excess safety stock, waste, and weaker service levels. Additionally, retailers also set many of the conditions manufacturers work under. A retailer may push its own private-label products when they earn better margins. Replenishment then becomes a balancing act: oversupplying stores with perishable promotional products brings financial penalties, while undersupplying leads to stockouts, lost sales, and a weaker brand image with retailers and consumers alike. Finally, the proliferation of SKUs makes forecasting harder still. Regional preferences, labelling requirements, allergen declarations and new recycling programs all multiply the variants to plan. If a promotion succeeds beyond expectations, manufacturers may have to pay a premium for outside sourcing or subcontracting, which can erase much of the campaign’s profit. Without a shared view across commercial and operations, teams end up adjusting schedules by hand and expediting freight to keep production running. To eliminate post-holiday waste and protect margins, forward-thinking companies are moving to AI-enabled supply chain orchestration. At the centre of it all is the supply chain 'digital twin.' Think of a digital twin as a living mirror of your end-to-end operations, one that continuously reflects real-world constraints and conditions. That includes the nitty-gritty, SKU-level details: bills of materials, bulk production capacities, packaging line availability and exact shelf-life expiration tracking. When planning runs through this single, reality-based view, your sales and supply teams are finally looking at the same current picture of what the business can actually do. With this digital twin technology in place, planners can run 'what-if' scenarios before putting capital behind a seasonal launch or a major LTO. The simulations show right away how a promotion would affect raw material needs, factory capacity, transportation logistics, and inventory holding costs. Say a demand scenario looks profitable on paper, but it would bottleneck a particular packaging line or break a minimum remaining shelf-life requirement. The twin flags the risk, so your team can adjust the timing or volume of the promotion before it ever hits the floor. Modern supply chain planning tools use continuous AI sensing to help supply chains shift from reactive to predictive. Decision-centric AI agents keep watch around the clock. They catch emerging risks, like a sudden surge in demand for a seasonal product or a supply crunch on key ingredients and then hand planners a focused set of optimised options. This kind of optimisation takes a lot of the daily complexity off a retailer’s plate. It cuts changeover frequency and asset idle time during manufacturing campaigns, optimises inventory across multi-level distribution networks, and schedules replenishment to make the most of capacity while hitting target service levels. Environmental costs and greenhouse gas emission rates can also be built into the optimisation, so companies can meet sustainability goals without sacrificing profitability. And because the AI handles those intricate calculations on its own, planners can spend their time on strategic, high-value decisions instead of manual data entry. Lo más importante de los últimos días Ver resumen del período · 9 días · Actualizado 2026-11-27

La semana estuvo dominada por movimientos de M&A a escala global, con Heineken expandiendo su red de pubs en el Reino Unido y un embotellador de Coca-Cola consolidando su posición en Sudáfrica. El auge de bebidas sin alcohol —cerveza no alcohólica, funcionales y RTD— emergió como la tendencia estructural más consistente en mercados maduros. En Argentina, el consumo en supermercados acumuló nueve meses de caída aunque octubre mostró una primera señal de recuperación. La presión regulatoria y fiscal sobre el sector se intensificó simultáneamente en Europa, EE.UU. y América Latina.

Top 5 eventos del período

  • Heineken UK adquirió 304 pubs de Stonegate Group, expandiendo su red Star Pubs a más de 2.600 locales y reforzando su integración vertical en el canal on-trade británico.
  • Un embotellador gigante de Coca-Cola adquirió una empresa sudafricana por aproximadamente USD 2.400 millones, convirtiéndose en el segundo mayor embotellador del mundo, mientras la UE abrió una investigación antimonopolio contra otro embotellador de la marca por restricciones en ventas transfronterizas.
  • Rémy Cointreau elevó su guidance anual tras crecer 16,6% en beneficio operativo en el primer semestre, pero anunció recortes de entre el 12% y el 15% de su plantilla global como medida estructural de eficiencia.
  • PepsiCo aseguró un contrato de suministro exclusivo de 10 años con Hyatt en más de 700 hoteles de EE.UU., ampliando su presencia en el canal hotelero frente a Coca-Cola.
  • El consumo masivo en Argentina registró su primer alza interanual en octubre tras nueve meses consecutivos de caída en supermercados, con el canal mayorista liderando la recuperación.
Finanzas

Rémy Cointreau sorprendió positivamente al elevar su guidance anual tras un crecimiento operativo del 16,6% en el primer semestre, aunque anunció recortes de personal del 12-15% para sostener márgenes. Ambev reportó el 3T26 con márgenes bajo presión por costos elevados, mientras Constellation Brands vio caer sus acciones por ventas de insiders y señales cautelosas en su segmento cervecero.

Marketing

PepsiCo cerró un acuerdo de suministro exclusivo de 10 años con Hyatt para más de 700 hoteles en EE.UU., consolidando un canal estratégico de consumo fuera del hogar. Quilmes apuesta al segmento sin alcohol en Argentina y Grupo Modelo reportó un crecimiento del 30% en volumen de cerveza no alcohólica en México durante el Q3, confirmando la aceleración de esta categoría en la región.

Supply Chain

Monster Beverage implementó aumentos de precios en EE.UU. por el alza en costos de aluminio, mientras Celsius evalúa cambios similares en su estrategia de pricing. El gobierno británico confirmó las tarifas EPR de responsabilidad extendida del productor para envases 2026-2027, generando fuerte rechazo del sector hotelero y gastronómico por su impacto en costos operativos.

Ventas / Comercial

En Argentina, las ventas en supermercados cayeron 3% en septiembre acumulando nueve meses consecutivos en rojo, aunque el canal mayorista y shoppings repuntaron; octubre mostró el primer incremento interanual del consumo masivo del año (+9%). A nivel global, los RTD a base de spirits crecieron 23% en canales NielsenIQ de EE.UU. y Total Wine superó los USD 75M en ventas de bebidas no alcohólicas para adultos, con un crecimiento del 125% en cuatro años.
