Texto completo del artículo
The diary giant conducted two studies with Ipsos. The first examined patterns and motivations related to the consumption of plant-based dairy alternatives and the second assessed the perceived overall benefits of plant-based drinks. The worldwide survey of perception covered 42,000 adults, aged between were 18–65 years old in 14 different countries - USA, Canada, UK, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, France, Poland, Hungary, Sweden, Mexico and Japan. More than half of respondents (55%) reported consuming plant-based foods, including drinks and yogurt alternatives. Among these consumers 42% were frequent consumers at once a week and 29% were infrequent consumers (once per month or past use). When they were asked about their motivations, health came out on top (42%) followed by taste (35%), naturalness (34%) and digestive comfort (32%), a driver that consistently stood out across countries and consumer groups. Consumers who selected “health” as the key reason for choosing dairy alternatives were more prevalent in Japan (59%), UK (50%), Spain (49%), USA (49%), France (45%) and Hungary (45%) compared to those from other countries. These consumers were significantly more likely to be aged 45 - 65 years and were more likely to be female. Among consumers who focused specifically on digestion, 35% of them perceived plant‑based products as “not heavy,” while 27% highlighted the fact that they are “naturally lactose‑free,” alongside other digestion‑related benefits. Among consumers of plant-based alternatives 61% reported consuming yogurt alternatives, 59% plant-based drinks while 32% consumed plant-based desserts. In the USA and Canada, 47% reported using alternative creamers and 40% plant-based ice cream. Gen Z and Millenials in the study showed the highest consumption of plant-based products, with health and wellbeing being growing priorities. The second survey of 2,500 consumers in the USA, the UK and Germany found that 92% agreed the plant-based drinks are easy to digest and 88% said they are light on the stomach. In the UK and Germany, plant-based drinks were mostly consumed with cereals (36%), in coffee (33%), with only 20% reportedly consuming them plain. Oat drinks were the most consumed (57%), particularly in the UK and Germany (50% and 76% respectively) as compared with 31% in the USA. In contrast, almond drinks were the most popular in the USA (58%).

Heineken, una de las principales cerveceras del mundo, consideró que la recuperación del mercado de la cerveza en México todavía es “frágil” y no hay certeza de que se mantenga en los próximos meses, a pesar de que ha mostrado señales de recuperación. Harald van den Broek, director de Finanzas de la empresa, declaró que el desempeño de México y Brasil mejoró entre julio y septiembre de este año respecto de los periodos anteriores, aunque reconoció que las condiciones económicas siguen representando un desafío para el consumo. “Me ha sorprendido un poco la resistencia del mercado de la cerveza en el tercer trimestre, y me alegró mucho ver que tanto México como Brasil estaban volviendo al crecimiento”, dijo en conferencia con motivo de la presentación de sus resultados del tercer trimestre. Uno de los factores que contribuyeron a su desempeño fue una mejor ejecución en los puntos de venta, sobre todo en su red de tiendas SIX y con sus socios del canal tradicional (tienditas de la esquina o de conveniencia). No obstante, consideró que aún es prematuro hablar de un cambio de tendencia hacia un crecimiento positivo y sostenido de la categoría, ya que de acuerdo con las conversaciones que ha tenido con los equipos de México y Brasil, las condiciones de ambos mercados siguen siendo frágiles. La posición de la cervecera neerlandesa responde, entre otros factores, al entorno macroeconómico, las altas tasas de interés, la incertidumbre política y la dificultad para anticipar el comportamiento de la economía estadounidense, cuyos efectos también impactan a México. Harald van den Broek explicó que estos factores llevaron a la compañía a mantener una perspectiva más cautelosa para la región de las Américas, que incluye al mercado mexicano, desde el inicio del año. “Así que así es como lo veo frágil, pero estamos haciendo lo correcto. En términos de la perspectiva de costos, creo que hemos encontrado el equilibrio adecuado entre invertir en asequibilidad, pero también asegurarnos de que tomamos precios medidos, particularmente a través de la gestión de la mezcla y, llamémoslo, precios no directos visibles”, subrayó. En este sentido, explicó que Heineken trabaja para mantener la asequibilidad de sus productos sin recurrir a reducciones directas de precios. “Estoy satisfecho con algún nivel de progreso de cuota de mercado, pero aún no donde necesitamos que esté. The Coca-Cola system is investing $1.22 million in a water security and ecosystem restoration project in Namibia, as the country faces recurring droughts and increasing pressure on its water resources. The 'Gam-i Swakop Basin Resilience Project' will focus on strengthening water security in the Swakop River Basin, an important water source for Windhoek and surrounding communities. Implemented in partnership with the Global Water Challenge (GWC) and WWF Namibia, the initiative will use nature-based solutions to improve water availability and support sustainable management of the river basin. Planned activities include the construction of two sand storage dams, the restoration of 235 hectares of land through the removal of invasive alien plants, and the planting of 750 trees in river buffer zones and degraded areas within Windhoek's informal settlements. The project will also involve drilling two boreholes to support groundwater recharge in the Windhoek aquifer. A water resilience action plan has also been developed as part of the initiative. Namibia faces significant water security challenges, particularly in and around Windhoek, where low and unpredictable rainfall and high evaporation rates put pressure on available resources. The investment forms part of Coca-Cola's Africa Water Stewardship Initiative, launched in 2024. The programme represents an investment of nearly $25 million to address water-related challenges across 20 African countries by 2030.
