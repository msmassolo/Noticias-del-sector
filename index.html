Lucro trimestral da Coca-Cola sobe 16% na comparação com 2025

Coca-Cola reportó ganancias de USD 4,42 mil millones en Q2 2026, crecimiento del 16% interanual, con ingresos de USD 13,3 mil millones (+7% YoY). A pesar de un entorno de consumo desafiante, la compañía logró expandir márgenes y generar crecimiento de ingresos y utilidades, según el director Henrique Braun. Este desempeño refleja la capacidad del gigante global de mantener rentabilidad mediante gestión de portafolio de marcas en contextos de presión de demanda, con implicancias relevantes para estrategia de precios, mix de productos y eficiencia operativa en mercados emergentes como Argentina.

Texto completo del artículo
A Coca-Cola teve lucro de US$ 4,42 bilhões no segundo trimestre, alta de 16% na comparação anual. A companhia registrou receitas de US$ 13,3 bilhões entre abril e junho, crescimento de 7% sobre o mesmo período de 2025. “Apesar de ainda enxergarmos um cenário de consumo desafiador, usamos nossas marcas para gerar valor e entregar crescimento de receitas e lucro, diz o brasileiro Henrique Braun, diretor-presidente da Coca-Cola, em nota. A companhia destaca que o aumento de seu faturamento ao longo do segundo trimestre, que chegou a 6% em termos orgânicos, foi impulsionado por uma alta de 4% nos volumes e 2% em mudanças de mix e preços. Na América Latina, incluindo o Brasil, as receitas aumentaram 16%, afirma a Coca-Cola. Em termos orgânicos, sem efeitos cambiais, o crescimento foi de 5%, incluindo alta de 1% nos volumes e 3% no mix e preços. Com os resultados de segundo trimestre, a companhia alterou suas estimativas para o ano, agora esperando uma alta de 5% nas receitas orgânicas, contra aumento de 4% e 5% anteriormente, e aumento no lucro por ação ajustado de 9% a 10%.

Spirits sales up 3% in LVMH H1 as Cognac rebounds

LVMH reportó un crecimiento del 3% en ventas de espíritus en el primer semestre de 2026, alcanzando €1.17 mil millones, con Hennessy Cognac liderando la recuperación en China. El segmento de vinos y espíritus en conjunto creció un 5% a €2.59 mil millones, sosteniendo la tracción del trimestre anterior. La reactivación del mercado chino de cognac es estratégicamente crítica dado que China representa el principal mercado de lujo global y el comportamiento de LVMH señala tendencias de demanda en premium spirits que impactan competencia regional y pricing power en la industria.

Texto completo del artículo
French firm LVMH saw organic spirits sales rise by 3% for the 2026 first half (H1) as Hennessy Cognac continued its recovery in China. For the first six months of 2026, wine and spirits sales totalled €2.59 billion (US$3bn) for LVMH – an organic rise of 5%. It followed a 5% rise in its first quarter (Q1) for wine and spirits collectively, with the latter also up by 5%. Spirits sales in H1 reached €1.17bn (US$1.33bn), with Hennessy Cognac highlighted for continuing its momentum in China post-Chinese New Year. In LVMH’s H1 2025 results, spirits plunged by 15% , which the firm said was in part to ‘weak’ demand for the Cognac brand in China. China’s anti-dumping investigation into EU brandy imports finished in June 2025 , with small relief granted to some large Cognac producers, including Hennessy. LVMH’s Cognac sales volumes for the first half of 2026 reached 38.4m bottles, up from 37.1m year on year. Hennessy’s Chinese New Year limited editions were said to have enjoyed ‘major success’ in China and other Asian markets. In addition, the Cognac brand has been a partner of Bad Bunny’s Debí Tirar Más Fotos world tour and launched a ready-to-serve (RTS) Very Special Cocktails range in the US. Hennessy is the world’s leading Cognac brand according to The Brand Champions 2026 report. It sold 5.8 million nine-litre case sales in 2025 (down by 7.6% on 2024). As part of its spirits recovery, LVMH also highlighted ‘good performances’ and ‘on-going innovation’ for Scotch whisky Glenmorangie and Belvedere Vodka. Other spirits volumes for LVMH hit 10.4m bottles in H1, up from 9.6m year on year. Other brands in the company’s spirits portfolio include Scotch whisky Ardbeg , Volcan de mi Tierra Tequila , Eminente rum and American whiskey Sir Davis . Champagne and wine was up by 7% organically in the first half of 2026, led by Champagne’s recovery in Europe and Japan. Profit from recurring operations for wine and spirits also increased by 11% to €582 million (US$661m) during the first half of the year. For the months ahead, LVMH said its wine and spirits houses will ‘remain focused on keeping their costs under control and continuing to invest in enhancing the desirability of their brands to drive consumer demand’. The company’s total revenue for H1 was €38.6bn (US$43.8bn), up by 2% organically. Regionally, LVMH said the US had a good first half of the year, while Asia saw ‘strong growth’ (excluding Japan). Japan was in growth for H1, and Europe also showed ‘good resilience’. LVMH also noted that it had ‘maintained its innovative momentum and remained very solid in a geopolitical and economic environment that remained disrupted’, which has been heightened by the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. Bernard Arnault, chairman and CEO of LVMH, said the business “demonstrated its solidity and effective strategy”. He continued: “Our maisons – which remained focused on ensuring the utmost quality in our products, and several of which are pursuing their creative renewal – continued to inspire dreams and enhance their desirability.” “The recovery in Champagne and Cognac also contributed to this excellent momentum,” he added.

Coca-Cola World Cup marketing effort scores bigger-than-expected rewards

Coca-Cola superó expectativas de mercado en Q2 con crecimiento de ingresos netos del 7% ($13.4bn) impulsado por campaña de marketing global vinculada al Mundial de Fútbol. Powerade logró visibilidad estratégica al patrocinar los descansos de hidratación en cada partido, generando incremento de volumen del 5% y crecimiento orgánico del 6% versus pronóstico analista de 3.5%. La marca madre de Coca-Cola fue driver clave en Europa, demostrando efectividad de activaciones deportivas masivas para captura de market share y penetración de portafolio en mercados estratégicos.

Texto completo del artículo
Coca-Cola’s eponymous brand was a key driver of volume growth in Europe Coca-Cola has smashed market expectations in its second quarter thanks to a marketing blitz ahead of the recent World Cup, where Powerade sponsored the controversial hydration break in every match. The drinks behemoth reported a 7% jump in net revenues to $13.4bn (£11.5bn) in the three months, as a 5% rise in volumes powered a 6% increase in organic sales. Analysts had predicted organic growth of 3.5%. The group said the Fifa World Cup had provided a “unique opportunity” to activate a marketing campaign “at unprecedented scale” across more than 180 markets. Before the start of the tournament, the company’s ‘Trophy Tour’ made more than 70 stops across about 30 markets, reaching in excess of 700,000 fans. Coke also spanned more than 20 million retail outlets with experiential activations and customer partnerships, while it generated more than 60 billion digital impressions and nine billion views across social media platforms, supported by an army of 2,500 content creators. It helped trademark Coca‑Cola become the number one brand by share of voice during the tournament. Coke said its World Cup campaign contributed to a portion of 5% volume growth for trademark Coca‑Cola and an 8% jump for Powerade, which sponsored the controversial hydration break at the tournament, during the quarter. The group raised its full-year guidance to the top end of its forecasts thanks to the performance, with growth now expected to be at around 5% rather than the 4%-5% previously guided. In Europe, the Middle East and Africa, volumes were up 4% in the three months ended 3 July 2026, primarily driven by growth in the Coca-Cola brand, water, sports drinks, coffee and tea . Latin America and North America both boosted volumes by 3%, while Asia Pacific was up 8%. Coca-Cola’s group operating margin improved by 80 basis points to 34.9% in the quarter, with earnings per share up 16% to $1.03, or 11% on a comparable basis. “We delivered another strong quarter by staying close to the changing needs of our consumers and customers,” CEO Henrique Braun said. “While we continue to see a dynamic consumer landscape, we leveraged our powerful brands and system to gain value share, delivering revenue, profit and earnings growth while also investing for the long term.” While measured against Coca-Cola’s easiest comparative period of the financial year, the company’s performance pleased analysts. RBC’s Nik Modi said the group was “in a better position than most” thanks to its flair in navigating outside shocks. “The only way to build a wine is by making fewer bottles than the market needs”

Familia Zuccardi, bodega argentina líder, presenta su estrategia de escasez controlada y diferenciación en el mercado premium internacional a través de la plataforma La Place en Londres. La empresa posiciona dos vinos de alta gama (Malbec y Chardonnay) con enfoque en viñedos únicos y técnicas específicas, reflejando la tendencia de construcción de marca mediante oferta limitada en mercados de alto valor. Esta estrategia de Zuccardi—uno de los productores argentinos más relevantes—ilustra cómo las bodegas locales compiten en canales premium globales con diferenciación versus volumen, modelo aplicable a bebidas premium de consumo masivo.

Texto completo del artículo
Success on La Place’s Hors Bordeaux campaign is a way to build brands and boost access for wineries, but as Sebastián Zuccardi, owner/winemaker at Argentina’s Familia Zuccardi, says, you cannot afford to be complacent. Arabella Mileham reports. Zuccardi was in London to present the two wines that are available on La Place: Finca Canal Uco, a single-vineyard Malbec expression from Paraje Altamira in the Uco Valley; and El Camino de Las Flores 2025, a high altitude non-malolatic Chardonnay from Monasterio in Tupungato, which is aged entirely in concrete vessels. Its inaugural release last year came out to great acclaim – db’s Bordeaux correspondent Colin Hay declared himself “an instant fan”, calling it “vibrant, racy and brilliantly crystalline” and awarding it 96 points. These two wines are in small production (the El Camino de Las Flores 2025 numbers only 2,000 bottles) represented now by five negociants on La Place. “We are not looking for volume on La Place, it’s not why we are there, we are trying to access areas where our distribution is not arriving,” Zuccardi says. Argentina, for example, is not very strong in Asia, but the negociants’ market capillarity gives brand access to regions across Asia that could not otherwise be penetrated (although it now has a representative based in Hong Kong working across Asia, two in the US, one in Brazil and one in London). And while many people have built a big business through La Place, over the last three years, there’s no doubt business has become more complicated, which he admits is worrying. Bordeaux’s “crazy” unsustainable price rises during and immediately after the pandemic not only resulted in the present market malaise but also fuelled negociants to take on a great number of wines from outside Bordeaux. “I think the negociants took too much, but now there will be a curve, and it will stabilize,” Zuccardi shrugs. “The only way to build a wine is by making less bottles than the market needs – if you make one bottle more than the market needs you start talking about price.” “And I want to grow slowly and I don’t want to reduce the prices.” “When we started, I thought, ‘I have to put the wines in their stocks and they will do everything’, but they do the distribution, but they don’t do the brand building, so you need to do work doing tastings,” he explains. “La Place helps to open doors, but you need to work, you need to go to the market, you need to be doing presentations,” he adds. “My passion is the vineyard and the winery, but today that is not enough. We have to go out and bring the message.” “Evangelise, evangelise, evangelise! It’s important because we are far [away] and not many people know the unique conditions that we have in the area. So, bringing the message [to them] is also important.” This is particularly the case where consumers have a fixed idea of Malbec from Argentina as very concentrated, black in colour, with a lot of oak. “But this is [only] one version of Malbec today,” Zuccardi says. “In the Uco Valley, you can see that many producers are not working like this – and we need to change the image of that for people. But this is a work that takes time.” “For many years, people thought that Malbec was a grape that was good for young wines. My feeling is that when it’s coming from very good places, and we try to avoid overripe, over extraction, and overdraw, the aging potential is very long.” For Zuccardi, Malbec “is the best vehicle to express our places”, likening it to Nabbiolo in Barolo or Pinot Noir in Burgundy. “I like to say that we make mountain wines. Everything that happened in our area is in relationship with the mountain,” he says. It is the the rivers flowing down from the Andes that created the unique alluvial soils (granite covering by calcaire) the multitude of stones encouraging deep roots. It is also the reason for the cool temperatures and high sun exposure, which results in the slow maturation of the grapes. “We cultivate in a quite unique condition that is a cold climate with a very pure sunlight. I like to say that our wine has to talk about the freshness of the altitude and the intensity of the light and the management of the light is very important.” Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2013 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 1100 metres on the southern slopes of the Uco valley on the start of the alluvial fan; aged in 500 litre used oak14.5% alcohol). Big, bold, bloody and rich, certainly in comparison with the rather more ethereal recent vintages. And incredibly youthful. Plump and ample in frame, if a little closed at first. But that allows the stone fruit and, above all, the spicier elements to come through aromatically. The rose petals notes that in time become the central focus appear in the glass only later with a little gentle aeration. The wonderfully expressive limestone tannins bring massive grip to the mid-palate, wrestling the fruit to the tight and tense finish. Fresh, vivid, bright and crisp – if in fact rather more so on the palate than aromatically. Youthful and almost a little strict on the finish, this has considerable aging potential still. 93 . Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2016 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 1100 metres on the southern slopes of the Uco valley on the start of the alluvial fan; 14.5% alcohol; only 30% oak aged; vinified in concrete). A cool and comparatively wet vintage that really sorts the wheat from the chaff. It is gorgeous here and immediately captivating aromatically. Unlike the 2013, there’s no aromatic trace of the oak and that allows it to reveal immediately a lovely redolent bulbly florality that is the signature of the entire flight of wines here. In the mouth this is gracious, full and quite ample in frame. And here we find the beginning of the familiar crystallinity that is the Zuccardi calling card in the more recent vintage. Incredibly fresh once again, distinctly sapid on the finish and deceptively youthful. 95 . Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2019 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 1100 metres on the southern slopes of the Uco valley on the start of the alluvial fan; 14% alcohol). A great vintage and a very complete and composed wine. This is tight and compact, with a less ample frame but massive intensity and mid-palate density and concentration. The Zuccardi style that I recognise and love from the 2021, 2022 and 2023 is already present here, albeit now with a little more bottle maturation. The earlier vintages do not have quite the same finesse. Indeed, there’s a very rare, radiant purity to this. Loganberry, a little red cherry, with perfectly bright and crunchy berry fruits. Floral and herbal in equal measure and with those lovely beady limestone tannins. Ethereal, juicy and succulent with great finesse despite the density. Lifted and aerial, this finishes very much at the top of the palate. Supremely harmonious and fresh on the silken finish. Truly wonderful. 97 . Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2020 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 14.5% alcohol). A warm vintage, small in quantity, high in extract and relatively early picked. This is elegant, refined, subtle and finessed. It’s a little more intimate perhaps than the 2019, but no less aerial and lifted aromatically, the verticality from those gracious limestone tannins a little more apparent here than in the more ample vintages either side. There’s perhaps a little more fruit intensity than with the 2021. Very long and pure on the finish. Lithe, pure and very vertical in its aromatic presentation, this feels every inch a wine from a high altitude limestone terroir , as indeed it is. I love both the florality and the visceral intensity of the crushed raspberry and loganberry fruit, which is what this wine is all about. Lifted aromatically and tight on the palate with lots of purity and precision. Very fine. A most eloquent expression of its terroir. 96 . Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2021 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 14.5% alcohol). An excellent harvest. Cooler, not unlike the 2022, but a little drier. Intensely expressive and intensely floral aromatically, this is very vertical and aerial too. The parfumier’s essences of lilac and wild rosemary waft from the glass with a little dark purple peony too. Gracious and glowing, elegant and very fluid this has a wide and ample mouthful of fresh fruit juice pushing at the cheeks. Saline, with liquorice notes building towards the pleasingly sustained and sapid finish. Staggeringly fresh and incredibly vivid, with a pure, rippling, pulsating fruit signature, elongated by the gentle massage of the limestone tannins. Brilliantly juicy and electric. 96 . Finca Canal Uco (Zuccardi) 2022 (Mendoza; 100% Malbec; 14.5% alcohol). Beeswax and candlewax that I noted a year ago and now again almost exactly a year on. This is a little more substantial than the 2021 and maybe a little less immediately tense, with greater density and mid-palate concentration. The fruit is darker in shade and more intense still – assorted plump berries with a little damson and damson stone. There’s maybe a hint of walnut too and an almost Bordeaux-esque cassis and cedar combination. This is beguilingly composed with a very shapely spherical core outlined by the ultra-soft and fine-grained tannins. A wine of plunge-pool purity that is almost Pichon Comtesse-like in texture, but with Argentinian zing, spice and florality. Truly excellent. 97 .   • Expiración del decreto de consentimiento DOJ que regulaba la fusión AB InBev-SABMiller abre un nuevo escenario competitivo en distribución en EE.UU., con implicancias directas para el acceso al canal de marcas independientes.
Finanzas

Boston Beer fue el gran perdedor de la semana con EPS ajustado de $3.65 vs. estimado de $4.83, ingresos netos cayendo 3.3% a $568M y depletions en -6%, lo que refleja una contracción estructural en su portafolio más allá de Twisted Tea. En contraste, United Spirits (Diageo India) reportó un salto de 52% en ganancias netas y Arca Continental sostuvo un EBITDA del 20.7% pese al nuevo impuesto mexicano a bebidas, demostrando mayor resiliencia operativa en mercados emergentes.

Marketing

La premiumización y las bebidas sin alcohol continuaron siendo los ejes creativos de la semana: Heineken posicionó su tecnología SmartDispense como motor de márgenes en pubs británicos y su línea 0.0 como palanca de crecimiento global, mientras Diageo India incrementó su inversión publicitaria a Rs 312 crores y tomó una participación del 10% en la startup de craft spirits Nuvola. El boom de marcas de celebrities spirits mostró señales claras de agotamiento, con Casamigos entre los casos de declive citados por analistas del sector.

Supply Chain

Bacardi formalizó un acuerdo global con EcoSpirits para packaging de circuito cerrado, sumándose a Pernod Ricard en una apuesta por logística sostenible que podría redefinir estándares de la industria de destilados. Suntory PepsiCo inauguró una planta de $300M en Vietnam orientada a bebidas más saludables, señalando una reconfiguración de capacidad productiva en Asia-Pacífico hacia categorías de bajo contenido calórico.

Ventas / Comercial

Coca-Cola sellò una alianza estratégica con Marriott International para expandir distribución de su portafolio en el canal hotelero global, un movimiento que refuerza el on-premise premium. Heineken Costa Rica adquirió la marca de agua artesanal RainForest Water, ampliando su presencia en el canal de bebidas premium no alcohólicas en Centroamérica y marcando un movimiento relevante en la estrategia de diversificación de portafolio en la región.
